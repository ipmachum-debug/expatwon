## 1. Line up the closure date and the leaving date first

The date the business closed and the date each employee actually left are not
necessarily the same day.

It is common for staff to work on for a week after trading stops — clearing
stock, returning equipment, handing over. That work is employment, and the
employment relationship ends when it ends.

So the loss-of-coverage date is taken from the actual end of the employment
relationship, not copied across from the tax office closure date. Alongside it
sits the rest of the payroll wind-up: the final wage payment, anything unpaid,
retirement benefits, withholding tax, and the employment income statement.

## 2. The four insurances run on three different deadlines

<div class="callout callout-warn">
  <p class="callout-title">"Everything within 14 days" is convenient and wrong</p>
  <p>Two of the three deadlines are 14 days. The national pension is not — it runs
  to the <strong>15th of the following month</strong>, which is longer than 14 days
  for a closure early in the month and shorter for one at the end of it. A
  practical checklist that says 14 days across the board is safe by accident in
  one direction and late in the other.</p>
</div>

**Health insurance.** Where a withdrawal event such as closure occurs, the
workplace withdrawal report is made within
[14 days](/tracked/nhis-business-closure-deadline/). The workplace's withdrawal
and an individual employee's loss of insured status are separate filings —
closing the workplace does not by itself dispose of the employee side.

**Employment and industrial accident insurance.** These two are collected
together and end together. Where the insurance relationship is extinguished
because the business closed, the report is made within
[14 days from the date coverage ends](/tracked/employment-accident-insurance-closure-deadline/).
Note the difference in what starts the clock: this one runs from the
extinguishment of the insurance relationship, which follows when the business
actually ceased.

**National pension.** The workplace withdrawal and the insured members' loss of
status are reported by the
[15th of the month following the month in which the event occurred](/tracked/nps-business-closure-deadline/).

You can submit all of it through the joint social insurance portal, and you
generally should. Just do not read that convenience as merging the deadlines —
it is one submission against separate statutory clocks, and only one of them
moves with the calendar month.

## 3. Reporting the loss does not settle the premiums

An accepted loss-of-status report is not the end of the money.

Premiums were charged during the year against a reported average or standard
monthly wage. The final settlement compares that against what was actually paid.
Where the two differ, each scheme reconciles — and the reconciliation happens
after the reports are in.

So an additional assessment, a refund, or a retroactive adjustment can arrive
weeks after everything looked closed.

<div class="callout callout-note">
  <p class="callout-title">Do not close the business account on the same day</p>
  <p>Final premium assessments, refunds and tax repayments all need a live
  settlement account, and reopening the payment arrangements after the fact is
  far more work than leaving the account alone for a few months. Keep it until
  tax and social insurance are both finally settled. What is involved in the
  account itself is in
  <a href="/banking/how-to-open-a-bank-account-in-korea-as-a-foreigner/">opening a Korean bank account</a>.</p>
</div>

## 4. The owner's own health insurance is re-decided too

When the workplace's employee-insured status ends, the owner's own health
insurance status is redetermined with it.

Three outcomes are possible. Becoming an employee somewhere else restores
employee-insured status there. Meeting the dependant conditions on a family
member's cover moves the owner onto that. Failing both, the owner converts to
regionally insured status under the residence and enrolment rules — with the
premium calculated on income and property rather than on a salary.

For a foreign owner that third outcome is the one to check in advance, because
the regional premium is assessed on a basis that has nothing to do with what the
business was earning at the end. The income and property tests on the dependant
route are in
[NHIS dependants: the income and property tests](/insurance/nhis-dependants-the-income-and-property-tests/).

What the employees cost while they were employed — and therefore what the final
reconciliation is measured against — is in
[what your first employee actually costs](/employment/what-your-first-employee-actually-costs/).

## 5. A sole proprietor's income tax does not disappear

Closing in the middle of 2026 does not remove 2026 from the tax year.

Business income earned from 1 January 2026 to the closure date goes into the
**global income tax return filed in May 2027**, in the ordinary way.

Which means the records have to survive the closure. Sales, deductible expenses,
depreciation, payroll, retirement benefits, bad debts and gains or losses on
disposing of assets — all of it is needed months after the shop is empty, and
none of it is easier to reconstruct then.

Treating the final VAT return as the last tax event is the error. It is the
first of two.

## 6. A company is on a completely different clock

Here the sole trader's rule stops applying, and repeating it costs a penalty.

A company files its corporate tax return **within three months from the last day
of the month in which its business year ends**. Not the following May. The
closure of the business registration does not move that date, and
[the deadline is tracked](/tracked/corporate-tax-after-business-closure/) for
exactly that reason.

More importantly: **closing a company's business registration is not dissolving
the company.** A company that has merely stopped trading and closed its
registration still exists as a legal person. Ending it properly is a separate
process under the Commercial Act — a resolution to dissolve, registration of the
dissolution, liquidation, settling debts and receivables, and distributing what
is left. And a company that does dissolve may have its business year split at
the date the dissolution is registered.

## 7. Money left in a company account is not the owner's

The company is a separate legal person from its representative. That is true on
the way in and it stays true on the way out.

Cash or assets left in the company after trading stops, taken by a director or a
shareholder, are not "closing up the accounts". Depending on how it is done, it
is salary, a bonus, a dividend, a provisional payment to a director, or a
liquidation distribution — and each of those has its own tax treatment attached.

So for a company the order runs backwards from the instinctive one: settle
assets, liabilities and the plan for distributing what remains to shareholders
**before** closing the business registration, not after.

## The whole sequence, on one page

| Stage | The work | The deadline |
|---|---|---|
| Trading stops | Fix the actual closure date | The real last day of business |
| Business registration | Close at Hometax or the tax office | Without delay |
| Licences | Close with the issuing authority | By sector |
| VAT | Final return and payment | 25th of the month after closure |
| Leftover goods | Deemed supply on stock and fixed assets | In that same VAT return |
| Health insurance | Workplace withdrawal | 14 days |
| Employment · accident | End of coverage | 14 days from the date it ends |
| National pension | Workplace withdrawal and loss of status | 15th of the following month |
| Sole proprietor | Global income tax | May of the following year |
| Company | Corporate tax | 3 months from the fiscal year end month |
| Company dissolution | Dissolution and liquidation | Its own separate timetable |

The dangerous thing about closing a business is not that the revenue stops.

It is that trading has ended while, on paper, stock is still held, employees are
still insured, and assets are still sitting in a company account. The financial
end of a business arrives when the stock, the assets, the staff, the tax and the
insurance are all closed against the same date — and the first half of that
work, the VAT return and the leftovers, is in
[how to close a business in Korea](/business/closing-a-business-in-korea-final-vat-and-stock/).
