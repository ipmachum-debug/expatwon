A trip your company sends you on is not automatically a business visit in
Korea's immigration system. The system splits at a different place — not at who
pays, and not at how long you stay, but at **what you will actually be doing
once you are here.**

<figure class="figure hero">
  <p class="figure-title">Two sides of one line</p>
  <p class="figure-sub">Published examples, from the portal's own category pages</p>

```
BUSINESS VISITOR              SHORT-TERM EMPLOYEE
C-3-4 / C-3-5 / C-3-6         C-4
under Short Term Visit        under Professional
─────────────────────────     ─────────────────────────
market research               temporary show
business meetings             advertising, modelling
consultation                  lecturing, speaking
signing a contract            research
small-scale trading           technology consulting
```

  <p class="figcap">Read the two consulting entries next to each other. The word is on both sides; what is being done is not.</p>
</figure>

Before any of this, check whether the trip needs a visa at all — for many
passports a short business visit is inside visa-free entry.

  <p class="callout-title">Start here</p>
  <p>The <a href="/tools/korea-entry-check/">Korea entry requirements check</a> answers visa-free scope, K-ETA and the arrival declaration separately. The visa-free purposes explicitly include “business purposes” — with no commercial activities allowed, which is the same boundary in different words.</p>
</div>

## The portal files them in different places, on purpose

Open **Short Term Visit** and the three business-visitor routes are there
together:

![The Short Term Visit category expanded on the Korea Visa Portal, listing twelve routes including Business Visitor General, Agreement and Sponsored](../../assets/entry/visa-2-short-visit-codes.png)
<p class="figcap">Business Visitor (General) C-3-4, (Agreement) C-3-5 and (Sponsored) C-3-6 — three of the twelve short-visit routes.</p>

Now open **Professional**, and C-4 is not with them:

![The Professional category expanded, listing Short-Term Employee C-4 alongside Job Seeker, Professor, Researcher, Technical Instructor and the other E-series statuses](../../assets/entry/biz-4-professional-category.png)
<p class="figcap"><strong>Short-Term Employee (C-4)</strong> heads a list that runs on through Professor (E-1), Researcher (E-3) and Technical Instructor (E-4). That is the company it keeps.</p>

C-4 is filed with the work statuses, not with the visits. It is not a harder
business visa; it is a short employment visa that happens to be brief.

## C-3-4 — Business Visitor (General)

![The Visa Portal detail page for Business Visitor (General), showing its eligible applicants and activities](../../assets/entry/biz-1-c34-general.png)
<p class="figcap">One line, and it is the most useful line on the subject.</p>

> Person who plans to engage in commercial activities such as market research,
> business meetings, consultation, signing a contract, or small-scale
> international trading or an APEC card holder visiting Korea without a visa.

Five activities and one card. If the substance of the trip is meeting a Korean
company, looking at the market, discussing terms or signing something, this is
the business-visitor side.

Note the last clause: an **APEC Business Travel Card holder** appears in
C-3-4's own definition. That is the same card that separately gets a holder
C-3-4 status for 90 days — and, worth knowing, does *not* excuse the e-Arrival
Card declaration.

## C-3-5 — Business Visitor (Agreement)

![The Visa Portal detail page for Business Visitor (Agreement)](../../assets/entry/biz-2-c35-agreement.png)
<p class="figcap">Not a longer C-3-4. A different basis.</p>

> A person who plans to enter ROK for short-term business in accordance with an
> agreement with ROK.

The qualifying fact is the **agreement**, not the length of the trip or the
kind of meeting. You do not choose C-3-5 because the visit is brief and
business-related; you are in it because an agreement covers you.

## C-3-6 — Business Visitor (Sponsored)

![The Visa Portal detail page for Business Visitor (Sponsored)](../../assets/entry/biz-3-c36-sponsored.png)
<p class="figcap">Again a structure, not a duration.</p>

> A person who has been invited by a company or an organization designated by
> the Minister of Justice of ROK as a preferential company.

The inviting body is the qualifying fact, and it has to be a designated one.
That also shapes how the application is made: C-3-6 appears among the statuses
eligible for **Confirmation of Visa Issuance**, which the sponsor in Korea
files on the applicant's behalf. The confirmation is valid for three months and
works once; the applicant then applies at a mission with its number.

<div class="callout callout-note">
  <p class="callout-title">Three routes, three different qualifying facts</p>
  <p><strong>C-3-4</strong> — the activity. <strong>C-3-5</strong> — an agreement. <strong>C-3-6</strong> — a designated inviter. None of the three is “the same thing for longer”, so none can be picked from the calendar.</p>
</div>

## C-4 — Short-Term Employee

![The Visa Portal detail page for Short-Term Employee (C-4)](../../assets/entry/biz-5-c4-short-term-employee.png)
<p class="figcap">The published examples are the useful part.</p>

> A person who plans to visit for a short-term employment such as temporary
> show, advertising modeling, lecturing speeching, research, technology
> consulting, etc.

A performance. A modelling or advertising booking. Speaking on the programme
rather than sitting in the audience. Research as the assignment. And
**technology consulting** — which is where the trap is.

<div class="callout callout-warn">
  <p class="callout-title">⚠️ “Consulting” appears on both lists</p>
  <p>Plain <strong>consultation</strong> is a C-3-4 business-visitor example. <strong>Technology consulting</strong> is a C-4 short-term employment example. So the word on your business card settles nothing.</p>
  <p>The question underneath it: are you discussing a matter with a counterpart, or has a Korean company brought you in so that your expertise is the thing being delivered? The first is a visit. The second is work, whatever the invoice calls it.</p>
</div>

## What the form asks, and why vagueness fails

The application's own purpose list keeps the split visible:

![The e-Form application screen showing the Purpose of Entry options and the Status of Stay dropdown](../../assets/entry/biz-8-status-of-stay.png)
<p class="figcap">Separate radio buttons for <strong>Meeting, Conference</strong>, <strong>Business Trip</strong> and <strong>Work</strong> — and a separate Status of Stay list in which C-3 and C-4 are different rows.</p>

Two fields, asked separately, because they are two facts. "Business" as a
single word answers neither.

The same screen asks whether you have an inviting company, and if so wants its
Visa Portal ID, business registration number, name, representative,
relationship to you, address and telephone:

![The e-Form section asking whether the applicant has an inviting company, with fields for its Visa Portal ID and business registration number](../../assets/entry/biz-9-inviting-company.png)
<p class="figcap">The Korean host is part of the application, not context for it.</p>

## e-Visa and e-Form are not the same thing

This one costs people a trip to a consulate.

![The Visa Portal's e-Visa page, explaining what an e-Visa is and which statuses can use it](../../assets/entry/biz-6-evisa.png)
<p class="figcap">e-Visa exists to remove the in-person visit — for the statuses it covers.</p>

> e-Visa refers to an electronic visa which can be applied and granted via the
> Visa Portal. However, applicants can apply for their e-visas for only certain
> status of stay.

![The Visa Portal's e-Form page, explaining that the completed form must be printed with its barcode and taken to a diplomatic mission](../../assets/entry/biz-7-eform.png)
<p class="figcap">e-Form is an online <em>form</em>. The application still happens at the mission.</p>

> In order to complete the application process, you must print the e-form (with
> a bar code) that you filled out on the Visa Portal (with other required
> documents) and visit the Korean embassy or consulate general in your country
> of residence.

The e-Form route runs **Send e-Form → Submission and payment at the diplomatic
office → Reception & Review → Visa Issuance**, and covers single-entry visas of
90 days or less for C-1, C-3 and C-4. Multiple entry is available for C-1 and
C-3 only where a bilateral agreement exists.

<div class="callout callout-warn">
  <p class="callout-title">⚠️ The C-3-4 e-Visa condition is about the channel, not the visa</p>
  <p>The e-Visa page lists C-3-4 for an invitee of a domestic company, and attaches a qualification: the applicant must have entered Korea <strong>three times or more on a short-term C-3 visa</strong> since the fingerprint confirmation system began on 1 January 2012, and have no history of illegal stay or criminal cases.</p>
  <p>That is not “you need three prior trips for a C-3-4”. It is what that one online channel requires. C-4's e-Visa entry is narrower still — foreign scientists invited by a government-funded research institution or an accredited university.</p>
</div>

## Which side is your trip on?

| What you will actually do | Look at |
| --- | --- |
| Market research | C-3-4 |
| Business meetings | C-3-4 |
| Consultation with a counterpart | C-3-4 |
| Signing a contract | C-3-4 |
| Small-scale international trading | C-3-4 |
| Short-term business under an agreement with Korea | C-3-5 |
| Invited by a designated preferential company | C-3-6 |
| A temporary show or performance | C-4 |
| Advertising or modelling work | C-4 |
| Lecturing or speaking on the programme | C-4 |
| Research as the assignment | C-4 |
| Technology consulting as the assignment | C-4 |

A routing aid, not a determination. What you are paid, by whom, under what
contract, for which Korean host, and what your mission asks for can all move
the answer — and the mission decides, not a table.

## Two things that do not work

<div class="callout callout-warn">
  <p class="callout-title">⚠️ Entering on the easiest C and fixing it later</p>
  <p>The application form states that category C visa holders cannot change their status of stay after entering Korea, under Article 9(1) of the Enforcement Regulations of the Immigration Act. C-3-4, C-3-5, C-3-6 and C-4 are all category C. The choice is made before you fly.</p>
</div>

<div class="callout callout-warn">
  <p class="callout-title">⚠️ Describing work as tourism</p>
  <p>The form warns that false information or documents lead to revocation of the visa and of permission to stay, and may bring criminal punishment and an entry ban. If the trip is work, the tourist route is not a shortcut — it is the offence.</p>
</div>

## The order to answer in

<div class="steps">
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">1</div><div class="step-body"><strong>Can this trip use visa-free entry at all?</strong> Short business visits often can — the purposes include business with no commercial activities allowed.</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">2</div><div class="step-body"><strong>What will I physically do in Korea?</strong> Described as an activity, not as a job title.</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">3</div><div class="step-body"><strong>Is that a commercial visit or short-term employment?</strong> This is the line the whole system turns on.</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">4</div><div class="step-body"><strong>Is an agreement or a designated inviter involved?</strong> If so, C-3-5 or C-3-6 rather than C-3-4.</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">5</div><div class="step-body"><strong>Which channel am I eligible for?</strong> e-Visa, e-Form, Confirmation of Visa Issuance, or applying at the mission. Separate question from the status.</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">6</div><div class="step-body"><strong>What does my own mission currently require?</strong> Its list governs, whatever any guide says.</div></div>
</div>

Do not run that list backwards by picking a convenient visa and then describing
the trip to fit it.

## The short version

Korea does not ask whether you are travelling for business. It asks whether you
are coming **to conduct business** or **to perform work** — and it files those
answers in two different parts of the system.

C-3-4 for market research, meetings, consultation, contracts and small-scale
trading. C-3-5 where an agreement applies. C-3-6 where a designated company
invites you. C-4 for shows, modelling, speaking, research and technology
consulting.

The word "consulting" is on both lists. Everything else follows from being
honest about which one you are doing.
