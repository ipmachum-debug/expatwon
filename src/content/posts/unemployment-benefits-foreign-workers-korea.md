A foreign worker can have a lawful job, receive regular payslips and still discover after losing that job that **the unemployment-benefit component of Korean employment insurance was never in force**. That is why starting with “How much does Korea pay?” is the wrong approach.

First establish benefit coverage, then claim eligibility. Only then calculate payments. Immigration permission to remain and seek work is separate.

The Employment Insurance Act gives useful numbers—**180 insured-unit days**, a usual **18-month** reference period, **120–270 payable days**, and a **12-month** outer window. None creates entitlement alone.

## The first gate: was unemployment-benefit insurance actually in force?

Foreign nationality alone does not resolve employment-insurance coverage. Official Work24 guidance differentiates residents with statuses **F-2, F-5 and F-6**, generally covered on a mandatory basis, from categories for which enrollment in the unemployment-benefit component may be voluntary. In particular, its guidance distinguishes **E-9 and H-2** workers: employment stabilization and vocational-development coverage can be mandatory while **unemployment benefits are subject to voluntary enrollment**. Other work-authorized categories, including **E-7 and F-4**, generally require checking whether voluntary enrollment was actually made.

This is not a claim that every F-visa holder has an approved claim or that every E-visa holder is excluded. Different worker types, employment relationships and statutory exceptions matter. The official guidance is the starting point; the person's actual insurance record is the evidence.

| Residence/work status | General unemployment-insurance question | First action |
|---|---|---|
| F-2, F-5, F-6 | Generally mandatory employee coverage | Check the employee's actual insured history |
| E-9, H-2 | Unemployment-benefit component may require voluntary enrollment | Check whether it was applied for and when coverage began |
| E-7, F-4 | Often voluntary under the foreign-worker framework | Obtain confirmation of enrollment rather than assuming |
| Other statuses or nonstandard work | Classification depends on the status and employment arrangement | Ask the competent agency to assess the specific facts |

A payroll deduction does not prove job-seeking-benefit entitlement; separate accident, health or departure insurance certainly does not. Ask **COMWEL** to confirm the relevant enrollment and effective dates.

**Official coverage guide:** [Work24 — Employment Insurance System, foreign-worker coverage](https://m.work24.go.kr/cm/c/f/1100/selecSystInfo.do?systClId=SC00000350&systId=SI00000419).

## The second gate: the 180 days are not simply six calendar months

For an ordinary employed insured worker, **Employment Insurance Act Article 40** generally requires an aggregate of at least **180 insured-unit days** within the statutory reference period, normally the **18 months** before separation. Article 41 defines the insured-unit-day concept in relation to days used as the basis for wage payment. This is not automatically identical to 180 days since the employee's first day at work.

Two workers employed for six calendar months may have different insured-unit-day totals. The ordinary 18-month formula does not govern every special category.

Review the entire reference period, not only the final employer. Where multiple employment periods may count, verify the agency's recorded dates and whether the statutory rules permit aggregation. Keep contracts, attendance records and wage statements; they help explain a discrepancy but do not replace the official insured-history determination.

| Measure | Why it matters | Common mistake |
|---|---|---|
| Employment start date | Establishes the work timeline | Counting every calendar day as an insured-unit day |
| Insured-status effective date | Shows when the relevant coverage began | Assuming enrollment started on the first payslip |
| Insured-unit days | Determines the statutory 180-day threshold | Reading “180” as six months for everyone |
| Reference period | Normally 18 months before separation for ordinary employees | Looking only at the last job |
| Periods with special circumstances | Can affect the applicable statutory reference rules | Applying the standard period when an exception applies |

**Primary law:** [Employment Insurance Act Article 40](https://www.law.go.kr/LSW/lsLinkCommonInfo.do?chrClsCd=010202&lsJoLnkSeq=1032426967) and [Article 41](https://www.law.go.kr/LSW/lsLinkCommonInfo.do?chrClsCd=010202&lsJoLnkSeq=1029336953).

## The reason the job ended can change the claim

Job-seeking benefits are not a general severance payment. The claimant ordinarily must be willing and able to work, remain unemployed despite that willingness and ability, have a separation reason that is not disqualifying under the Act, and conduct required re-employment activities. The reason recorded by the employer therefore matters.

An involuntary layoff, an ordinary resignation, an employer's claim of misconduct and a disputed separation should not be given the same answer. The law recognizes defined circumstances in which an apparent voluntary resignation may still be assessed differently, but an article cannot certify an exception without the worker's evidence and the applicable rule.

If the employer's separation report says “voluntary resignation” but the worker says the employer ended the contract, collect termination notices, messages, the final contract, schedules, wage records and contemporaneous correspondence. Request a correction or formal determination through the appropriate channel. A benefit application built on a disputed separation code is not fixed merely by entering a different reason in an online form.

## The 120–270 days table is not the amount of time you may stay in Korea

**Employment Insurance Act Article 50 and Appendix 1** set the standard number of payable job-seeking-benefit days according to insurance duration and age at separation. The statutory table is not a universal payment promise.

| Insured period | Under 50 at separation | Age 50+ or qualifying disability |
|---|---:|---:|
| Under 1 year | 120 days | 120 days |
| 1 to under 3 years | 150 days | 180 days |
| 3 to under 5 years | 180 days | 210 days |
| 5 to under 10 years | 210 days | 240 days |
| 10 years or more | 240 days | 270 days |

The table does not override a coverage failure or a disqualifying separation. Age is measured at the legally relevant separation date, and the disability category has a statutory meaning.

There are two clocks after separation. The first is the **scheduled payable-day allowance** (120–270 days). The second is the general **12-month outside benefit period** under **Article 48**, measured from the day after separation. An eligible claimant who waits too long may run out of the outer window before using all scheduled days. Certain circumstances support statutory extension, but that is a separate application and should not be assumed.

**Primary law:** [Article 50, payable days](https://www.law.go.kr/lsLinkCommonInfo.do?chrClsCd=010202&lsJoLnkSeq=1029336813), [Appendix 1, duration table](https://www.law.go.kr/LSW/flDownload.do?bylClsCd=110201&flSeq=162252893&gubun=), [Article 48, 12-month window](https://www.law.go.kr/LSW/lsLinkProc.do?joNo=004800000&lsNm=%EA%B3%A0%EC%9A%A9%EB%B3%B4%ED%97%98%EB%B2%95&mode=10).

## The waiting period comes before payable days

Under **Article 49**, the usual waiting period is **seven days from reporting unemployment**, during which job-seeking benefits generally are not paid. The law includes exceptions and a separate extended-waiting framework in specified repeat-claim circumstances. In particular, it would be misleading to promise every claimant the same payment date simply by adding seven days to a resignation date.

The seven-day wait does not necessarily begin on the last day worked. Payment still requires the unemployment-recognition process, so budget for a gap after the last salary.

**Primary law:** [Employment Insurance Act Article 49](https://www.law.go.kr/LSW/lsLinkCommonInfo.do?chrClsCd=010202&lsJoLnkSeq=1033131271).

## How the daily benefit is calculated

For an ordinary eligible employee, the starting daily benefit formula is generally **60% of the statutory wage base**, subject to the applicable legal floor, ceiling and special rules. The wage base is calculated under law; simply multiplying the most recent monthly take-home pay by 60% will usually be wrong.

The applicable figures should be rendered from observations with an explicit effective date, rather than copied permanently into the body. The ordinary eight-hour lower-bound calculation is **hourly minimum wage × 8 hours × 80%**. A claimant whose relevant scheduled hours are fewer than eight should **not** be told the eight-hour floor automatically applies. The legal calculation depends on the work schedule, wage base and separation date.

## Worked cases: estimate only after the eligibility decision

Consider two hypothetical employees who meet the coverage and separation rules, have an eight-hour scheduled workday, and have been assigned **150 payable days**. Their pre-limit calculated daily benefits differ:

| Hypothetical daily result before limits | Applicable step | Formula for 150 eligible payable days |
|---|---|---|
| ₩60,000 | Compare with the eight-hour floor and use it if applicable | {{fig:unemployment-benefit-daily-floor-8h}} × 150 |
| ₩75,000 | Apply the applicable daily ceiling | {{fig:unemployment-benefit-daily-cap}} × 150 |

These are arithmetic illustrations, not eligibility decisions. The coverage, separation, recognized days, lawful job-search status and continuing reporting duties must first be established.

The **outer 12-month period** and **payable-day allocation** remain separate. Report re-employment and other changes as required.

## What to do after losing a job

First, obtain the separation notice and preserve the employment contract, recent wage statements, work calendar, messages and insurance history. Next, check the official employment-insurance record: coverage category, effective dates, previous jobs that may be counted and the employer's reported reason for separation. If any entry is wrong, begin the correction process and keep evidence of the request.

Then register and follow the job-seeking-benefit process through **Work24 / the responsible employment center**. Application submission, recognition of eligibility, continuing unemployment recognition and payment are distinct steps. An approved initial claim does not necessarily eliminate later reporting obligations. Keep records of job-search activities in the format the center requires. Report new work or relevant changes instead of assuming small or temporary earnings never matter.

If the company never enrolled an eligible employee, the first question is whether enrollment should have been mandatory or was voluntary for that worker. The second is whether the agency can correct the record and from what effective date. A company promise to “add insurance later” is not the same as actual enrollment, especially where the statutory scheme treats a foreign worker's voluntary application as effective only under particular rules.

## A foreign worker has a second calendar: immigration

Unemployment-benefit eligibility does not extend a visa. Conversely, holding a valid residence card does not establish entitlement to job-seeking benefits. A worker approaching the end of employer-sponsored residence should check immigration reporting, permitted job-search activities and any status-change or extension deadlines immediately.

An **E-7** employee who loses a job, for example, should separately investigate whether the person may remain, change employers or apply for another lawful status. The benefit claim cannot supply that immigration answer. An **F-5** resident has different residence constraints, but still must satisfy the employment-insurance and claim conditions. A worker who travels outside Korea while claiming must also verify unemployment-recognition and job-search reporting consequences; don't assume a benefit continues without required availability and declarations.

Build two files: one for insured employment and the benefit claim, another for residence and work authorization. Keep both current. Joining them into a single assumption is how seemingly reasonable plans fail.

## Common reasons an application stalls

**No benefit coverage in the record.** Check the foreign-worker enrollment category and whether the unemployment-benefit component was applied for. A payslip that refers only generally to insurance does not settle this.

**Not enough insured-unit days.** Reconstruct paid days, prior employment and any relevant reference-period exception. A single short contract may not tell the whole story.

**The employer reported the wrong separation reason.** Obtain the specific classification and use the correction or review route. Do not invent a layoff or submit altered evidence.

**A claim started too late.** Compare the 12-month outer window with the time remaining and ask about any formally available extension before assuming the payable days remain usable indefinitely.

**Work authorization is unresolved.** Verify the immigration position independently. Insurance eligibility and lawful job search are related in practice but governed by different requirements.

## The practical bottom line

Before estimating benefits, confirm **coverage**, **insured-unit days**, **separation reason** and **ongoing eligibility**. Then use Article 50's days table, the general twelve-month benefit window and the annually maintained amount observations to budget realistically. The system is precise when read in the correct order. The common error is not bad arithmetic; it is calculating a payment for an insurance status that the worker has not established.

<p class="hand">Scope: Ordinary employed workers claiming job-seeking benefits. Artists, labor providers, self-employed insured persons, daily construction workers and other special categories may be subject to different rules.</p>
