You live in Korea and still have accounts back home. Maybe shares through an
overseas brokerage, maybe a crypto account with an exchange outside Korea. You
have moved nothing into Korea this year, sold nothing, and earned little
taxable income from any of it.

Does Korea need to know?

Possibly. Korea runs an overseas financial-account reporting system —
해외금융계좌 신고 — requiring certain residents and domestic corporations to
report qualifying overseas accounts once their combined balances pass a
statutory threshold.

It is not income tax filing. It is not a tax on the balance. It is not limited
to accounts that produced income, and it does not turn on how much came into
Korea. What matters is the **combined value of qualifying accounts at each
month-end** during the year.

**One month-end above the threshold can create an obligation for the following
June.**

But there is an exemption for certain foreign residents with five years or
less in Korea across the relevant ten-year period — and that gets checked
before anything else.

## The three numbers

| Figure | What it means |
|---|---|
| **₩500 million** | Combined balance across all qualifying overseas accounts |
| **12 month-ends** | The dates the annual test runs on |
| **1–30 June** | Filing period, the following year |

Reporting law: Adjustment of International Taxes Act, arts. 52–57. Valuation:
Enforcement Decree, arts. 92–94. The threshold is **more than** ₩500 million —
not ₩500 million or more.

## What the system is

Article 53 carries the obligation: a qualifying account holder reports where
the combined balances of overseas financial accounts exceed ₩500 million on at
least one month-end in the year, filing between 1 and 30 June of the following
year. Accounts held during 2025 were tested at the ends of January through
December 2025 and due in June 2026; 2026 holdings run to June 2027.

This is information reporting. Submitting it does not make the account value
taxable income — and the absence of income tax on an account does not make it
exempt from reporting.

**The reporting test is about ownership and value. The income-tax test is
about taxable income.** Keep them apart.

## Who reports, and who is exempt

The obligation falls on qualifying Korean residents and domestic corporations,
with status determined **as of the end of the reporting year**.

Nationality does not decide it. A foreign national can be a Korean tax
resident; a Korean citizen can be a non-resident. A residence card and
immigration status are background, not the test — Korean residence generally
turns on domicile or a place of residence for 183 days or more, read with the
circumstances, and someone with homes and connections in several countries
needs a closer look. A treaty may determine treaty residence. That ground is
[resident or not](/business/income-tax-for-foreigners-resident-or-not/).

### The five-in-ten-year exemption

Article 54 exempts a foreign resident whose cumulative Korean domicile or
residence over the relevant preceding ten years is **five years or less**,
assessed at the end of the reporting year.

Someone who arrived in 2024 with no earlier Korean periods, holding overseas
bank and investment accounts, may be exempt from this report — **even where
the balances would comfortably exceed the threshold.**

What it does not do: it does not remove income-tax obligations. Foreign-source
income may still be taxable; employment performed in Korea certainly is; other
reporting may apply. It exempts you from this report, not from Korean tax.

### Cumulative, not consecutive

The test asks for cumulative qualifying periods across the preceding ten
years, not the year your current visa was issued. Lived here before, left,
came back — the earlier periods may count, and they need not be consecutive.
A brief absence does not automatically end Korean domicile.

Keep entry and departure records, housing and employment records, earlier
Korean periods, where your family and economic life sat, and the calculation
for each reporting year.

Because qualifying one year does not mean qualifying the next, and the
accounts need not change at all for that to happen.

**The first year the exemption stops applying is the first year this filing
becomes relevant** — which is why long-stay expatriates should keep an account
inventory while still exempt.

### Same five years, different obligation

Income Tax Act art. 3 limits which foreign-source income Korea taxes for
qualifying foreign residents. Adjustment of International Taxes Act art. 54
exempts certain foreign residents from this report. Both use a cumulative
residence period. **They are not one combined benefit.**

Exemption from reporting does not make taxable income disappear, and paying
tax on foreign income does not satisfy a reporting obligation. The income side
is [Korean-source or
foreign-source](/business/korean-source-vs-foreign-source-income/).

## Combined, not per account

| Account | Month-end value |
|---|---:|
| Overseas bank A | ₩200m |
| Overseas bank B | ₩180m |
| Overseas brokerage | ₩160m |
| **Combined** | **₩540m** |

No single account is near ₩500 million. The combined figure exceeds it, and
that month-end can trigger the obligation.

Now make it ₩200m + ₩170m + ₩130m = **exactly ₩500 million**. Article 53 needs
the threshold *exceeded*, so that month-end does not qualify — though another
month in the same year might, which is why the whole year gets reviewed.

## One month-end is enough

Not the annual average. Not the 31 December balance. Each month-end,
separately.

| Month-end | Bank A | Bank B | Brokerage | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| January | ₩210m | ₩110m | ₩130m | ₩450m |
| February | ₩240m | ₩160m | ₩90m | ₩490m |
| **March** | ₩180m | ₩140m | ₩240m | **₩560m** |
| April | ₩110m | ₩100m | ₩250m | ₩460m |

March crosses it. April falling back to ₩460m changes nothing.

**The test is whether any month-end exceeds the threshold, not whether the
year-end does.**

## Which month-end you report from

The reference date is the month-end with the **highest combined balance** —
March 31 above, at ₩560m, with values of ₩180m, ₩140m and ₩240m.

🔴 Do not take each account's own annual peak and add them. One bank may peak
in January and a brokerage in September; combining unrelated peaks produces a
number that never existed. **The calculation needs synchronised values at each
of the twelve month-ends.**

### Accounts opened or closed during the year

Under the NTS's 2026 example, the report is based on the accounts held at the
highest combined month-end. An account opened in May is not swept into a March
31 snapshot because it existed later. An account held in March but closed in
September can still belong to that reference-date report.

So the filing is not a list of what is still open on 31 December.

### A mid-month spike is not a month-end balance

₩800 million on 15 April falling to ₩300 million by 30 April does not, by
itself, meet a test framed on month-end values.

⚠️ That is not an invitation to move funds around the dates. The underlying
transactions can raise income-tax, foreign-exchange and anti-money-laundering
questions of their own, and artificial arrangements raise others. Calculate the
actual prescribed month-end balances — not the highest intraday figure, and
not the lowest.

## "Financial account" is broader than a bank

| Account or asset | Consideration |
|---|---|
| Foreign bank savings and deposits | Generally included |
| Overseas securities accounts | Generally included |
| Listed foreign shares in those accounts | Included, under the valuation rules |
| Bonds and investment funds | Included where held through qualifying accounts |
| Certain insurance products | Included, subject to rules and exclusions |
| Overseas derivatives accounts | Potentially included |
| Custodial virtual-asset accounts with qualifying overseas providers | Included |

The structure matters: a shareholding and a deposit are not valued alike, an
insurance product may have a prescribed basis unlike its apparent cash value,
a virtual asset needs a month-end market price. **One foreign bank statement
does not establish the threshold.**

### Korean brokerage, foreign shares

₩600 million of US-listed shares bought and held through a **Korean**
securities company does not create this obligation merely because the shares
are foreign — the NTS says so directly. The same shares in an account opened
with an **overseas** brokerage can fall inside the system.

| Arrangement | Ordinary position |
|---|---|
| Foreign shares via a Korean brokerage | Not reportable on that basis |
| Foreign shares via an overseas brokerage account | Potentially reportable |
| **Korean company shares via an overseas account** | **Potentially reportable** |

That last row catches people. Shares in a Korean company do not drop out of
the system because the company is Korean. The question is the overseas
account and what it holds.

### Crypto: custody decides it

Qualifying overseas virtual-asset accounts came into the system with the 2022
holdings reported from 2023. So ₩180m in an overseas bank, ₩170m in an
overseas brokerage and ₩190m in a qualifying overseas exchange account is
₩540m combined — and not converting to cash does not remove it.

But not every wallet is an overseas financial account. The NTS's 2026
guidance distinguishes certain non-custodial and decentralised arrangements —
wallet software where the provider neither controls the assets nor
participates in transactions, a privately held hardware wallet — from accounts
maintained with a qualifying overseas custodial provider. Those non-custodial
arrangements are not subject to this reporting.

Being outside reporting settles nothing about income tax, gift tax or other
treatment. **Reporting follows the account and custody structure, not whether
the asset is digital.** A product calling itself a wallet does not establish
the answer.

### Valuing crypto

Not acquisition cost. The Decree generally takes the month-end quantity times
the prescribed final market price for that date, with a prior-trading-date
rule where the market was not operating, and a further rule allowing an
eligible closing price from a domestic or overseas market where a month-end
close cannot be determined. Then convert to won on the prescribed
methodology. Four units at an assumed ₩25 million closing price is ₩100
million — an invented price, shown to make the method concrete.

### Valuing shares

Quantity at month-end times the applicable closing price, with the preceding
trading day where the month-end is not a trading day. Shares bought for ₩300
million and worth ₩520 million at a month-end are not held at ₩300 million
because nothing was sold.

And the reverse: a market value that counts for reporting is **not** a
realised taxable gain. **Unrealised value can matter for account reporting
without being income.**

### Insurance and retirement accounts

The Decree generally uses **premiums paid** as the basis for applicable
insurance products, with exclusions for certain risk-only arrangements — so
neither reporting every premium value identically nor assuming every overseas
policy is exempt is right.

There is a specific exclusion for qualifying overseas retirement pension
accounts meeting **all** the prescribed conditions: the foreign tax treatment,
annual reporting to the foreign tax authority, early-withdrawal restrictions
and contribution limits. **Calling an account a pension account does not
establish the exemption.**

## Currency moves the answer

USD 350,000 sitting untouched:

| Month-end | USD | Illustrative rate | KRW |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| January | $350,000 | ₩1,350 | ₩472,500,000 |
| February | $350,000 | ₩1,450 | ₩507,500,000 |

Same dollars. February crosses the threshold. Nothing was deposited — the rate
moved.

The Decree requires the prescribed daily standard or cross rates under Korea's
foreign-exchange rules. **Do not pick a rate because it lands under ₩500
million.**

## Joint accounts count in full

Spouses holding one overseas account worth ₩600 million, each assuming
₩300 million, are both wrong on the ordinary rule. Article 53 treats each joint
holder as holding the account, and the Decree generally attributes the **full
balance** to each for the threshold test.

Article 54 and the related rules do provide exemptions where another related
holder's filing makes all the required information available to the NTS — but
the conditions have to be met. Do not assume one spouse's filing covers the
other, and do not halve a joint balance to get under the threshold.

## The beneficial owner may also report

The name on the account may not be the person who controls it or bears the
economics. Article 53 reaches both the nominal holder and the beneficial
owner, and Decree art. 94 sets how beneficial ownership is determined — who
bears the economic risk, receives income, holds disposal rights, effectively
manages the account — with provisions and exclusions for certain foreign
corporate structures.

So an account registered in someone else's name is not an exit. Equally,
knowing an overseas company has a bank account does not make every shareholder
personally report its balance. Work out who legally and beneficially holds it.

## What is not a financial account

A ₩700 million apartment in Vietnam is not a bank or brokerage account, and
its market value does not go into the combined balance as though it were cash.

But an overseas bank account collecting the rent can be reportable if the
conditions are met, and overseas real estate may carry its own obligations.

```
an overseas asset is not necessarily an overseas financial account
an overseas financial account can be reportable even where the funds
  are not current-year taxable income
```

### Loans do not net off

₩650 million of qualifying account value against a ₩300 million loan is not
₩350 million. The threshold runs on the prescribed values of the qualifying
accounts, not on personal net worth, and a mortgage on overseas property does
not reduce a separate brokerage account. Particular products have their own
valuation rules — but **this is not an assets-minus-liabilities test.**

## A full year, worked

A long-term foreign resident past the exemption, Korean tax resident at
year-end, with a US bank account, an overseas brokerage and a custodial crypto
account:

| Month-end | Bank | Brokerage | Crypto | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| January | ₩140m | ₩170m | ₩80m | ₩390m |
| February | ₩150m | ₩180m | ₩90m | ₩420m |
| March | ₩180m | ₩210m | ₩100m | ₩490m |
| April | ₩170m | ₩230m | ₩130m | ₩530m |
| May | ₩160m | ₩220m | ₩110m | ₩490m |
| **June** | ₩190m | ₩250m | ₩150m | **₩590m** |
| July | ₩180m | ₩230m | ₩110m | ₩520m |
| August | ₩150m | ₩190m | ₩100m | ₩440m |
| September | ₩160m | ₩180m | ₩90m | ₩430m |
| October | ₩140m | ₩190m | ₩80m | ₩410m |
| November | ₩130m | ₩180m | ₩70m | ₩380m |
| December | ₩120m | ₩190m | ₩80m | ₩390m |

Three months cross. June is highest at ₩590m, so 30 June is the reference
date, and the report runs on ₩190m / ₩250m / ₩150m.

Two things to notice. December's ₩390m does not undo the obligation the
earlier months created. And **₩590 million is an account value, not annual
income** — nobody earned it this year.

## What gets reported, and how

The information covers the holder — legal name and address — and the account:
the overseas institution, the account number or identifier, the country, the
prescribed balance information, and details of joint holders, nominal holders
and beneficial owners where relevant. The form is the **해외금융계좌 신고서**,
the prescribed form under the relevant Enforcement Rule. An account nickname
in a banking app is not the legal identifier.

Filing is electronic through Hometax or the SonTax app. The NTS's 2026
guidance gives the path:

```
Hometax / SonTax
  → 증명·등록·신청·사업장현황
  → 소득·법인세 관련 신청·신고
  → 해외금융계좌 신고
```

The interface changes. The prescribed paper form may also be submitted to the
competent tax office.

Work in order: **confirm the reporting year** (last year's twelve month-ends,
not this June's balance) → **verify status** (year-end residence, and every
exemption) → **calculate the annual maximum** (value every account at each
month-end, convert on the prescribed rules, find the highest combined) →
**gather account information** → **file** → **save the confirmation**. A
part-entered form is not a filing.

### The prefill carries names, not values

The NTS prefill service can carry forward institution names, account numbers
and other identifying information from a previous report. It does not carry a
correct current-year value. An account may have closed, another opened,
ownership changed, and the highest combined month-end moved. **It saves
typing, not judgement.**

### Nothing changed — file anyway?

Yes, where the obligation still applies. The NTS addresses this directly: an
earlier filing does not exempt a later year merely because the accounts are
unchanged, and the NTS already holding last year's information does not
satisfy this year's duty. Equally, having filed once does not create a
permanent obligation once the conditions stop being met.

**It is an annual obligation, not an account registration.**

### No notice does not mean no obligation

In June 2026 the NTS sent notices to taxpayers it considered potentially
subject to reporting — and said plainly that those who received none remained
responsible for determining their own position. The obligation arises under
law, not from a reminder, which matters for foreign residents who may not
receive notices in an expected language or channel. For hard questions the NTS
consultation route is **126 → 2 → 6 → 2**, or the competent tax office.

## What a failure costs

Failing to report qualifying accounts, or understating them, can carry an
administrative penalty of **10% of the unreported or underreported amount**,
capped at ₩1 billion.

🔴 That is 10% of the **account amount**, not of the interest it earned. On
₩600 million unreported, assuming an established violation and no relief:

```
₩600,000,000 × 10% = ₩60,000,000
```

**A reporting failure can be expensive on an account that produced almost no
income.**

There is more than one mechanism. Where the source of unreported funds is not
adequately explained when required, a further 10% penalty can apply to the
unexplained or falsely explained amount. Amounts above **₩5 billion** can lead
to public disclosure of the violator. Serious cases carry criminal provisions
— the NTS describes up to two years' imprisonment or a fine of 13–20% of the
relevant amount, subject to the criminal procedure.

None of this is automatic in every case. The facts and statutory conditions
govern. But it is a substantial legal obligation, not a questionnaire.

### Correcting a missed report

Amended and late reports exist. Which one depends on what happened: filed on
time but incomplete → amended; never filed → late. Qualifying voluntary
filing before enforcement action can reduce penalties by **up to 90%**,
depending on timing and conditions — *up to*, and conditional.

Establish which years were affected and what should have been reported. 🔴
**Adding a missing account to next year's report does not correct an earlier
filing.** Each year is addressed on its own.

### Secrecy is not a strategy

Korea participates in international tax-information exchange, and the NTS has
said it uses that information to examine reporting failures. Its September
2026 release reported **7,484 filers** and **₩107.1 trillion** for the 2026
season — up 9.1% in filers and 13.3% in amount — including ₩61.3 trillion of
overseas shares.

Those figures show the scale; they do not mean every account holder must file.
The threshold and the exemptions still decide it. But an account's perceived
privacy is not a compliance position.

## June 2026 added a separate trust regime

In June 2026 the NTS began receiving reports under a new **overseas trust
reporting** system for 2025 arrangements. It is a different regime, and the
NTS distinguishes the two.

🔴 **The ₩500 million threshold belongs to the financial-account regime.** The
trust system does not use the same minimum balance, so a trust is not exempt
because it is worth less than ₩500 million. It can cover arrangements where
someone establishes an overseas trust or transfers property into a qualifying
one, with its own conditions and required information. A trust may also have
associated financial accounts, and the legislation addresses duplication where
trust information has been submitted with the account information — under its
own exemption rules.

An overseas trust needs its own review.

## Not the May return, not the credit

| | May income-tax return | June account report |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | Calculate and settle taxable income | Disclose qualifying overseas accounts |
| Focus | Income, deductions, credits, tax | Ownership and prescribed balances |
| Period | May | June |
| Threshold | Depends on income and filing provisions | Combined month-end above ₩500m |
| Legal basis | Income Tax Act | Adjustment of International Taxes Act |

You may owe both, one, or neither. The May return does not satisfy the June
report, and little taxable income does not remove the report. Who has to file
in May is [its own guide](/business/may-global-income-tax-filing-for-foreigners/).

The **foreign tax credit** is a third thing again. An overseas brokerage
holding ₩600 million that receives dividends with foreign tax withheld raises
three separate questions: are the dividends taxable in Korea; can the foreign
tax be credited; does the account meet the June requirements. The answers can
differ. [The credit](/business/foreign-tax-credit-in-korea/) does not replace
the report, and the report does not establish a credit.

## Five mistakes, and a sixth

**Checking only 31 December.** The test runs at every month-end; an earlier
month can create the obligation.

**Treating ₩500m as per-account.** Qualifying accounts are combined.

**Using gross deposits instead of prescribed values.** Incoming transfers
during the year are not month-end account values.

**Assuming shares are not reportable until sold.** Prescribed market values can
apply; realisation is a separate question.

**Assuming no income tax means no report.** Two separate obligations.

And for foreign residents: **do not assume the five-in-ten-year exemption
lasts because you hold a foreign passport.** It depends on the cumulative
residence period, and it ends on its own.

## The order

```
residence
  → exemption
  → which accounts qualify
  → twelve month-end values
  → highest combined balance
  → June report
```

Confirm the reporting year — June concerns last year. Establish year-end
Korean residence. Review the exemptions, the five-in-ten-year test first.
Identify every qualifying account, without assuming all foreign assets are
financial accounts. Value them at each month-end on the proper valuation and
exchange rules. Find the highest combined month-end. Compare against ₩500
million, strictly exceeded. Prepare the account and ownership information.
File through Hometax or SonTax. Keep the confirmation, the statements and the
calculations.

That is more reliable than starting from a list of foreign bank accounts and
assuming each one must be reported.

Because the obligation is not about how much foreign income you earned, and it
is not about where the money originally came from. **It is about the legal
structure of the accounts and their prescribed values on twelve particular
days.**
