The hard part of K-ETA is not the form. It is knowing whether you should be
applying at all — and then noticing that the passport scan typed something
slightly wrong.

This is a walkthrough of the official application at `www.k-eta.go.kr`, taken
screen by screen. Four things on it are worth knowing before you start, and
none of them appears in the summaries.

## What the site says it costs and how long it takes

Straight off the homepage:

```
Fee                   KRW 10,000        (about USD 7–8)
Assessment            generally within 72 hours
Where                 www.k-eta.go.kr, or the K-ETA mobile app
```

The fee page adds an online payment processing charge of roughly 3% on top,
and states that the fee covers processing the application — it is **not
refunded if the application is denied.**

The homepage carries its own warning about this, and it is worth reading as
written: be careful of similar agencies and phishing sites charging high fees
under the pretext of providing K-ETA application agency **or expedited**
services. There is no official fast track. The 72 hours is the 72 hours.

<div class="callout callout-warn">
  <p class="callout-title">⚠️ Check eligibility before you pay, not after</p>
  <p>The fee is not refunded if the application is denied, and the temporary exemption means some travellers are paying for something they do not currently need. Settle “do I need this” first.</p>
</div>

## The seven steps, as the site numbers them

```
STEP 1   Terms and Conditions Agreement
STEP 2   Email Verification
STEP 3   Enter Passport Information
STEP 4   Enter Application Information
STEP 5   Check Information
STEP 6   Make Payment
STEP 7   Application Complete
```

A progress rail across the top of every screen shows which one you are on.

## Member or non-member

Two tabs on the homepage. The site states the commonalities itself: the
application fee, the assessment time and the permit validity period are the
same for both.

What membership adds is narrower than people assume — you can check results and
update travel information without going through email verification again, and
you can see all your applications at once. For one trip, non-member and **Apply
from the beginning** is the shorter path.

## STEP 1 — Four consents, not one

The terms screen splits into four separate agreements, and the required block
is broader than most travellers expect.

  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">1</div><div class="step-body"><strong>Terms of use.</strong> Chapter 1, General Provisions, and the rest.</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">2</div><div class="step-body"><strong>Required personal information.</strong> Email, ID photo, copy of the passport bio page, personal identification number, purpose of entry, intended place of stay, contact information, expected date of arrival, expected date of departure, occupation.</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">3</div><div class="step-body"><strong>Optional personal information.</strong> The review-related items — whether you have visited Korea before, multiple nationalities, country and place of birth, current residence, name changes, acquaintances in Korea, accompanying travellers, economic activity, income, and history of visiting other countries.   <div class="step"><div class="step-num">4</div><div class="step-body"><strong>Sensitive information.</strong> Information on infectious disease, overseas criminal record, and criminal record in Korea.</div></div>

Retention is listed as semi-permanent, and the basis given is the Immigration
Act and its Enforcement Decree.

Then one question that catches families:

<div class="callout callout-note">
  <p class="callout-title">Are you 14 or older?</p>
  <p>The screen asks, and adds that when applying as a non-member, a child under 14 <strong>cannot be designated as the Representative</strong>. Someone else has to hold that role.</p>
</div>

## STEP 2 — Email verification, and the Gmail problem

Enter an address, press **Send Verification Code**, and a six-digit number
arrives with the subject `[K-ETA] Application Authentication number`. Paste it
in and press **Verify**; the screen answers `Verification succeeded.`

**You have five minutes.** The timer beside the Verification Number field
starts at `05:00` and counts down from the moment the code is sent — the limit
belongs to the entry screen, not to the email sitting in your inbox. A
**Resend** button sits next to it, so a code that runs out is not the end of
the application.

The part worth planning around is what the site displays the moment the code is
sent:

<div class="callout callout-warn">
  <p class="callout-title">⚠️ The site's own words</p>
  <p>“The verification number has been sent. Please note that verification codes may be delayed when using Gmail. If you do not receive the code, we kindly request that you use a different email address.”</p>
</div>

A government site naming one mail provider is unusual enough to take at face
value. If you have a non-Gmail address, use it here — and remember the address
is how you will check the result afterwards as a non-member.

## STEP 3 — The passport upload

Upload a photo of the passport information page. The sample the site shows
marks a red box around the whole bio page **including the machine-readable zone
at the bottom** — those two lines of `<<<` characters have to be in the frame.
Once uploaded, the personal information is filled in automatically.

Before the upload dialog will let you proceed, a warning appears. This is the
strongest language anywhere in the application:

<div class="callout callout-warn">
  <p class="callout-title">⚠️ AI enhancement</p>
  <p>“AI enhancement may cause image distortion. Please do not use AI photo editors when taking a passport photo. The passport bio-page image uploaded for your application must match the passport you present at immigration control. Any modification, alteration, or forgery of the image is strictly prohibited. Failure to comply may result in the denial or cancellation of your application and may lead to legal penalties under relevant laws, including the Immigration Act.”</p>
</div>

This rules out more than forgery. Phone cameras now ship with generative
"enhance" and "clean up" features that rewrite characters they think are
blurred — and a passport number is exactly the kind of small, high-contrast
text those tools rebuild. Turn that off before you photograph the page.

After selecting the file, the site opens a small editor with three tools:
**Crop**, **Turn**, **Margin**, and an **add** button. That is the extent of
the editing you are meant to do — make the page readable, straight and fully
in frame. Keep both the printed details and the MRZ inside the crop.

## STEP 3 continued — check what the scan typed

The recognised values come back as a form: Nationality, Surname, Given Name,
Passport Number, Sex, Date of Birth, Passport Expiration Date. Above them sits
a warning that tells you exactly where to look:

<div class="callout callout-warn">
  <p class="callout-title">⚠️ O and 0</p>
  <p>“In some cases, contamination or light reflection of your passport bio page may cause incorrect input of your passport information. Please double-check to ensure the information is correct. (※ In particular, please exercise caution when distinguishing between the letter 'O' and the numeral '0'.) The applicant is responsible for any problems that arise from failing to double check incorrect information.”</p>
</div>

That last sentence is the operative one. The scan is a convenience and the
error is yours.

<div class="check-card">
  <div class="check-row ok"><span class="mark"></span><span>Read the <strong>passport number</strong> off the passport, character by character — O against 0, and I against 1 while you are there.</span></div>
  <div class="check-row ok"><span class="mark"></span><span><strong>Surname and given name</strong> in the right boxes, spelled as printed.</span></div>
  <div class="check-row ok"><span class="mark"></span><span><strong>Expiry date</strong> — a scan that misreads a year is not obviously wrong on screen.</span></div>
</div>

## STEP 3 ends — the nationality check

This is the part no summary shows, because you only see it if you are not
eligible.

Confirm the passport information and the system checks the nationality it just
read. Ours was a Republic of Korea passport, used to document the process, and
the application stopped:

```
This is not a country eligible for K-ETA application.
Please check your nationality.
```

Which is correct — a Korean national does not apply for K-ETA. Two things
follow from seeing it.

**The check is real, and it is late.** The site is not taking any passport and
walking it to the payment screen. But it also does not run the check until
after you have consented, verified an email, photographed a passport and
uploaded it. Settle your eligibility on the guide pages first rather than
letting the form tell you at step three.

**We stopped there.** We did not enter a different nationality or use someone
else's passport to see the remaining screens. The warning two sections up says
what that would be, and a walkthrough is not worth it.

So the rest of this is the official procedure rather than our screens, and it
is marked as such.

## Do not apply the day before you fly

Assessment is *generally* within 72 hours, which is not the same as a maximum,
and the site says so. There is no official expedited service to fall back on if
it runs long, and the fee is gone whether or not the answer arrives in time.

## Four things, and none of them replaces another

Before the last section, the orientation that saves most of the arguing:

| | What it decides |
| --- | --- |
| Visa-free entry | Whether you may enter Korea at all without obtaining a visa |
| K-ETA | Whether a visa-free traveller needs authorisation before boarding |
| Visa | The route when nationality, length of stay or activity puts you outside visa-free entry |
| e-Arrival Card | Whether you file an arrival declaration, whichever of the above got you here |

They answer in that order, and the order is the point. *Can I enter without a
visa* comes first; *do I need K-ETA* only arises if the answer was yes; and the
declaration is asked of everyone, decided by what you actually enter on.

## And then check the other form

This is where most of the confusion on this subject actually lives.

| You are | K-ETA | e-Arrival Card |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Inside the temporary exemption, no K-ETA applied for | Not required | **You file it** |
| Holding an approved K-ETA, entering on it | Held | Excluded |
| Holding a K-ETA but entering on a visa | Held | **You file it** |

The middle row is the reason some exempt travellers apply anyway: an approved
K-ETA carries an exemption from the arrival declaration. The top row is the one
that catches people — being waived out of K-ETA removes the very thing that
would have excluded you from the other form.

## Before you start, have these ready

```
Passport                  the one you will actually enter Korea on
A photo of the bio page   whole page, MRZ included, no AI editing
An ID-style face photo
An email address          not Gmail, if you have a choice
Purpose of entry
Address in Korea          where you will actually stay
A contact number in Korea
Arrival and departure dates
Occupation
A card that works abroad
```

## The short version

Official site only, KRW 10,000, no refund if denied, generally 72 hours and
sometimes longer. Photograph the passport plainly and let the site's own crop
tool do the rest. Read the passport number back off the passport, watching O
against 0. And before any of that, find out whether you need it — the form
will not tell you until step three.
