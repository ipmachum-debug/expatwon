Everything in the general dependant test applies to a foreign family member
without modification. What is added is a residence condition, and a documentary
problem: the insurance service cannot look your family up.

## The 2024 condition

From **3 April 2024**, a residence condition applies to foreign dependants under
Article 109 of the National Health Insurance Act, with transitional provisions
applying it to those entering from that date.

The stated purpose was to reduce arrivals registering as dependants, obtaining
expensive treatment and departing.

Practically: a foreign parent or adult family member generally needs
[six months of residence in Korea](/tracked/foreign-dependant-residence-requirement/)
before the registration can proceed — on top of everything else.

## Two mechanisms that are not the same

This is where most published guidance goes wrong.

| | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| Spouse, children under 19, spouse's children under 19 | **The Act excludes them** from the residence condition |
| Statuses indicating residence of six months or more is evident | A **separate acquisition-timing rule**, not an exemption from this one |

<div class="callout callout-warn">
  <p class="callout-title">So a visa list is the wrong shape of answer</p>
  <p>"D-2, E-9, F-5 and F-6 are exempt from the six months" describes the second
  mechanism using the vocabulary of the first. The difference is not academic: it
  changes which provision you are relying on and therefore what you are being
  asked to evidence. If you bring a spouse and a young child, you are in the first
  row and the wait does not arise.</p>
</div>

## Six months is not a calendar count

Residence is assessed against **immigration records**, and long absences affect
continuity. The rules also deal with the acquisition point itself, including
where the person is briefly outside Korea at that moment.

```
enter → leave for two months → return → "that's six months on the calendar"
                    ── not how it is assessed ──
```

Bringing a parent in and out to accumulate days is precisely the pattern the
rule addresses. Plan for continuous residence, and check the position with the
insurance service rather than counting stamps.

## Overseas family documents

Korean family registration records will not show your relationship. So it has to
be evidenced with documents from the home country — a marriage certificate,
birth certificate or family relationship record — in a form the service can rely
on.

  <p class="callout-title">A translation your cousin did is not a step</p>
  <p>It is the most common reason a well-prepared application is sent back. The
  form of the translation is specified, and a Word file produced by a family
  member does not satisfy it.</p>
</div>

## Do not fix a validity period from an article

You will see "documents must be issued within 90 days" or "within nine months"
stated as a rule. Treat both with suspicion: the requirements specify how
documents are authenticated and translated, and it is not safe to assert one
universal validity window across every document type and application.

So sequence it this way, and ask rather than assume:

```
recent original
  → apostille or consular authentication, per country
  → Korean translation in an accepted form
  → confirm the accepted issue window with NHIS before paying
```

Authentication and certified translation cost money and take time in another
country. Confirming first is cheap; redoing it is not.

## The general tests still apply, all of them

Clearing the residence condition does not register anyone. The relationship,
support, [income](/tracked/nhis-dependent-income-threshold/) and property
conditions apply in full and unchanged — including the business income rule, the
requirement that both spouses satisfy the income condition where the prospective
dependant is married, and the property assessment base with its middle band.

They are set out in
[NHIS dependants: the income and property tests](/insurance/nhis-dependants-the-income-and-property-tests/).

<div class="callout callout-note">
  <p class="callout-title">A retired parent abroad is not automatically low-income</p>
  <p>An overseas pension counts. So does investment income. The most common shape
  of failure here is a family that solved the residence problem and the document
  problem, and then met the income ceiling.</p>
</div>

## Status is rechecked, and status of stay is part of it

Dependant status is re-verified against income and property data. For a foreign
family member there is an additional moving part: **changes in the family
relationship or status of stay** can end it.

So a family member whose status changes, or whose stay is not extended, is not
only an immigration matter — it is a health insurance one, and the coverage can
stop without a separate warning.

Where dependant status is lost, regional enrolment generally follows, with
premiums assessed on that person directly. Which of the three enrolment
positions applies to whom is in
[NHIS health insurance for foreigners](/insurance/nhis-korean-health-insurance-for-foreigners/).

## And while you are waiting

A family member who is in Korea but not yet registrable is uninsured for that
period, and Korean medical costs without cover are not a rounding difference on
a household budget. What that actually looks like, and how prices can be checked
in advance, is in
[hospital costs without Korean health insurance](/insurance/hospital-costs-without-korean-health-insurance/).

For a parent arriving on a long visit, that gap is the thing to plan for
deliberately — private cover for the interim, or timing the visit around the
condition — rather than something to discover at a hospital reception desk in
month three.

## The order for a foreign family member

```
relationship category
  → is this person statutorily exempt from the residence condition?
      no → six months' residence, assessed on immigration records
  → support in fact
  → income, including the business rule and both spouses
  → property, on the assessment base
  → overseas documents: authenticated, translated, in the accepted form
  → registration
  → cover for the interim, if there is one
```

Nationality is not an input anywhere in that list. What is added for a foreign
family is a wait, and a stack of paper that has to arrive in the right form from
another country — both of which are manageable, and neither of which is
manageable in the week your mother lands.
