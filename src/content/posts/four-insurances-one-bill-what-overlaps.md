Someone moving between countries can hold four things at once: the Korean
national scheme, a Korean private indemnity policy, travel insurance, and a
global expat policy.

More policies do not mean more money. Whether cover stacks or overlaps is
decided by a single distinction, and it does not follow the product names.

<p class="hand">Optimising this is not cancelling policies. It is sorting them.</p>

## The national scheme is not one of the private layers

Korean national health insurance is public insurance under statute. It is not a
private policy sharing a bill with other private policies.

The order is sequential, not parallel:

```
covered treatment
  → the national scheme pays its share
  → you pay the statutory portion
      → private indemnity insurance reimburses part of
        what you paid, on its own terms
```

So the national scheme is first-layer public cover and private indemnity
insurance is what comes after it — never a co-insurer splitting the same bill.
How the covered and non-covered halves of a Korean bill actually work is in
[what Korean health insurance does not cover](/insurance/what-korean-health-insurance-does-not-cover/).

## Korean indemnity cover is shared, not multiplied

Indemnity insurance reimburses what you actually spent. Two such policies do not
produce two payments:

```
hospital bill      ₩1,000,000
policy A                     ┐
policy B                     ┘ together, up to what you actually paid
```

The regulator's position is explicit: duplicate indemnity cover is settled
proportionally between insurers within the limit of the medical costs actually
borne. Which makes two similar Korean indemnity policies mostly a duplicated
premium.

<div class="callout callout-note">
  <p class="callout-title">Which is why it is the first place to look for savings</p>
  <p>Not because insurance is wasteful in general, but because this particular
  overlap is the one that provably cannot pay you more.</p>
</div>

## Fixed-benefit cover is the opposite case

Cover that pays an **agreed sum on a defined event** — accidental death,
permanent disability, a cancer diagnosis benefit, a specified surgery benefit —
can pay from each contract that is triggered.

```
accidental death, travel policy   ₩100,000,000
accidental death, life policy     ₩200,000,000
        both, where the terms are met
```

<div class="callout callout-warn">
  <p class="callout-title">So "duplicate insurance is waste" cuts too much</p>
  <p>Applied to fixed-benefit cover it removes protection that genuinely
  accumulates. The sensible move is asymmetric: reduce overlapping indemnity
  cover, keep the fixed-benefit risks deliberately.</p>
</div>

## Travel insurance is a bundle, not a category

A travel policy is several covers in one contract:

| Cover | Type |
|---|---|
| Overseas medical costs | Indemnity |
| Personal belongings | Indemnity |
| Personal liability | Indemnity |
| Flight and baggage delay | Varies by policy |
| Accidental death | Fixed benefit |
| Permanent disability | Fixed benefit |

So "is my travel insurance duplicated" has no single answer. The medical and
belongings covers may duplicate something you already hold; the death and
disability benefits generally do not.

## Global policies: read the clause, not a rule

Expat and global health policies often have wider networks and longer terms than
travel cover, and may offer direct billing or a guarantee of payment to the
hospital.

What they do **not** have is a uniform relationship to your Korean cover. Their
terms make them **primary, secondary or excess**, and may deduct what another
insurer has already paid under a coordination-of-benefits clause.

<div class="callout callout-warn">
  <p class="callout-title">So this is not a plan</p>
  <p>Claim ₩500,000 of your own share from a Korean indemnity policy, then claim
  the same ₩500,000 in full from a global policy. Insurers ask what other cover
  exists and what has been paid; Korean duplicate indemnity cover is recorded and
  visible; global insurers request the other insurer's settlement documents. This
  is not a loophole, it is the thing the clause exists to prevent.</p>
</div>

## A worked case

```
hospital bill                      ₩1,500,000
national scheme applies
your share                           ₩500,000
Korean indemnity policy pays         ₩350,000
                                     ─────────
still out of pocket                  ₩150,000
```

Whether a global policy then meets the ₩150,000, or something more, or nothing,
is answered by its other-insurance clause. What it will not straightforwardly do
is pay the whole ₩500,000 again.

## Where the real gap usually is

The instinct is to hunt for duplicated medical cover. The more common defect is
the opposite: heavy medical cover and nothing at all for the risks around it.

<div class="steps">
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">1</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>Medical evacuation and repatriation</strong> — a genuine strength of global policies, absent from most Korean cover.</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">2</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>Personal liability abroad</strong>, which the national scheme has nothing to do with.</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">3</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>Trip cancellation, delay, lost belongings</strong> — travel-policy territory that a global medical policy may not touch.</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">4</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>Fixed-benefit death and disability</strong>, which do not overlap with medical reimbursement at all.</div></div>
</div>

Which is the argument against cancelling a travel policy outright because a
global medical policy exists. Cancel the duplicated **medical component** if
that is what duplicates; the rest of the contract may be the only thing covering
a delayed flight or a liability claim.

## The expat policy does not replace the national scheme

Where someone meets the conditions for enrolment — a workplace subscriber, for
instance — that is not an election. Employer-provided global cover does not
substitute for it.

What the global policy adds sits above: English-language support, treatment
outside Korea, evacuation, non-covered treatment here, care in the home country.

```
expat policy exists  →  therefore no NHIS      ✗
```

## Before arriving, check five things in your home policy

Not the premium:

<div class="steps">
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">1</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>Is South Korea inside the area of cover?</strong></div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">2</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>Is routine outpatient care covered</strong>, or only inpatient and emergency?</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">3</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>How are pre-existing conditions treated?</strong></div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">4</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>Can Korean hospitals bill it directly</strong>, or is it pay-and-claim?</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">5</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>Does it require the national scheme to be used first?</strong></div></div>
</div>

Area bands matter more than they look — "worldwide excluding USA" and "worldwide
including USA" are different products at different prices, and a policy bought
for one region may not behave as expected in another. The claim mechanics on the
Korean side are in
[claiming Korean medical costs on foreign insurance](/insurance/claiming-korean-medical-costs-on-foreign-insurance/).

## Sort by where you will be treated

| Situation | Structure |
|---|---|
| Living in Korea | National scheme, plus Korean private cover if useful |
| Business trips and holidays abroad | Travel policy, chosen for what the global one lacks |
| Working across countries long-term | Global or expat medical cover |
| Posted abroad from Korea | Existing Korean cover paused if possible, plus global cover |

And note that Korean private indemnity cover is defined by the **generation** of
the policy — the one on sale changed in
[May 2026](/tracked/private-health-insurance-generation-on-sale/) — so "my
Korean indemnity policy" is not a single thing either.

## What optimisation actually is

Not cancelling. Sorting:

```
list every policy and what it covers
  → classify: Korean medical, overseas medical,
    death and disability, evacuation, travel and liability
  → two or more indemnity covers on the same loss?
      → check what can be suspended, and the premium
  → keep fixed-benefit cover, regardless of medical overlap
  → find the categories with nothing in them
```

Two questions, not one. Are you paying twice for the same loss — and is
something uninsured while you were busy insuring your medical costs three times.

What can actually be paused, and the several similar-looking periods that govern
it, is the other half of today's pair.
