You pay for software, internet, equipment, client meetings and transport.
Sometimes on a personal card. Sometimes from home. Occasionally for something
that serves the business and your life at once.

May arrives. Which ones count?

Not whichever account paid, and not whichever receipt has the business name on
it. Korea allows expenses that are **ordinary, acceptable and connected with
earning the revenue** — and then the nature of the cost, its timing, its
evidence and specific statutory restrictions all bear on it.

A software subscription can qualify. A holiday does not qualify because you
answered a client email during it. A laptop may be deductible over time rather
than at once. December inventory may still be an asset at year end.

**What the money bought and why decides it. Not which card.**

ℹ️ The wider calculation this sits inside — revenue against expenses,
inventory, depreciation, owner's draw, VAT, bookkeeping obligations — is
[income tax for foreign sole
proprietors](/business/korean-income-tax-for-foreign-sole-proprietors/). This
guide is the expense side: what qualifies, what is excluded outright, and what
you have to be able to show.

## Five tests

| Test | What to establish |
|---|---|
| Business purpose | Was it genuinely related to earning business income? |
| Legal eligibility | Does the law allow this type of expense? |
| Documentation | Can the transaction *and* its purpose be substantiated? |
| Correct period | Does it belong to this tax year? |
| Correct treatment | Immediate expense, inventory, depreciable asset, or something else? |

## Two articles, working together

**Article 27** sets the rule: necessary expenses (필요경비) are the ordinary
and acceptable expenses corresponding to the tax period's gross revenue. Three
requirements hide in one sentence — a connection to the income, ordinariness
and acceptability, and the correct period and treatment.

**Article 33** lists what cannot be included: personal and household expenses,
income tax and individual local income tax, certain penalties and fines,
depreciation above the allowable amount, specified input VAT, and
business-unrelated expenditure.

They work together, and that is the part people miss. An expense can look
connected to a business and still meet a specific restriction. And an expense
does not become deductible because it is absent from some informal list of
banned payments.

**Business relevance is necessary. It is not always sufficient.**

A designer's software has an understandable connection. The same designer's
groceries do not become business expenses because they work from home. Both
can appear on one card statement — the statement does not classify them.

## A receipt is evidence, not permission

Buy a ₩500,000 item on a card registered with Hometax and it appears in the
business-card records automatically. That does not establish a deduction. The
NTS is explicit that registration collects transaction data; it does not decide
whether a purchase is business-related. (What the registration service actually
does is [registering a business credit card on
Hometax](/business/registering-a-business-card-on-hometax/).)

A receipt gives you merchant, date, amount, payment method. It does not give
you *why*. Three transactions at one electronics shop — equipment for the
store, a television for the living room, a device used for both — produce
near-identical receipts and three different answers.

### What Korea actually requires

**적격증빙** — prescribed supporting evidence: tax invoices (세금계산서),
invoices (계산서), credit-card sales slips (신용카드매출전표), cash receipts
(현금영수증).

For business purchases of goods or services from another business, **over
₩30,000 including VAT** generally requires one of these, subject to statutory
exceptions.

🔴 Read that precisely. It is a **documentation** rule. It does not mean
expenses over ₩30,000 are automatically deductible, and it does not mean
anything at or under ₩30,000 needs no evidence at all — the obligation to
obtain the *prescribed form* relaxes, and the NTS still recommends keeping
records for small transactions.

⚠️ Nor is it a loophole: splitting one real ₩100,000 purchase into several
fabricated smaller ones does not put it below the threshold. The actual
transaction controls.

## Cash, personal cards and Saturdays

The NTS's 2026 guidance for entrepreneurs settles three things people assume
are fatal.

**Cash is fine.** Business-related costs paid in cash can be expenses where the
required evidence is kept. A photographer paying ₩150,000 cash for supplies,
with the supplier's cash receipt and a genuine business connection, is not
rejected for paying cash.

🔴 What fails is the ATM withdrawal. ₩150,000 leaving the account proves money
moved, not what it bought — supplies, shopping, or something else. **A
withdrawal is not a receipt**, and it should not be entered into the books as
an expense.

**A personal card is fine.** Genuine business purchases on an eligible card in
the owner's own name can be recognised, and the guidance also identifies
circumstances where an employee's or family member's card qualifies when the
business connection is objectively established.

That is not permission to claim every family-card transaction. The payer, the
actual purchaser, the reimbursement arrangement and the evidence all matter —
and **reimbursement moves money without changing what the original purchase
was.**

**A weekend is fine.** An expense does not become personal because it happened
on a Saturday. Event photographers work weekends; restaurants buy ingredients
on Sunday; advertising runs on holidays.

But the NTS warns these get mistaken for personal spending when the purpose is
not established — so keep the booking, the contract, the schedule, the invoice,
the delivery record. A consultant's Saturday hotel on a client trip is
defensible. A family holiday is not business travel because emails were
answered.

**The calendar day does not decide. The purpose does.**

## Mixed use: the allocation has to be real

**Home.** Rent, electricity, internet. A genuinely attributable portion may be
examinable — the whole apartment does not become business premises because
someone owns a laptop.

On ₩1,500,000 monthly rent with a supportable 20% business allocation, that is
₩300,000 a month and ₩3,600,000 a year. 🔴 **20% is not a Korean allowance or a
safe harbour.** It is a number invented for this paragraph. The real allocation
has to reflect the lease, the space, the activity, the actual use and the
evidence — and for a foreign resident, whether the premises may lawfully be
used for the business is a separate question from the tax one.

**Phone and internet.** One connection for work, entertainment and the
household. A genuine business component may be identifiable; deducting 100%
because the phone is occasionally used for clients is not.

₩100,000 a month at a substantiated 60% is ₩60,000, or ₩720,000 a year — and
the 60% is hypothetical too. In practice the overlap makes this hard to
evidence, which is why a separate business line simplifies it.

⚠️ Whatever the basis, be consistent. **Applying a different unsupported
percentage each year to land on a convenient result is the problem, not the
percentage.**

## Software, and what the subscription is for

Development tools, design software, cloud hosting, accounting systems, meeting
software, store management — all plausibly connected to earning revenue, and
none deductible because of the label on the product page. Something used only
for entertainment is not a business expense, and mixed use needs review.

The treatment can also turn on the nature of the payment: an ordinary recurring
subscription is not the same as acquiring a long-lived software asset or
intangible rights. And overseas software can raise VAT, withholding,
foreign-exchange and international tax questions of its own — keep the foreign
invoice, the payment confirmation and the business-use record.

**Ask what the software does for the business, not what its marketing says.**

## Marketing: failure is allowed, holidays are not

Search advertising, promotional design, a business website — ordinary expenses
when connected and documented. A content creator travelling to produce a paid
client video can examine travel, accommodation and production costs against the
project.

The same creator taking a holiday and posting about it afterwards has not
converted the holiday. The commercial activity, the contract, the delivered
work and the allocation of mixed costs decide it. For significant spend, keep
the invoices, the campaign contract, the payment confirmations, records of what
was delivered, and the link to the business.

⭐ And note what is *not* required: the advertisement does not have to work. A
legitimate campaign can fail commercially and remain an expense — Article 27 is
about the relationship to earning income, not about succeeding. **Commercial
failure is not a personal expense. But calling personal spending a failed
experiment does not change it either.**

## Client meals have their own regime

A client lunch may fall under business entertainment and promotion expenses —
**기업업무추진비**, long known as 접대비 — which carry their own limits,
documentation and classification rules.

🔴 You cannot escape those rules by booking every restaurant payment as
ordinary meals or staff welfare.

| Situation | Treatment to examine |
|---|---|
| Meeting a prospective customer | Business-related, but the promotion rules apply |
| Meals provided to employees in qualifying circumstances | Employee welfare or another appropriate category |
| Family dinner with no business purpose | Not deductible |

Three restaurants, three different answers. Participants, purpose, amount and
records decide it — and **no rule makes every meal on a business card
deductible.**

**Gifts and congratulatory payments** sit nearby. The 2026 guidance identifies
certain employee congratulatory and condolence payments as potentially
allowable welfare expenses where socially reasonable and supported, with
specific conditions for payments relating to a counterparty's employees. Not
unlimited allowances — the relationship, evidence, amount and statutory limits
all apply. A gift to a friend is not a business expense because the friend
might one day buy something, and compensation for services should not be
relabelled a gift to avoid withholding.

## Travel: the destination proves nothing

A trip to Hanoi for supplier meetings and factory inspections, with documented
appointments, can be examined as business travel. Stay on for a private
holiday and the personal portion is not swept in with it. Combined purposes may
need allocation, and **no rule makes a trip fully deductible because one
meeting happened.**

Keep the meeting invitations and correspondence, the contracts, the itinerary,
the accommodation and transport invoices, and evidence of what was actually
done.

**Overseas spending** is likewise not disqualified by being overseas — but it
adds currency conversion into won under the applicable rules, foreign invoice
formats, possible Korean withholding or VAT, and customs and freight where an
import affects inventory cost. Keep the contract, the foreign invoice and the
payment confirmation, and keep the basis for the won amount. 🔴 **The exchange
rate follows the applicable treatment, not whichever rate maximises the
deduction.**

## Loan principal is not an expense

Borrow ₩30 million for equipment, repay ₩1,000,000 of principal and ₩150,000 of
interest in a month, and the deductible figure is not ₩1,150,000 because both
went to the bank.

| Payment | General position |
|---|---|
| Loan principal | Settles a liability, not an expense |
| Qualifying business loan interest | May be deductible under the applicable rules |
| Personal loan interest | Not automatically a business expense |
| Interest tied to a qualifying capital asset | May need special treatment |

Interest can qualify where the borrowing genuinely funded the business, with
restrictions for business-unrelated borrowing and certain construction
financing. **The purpose of the loan and the actual use of the funds have to be
established** — a recent Supreme Court decision on a joint business turned on
exactly that distinction between personal financing and borrowing attributable
to the business.

## Vehicles are restricted by name

Article 33-2 restricts necessary expenses for business-use passenger vehicles
for taxpayers within its scope, and can involve usage records, insurance
requirements in relevant cases and depreciation limits. The NTS publishes
dedicated guidance on it.

⚠️ Older NTS materials carry figures such as ₩15 million for certain annual
expense-recording situations and ₩8 million for relevant depreciation
calculations. **Do not lift those as universal deductions** without checking
what applies to your vehicle, activity and taxpayer classification — a
commercial transport vehicle is not treated like an ordinary passenger car.

And the mixed-use problem returns: the same car for client visits and family
outings does not make all the fuel, insurance and maintenance deductible. A
mileage or usage record supports the split where relevant.

**A car registered to a business owner is not automatically a deductible
business vehicle.**

## What Article 33 takes off the table

**Income tax is not a business expense.** A proprietor with ₩30 million of
business income who pays ₩3 million of combined personal income tax does not
reduce the business income to ₩27 million. The tax is the settlement of what
the calculation produced, not a cost of producing it.

⚠️ Do not generalise from the word "tax" — some taxes and government fees
connected with business activity may be deductible under their own rules, while
others are specifically excluded. The category matters.

**Fines are not either.** Administrative fines, criminal fines, certain tax
penalties and collection charges, payments arising from specified withholding
failures. Paying from the business account changes nothing. A legitimate
regulatory fee incurred in normal operations is not the same as a penalty for
breaking a rule, so classify each by its legal character.

**A business can be liable for a fine and not be allowed to deduct it.**

## People: who is actually being paid

**Your own withdrawals are not wages.** A sole proprietorship is not a separate
legal person, so a transfer to your personal account is an owner's draw — ₩24
million of business income with ₩18 million withdrawn is still ₩24 million. The
full treatment, including money going the other way, is in [the sole proprietor
guide](/business/korean-income-tax-for-foreign-sole-proprietors/).

**Family can be employed, if the work is real.** The NTS has recognised that
qualifying compensation to related persons who actually work in the business
can be a necessary expense. The work performed, the arrangement, its
reasonableness and the records decide it — transferring money to a relative
does not make it wages, payroll withholding and social insurance may follow,
and ordinary household support is not employee compensation.

**Contractors need more than a transfer.** Pay a freelance designer ₩1,000,000
and a genuine service fee may be an expense — but if it is qualifying
personal-service business income you may have national and local withholding
obligations, a withholding receipt to issue and income information to submit. A
bank transfer settles none of that. Record the service, the gross compensation,
the recipient, the invoice, the payment and the withholding treatment. The
payer-side duties are [who withholds
what](/business/withholding-tax-for-freelancers-korea/).

🔴 **Getting the expense right does not discharge the withholding obligation.**

## Timing problems

**Inventory and equipment** both separate payment from deduction — buying stock
is not cost of goods sold, and equipment may be a depreciable asset rather than
this year's expense. Both are worked through in [the sole proprietor
guide](/business/korean-income-tax-for-foreign-sole-proprietors/); the
principle here is only that **the cash date is not automatically the deduction
date.**

**Services spanning periods** may need allocation, and a prepaid subscription
covering next year is not the same as a service already received. Article 27 is
about expenses corresponding to the period's revenue.

**Pre-opening costs** — advice, research, development, equipment paid for
before registration — are not automatically first-year expenses, and a later
registration does not retroactively validate earlier personal spending. Some
may be capital or formation-related. Keep the evidence of activity, purpose,
date and payment, and confirm the treatment before filing.

**A forgotten invoice** goes in the year it belongs to, not the year that
produces the lowest tax. Work out the correct period, check whether the records
and return are final, and use the proper correction procedure. 🔴 **Do not
delete the original transaction and recreate it under a different date** — the
tax authority may already hold the electronic records, and the integrity of the
books is the point.

## VAT, once

Recoverable input VAT is not a second income-tax deduction. On a ₩1,000,000
purchase plus ₩100,000 VAT, the cost is ₩1,000,000 and the ₩100,000 is
recovered through the VAT system. Not all input VAT is recoverable —
VAT-exempt activity and input-tax restrictions change it, and Article 33 has
provisions on this — so nonrecoverable VAT needs its own treatment and may form
part of a cost or asset.

**Do not claim the same VAT through two tax calculations.** The regimes are in
[Korean VAT for small business
owners](/business/korean-vat-for-small-business-owners/).

## One invoice, several categories

An online order of ₩300,000 containing ₩150,000 of storage supplies, ₩30,000 of
printer paper and ₩120,000 of household products is ₩180,000 of business
purchase and ₩120,000 of personal — in one card transaction. The records should
split it, and the recoverable VAT follows the eligible items.

This is why an **itemised invoice** is worth keeping when the payment
confirmation alone is not.

**Platform settlements** have the same shape. ₩10,000,000 of gross sales less a
₩1,000,000 commission arrives as ₩9,000,000. Where the platform collects on
your behalf and charges a separable commission, that is ₩10m of revenue and ₩1m
of expense. 🔴 **What you cannot do is report ₩10m, deduct the commission, and
deduct it again because only ₩9m landed.** Read the settlement statements, not
the deposits — and the right presentation depends on the actual arrangement and
a principal-versus-agent analysis, with cross-border VAT and withholding
alongside.

## What the ledger needs

| Field | Why |
|---|---|
| Date | When it happened |
| Supplier | Who provided it |
| Description | What was bought |
| Gross amount | What was paid |
| VAT component | Recoverable, nonrecoverable or neither |
| Business-use amount | The attributable portion |
| Expense or asset category | The treatment |
| Supporting document | The link to the evidence |
| Related project or product | The business purpose |
| Payment reference | Reconciliation to bank or card |

A spreadsheet of merchant names and amounts is not enough if it never says
*why*. An ERP importing every card transaction without separating personal from
business is not enough either. **Automation collects transactions; it does not
establish their character.**

Keep it all **five years** after the filing deadline, with longer periods in
certain loss-carryforward cases and under other laws. Electronic storage works
only while the records stay accessible and linked — **a folder of thousands of
unlabelled receipt images is worth much less than an indexed record.**

## A worked case

A consultant with ₩50 million of revenue and this spending:

| Expenditure | Amount | Review |
|---|---:|---|
| Professional software | ₩2,400,000 | Business |
| Client project travel | ₩1,800,000 | Business |
| Personal holiday | ₩2,000,000 | Personal |
| Home internet | ₩1,200,000 | Mixed |
| Dedicated workspace | ₩6,000,000 | Check eligibility |
| Laptop | ₩2,400,000 | Asset treatment |
| Professional liability insurance | ₩900,000 | Check policy and relevance |
| Groceries | ₩3,000,000 | Personal |

Assume the software, travel and insurance qualify; the workspace qualifies in
full; the internet has a substantiated 50% business portion; the laptop's
allowable current-year depreciation is ₩600,000; the holiday and groceries do
not qualify.

```
software            ₩2,400,000
client travel       ₩1,800,000
internet (50%)        ₩600,000
workspace           ₩6,000,000
insurance             ₩900,000
laptop depreciation   ₩600,000
                   ₩12,300,000

₩50,000,000 − ₩12,300,000 = ₩37,700,000 business income
```

Total recorded spending was ₩19,700,000. Deduct all of it and the return shows
₩30,300,000 instead of ₩37,700,000 — a **₩7,400,000** difference.

⚠️ That ₩7.4m is not the tax. The extra tax depends on the progressive rates,
other income, deductions and credits. But it is enough to change the return —
and the income recorded through the tax system is what an institution later
reads on an income certificate, which is one more reason the figure should be
right rather than small.

Every allocation above is an assumption. **The point is that total spending is
not the expense figure.**

## The order

```
identify the transaction
  → establish the business purpose
  → separate personal use
  → check the statutory rules
  → determine the treatment (expense, inventory, asset, prepayment)
  → check the documents
  → confirm the year
  → review VAT separately
  → reconcile the payment
  → retain the records
```

### Five errors

**A receipt makes it deductible.** It evidences a transaction; purpose and
eligibility are separate.

**Personal living costs through a business account.** The account does not
convert them.

**Every inventory or equipment purchase as this year's expense.** Different
period, different method.

**The same recoverable VAT claimed twice.** Once, through the VAT system.

**Owner withdrawals as wages.** A draw is not salary.

All five are the same mistake underneath: **treating a cash movement as
automatically a tax expense.**

Korean deductions are not decided by which card or account paid. Article 27
sets the framework, Article 33 removes specific categories, and the
documentation rules decide what you can show. Legitimate spending is not
disqualified for being cash, on a personal card, or on a Saturday — what
matters is the business connection and the evidence behind it.

**Record what the business bought, why, what treatment applies and how the
amount is substantiated.** A good expense record explains itself without
relying on your memory months later. The aim is not the largest possible
expense total. It is business income that is actually correct — which is what
makes the return defensible and the records useful long after it is filed.
