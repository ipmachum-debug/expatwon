A family arrives with a nine-year-old. They have a lease, passports and the
child's school records from home. The residence card is still in process. Then
somebody asks for the 취학통지서 — the school-entry notice Korean households
receive — and they do not have one.

That sounds like the end of the road. It is the wrong road.

Being able to apply for admission to a Korean school and being a Korean
national subject to compulsory attendance are two different legal questions.
Compulsory elementary and middle education exists, and the duty on a parent to
enroll is written in terms of Korean nationals — which is why a foreign child
is not on the municipal list and no notice arrives.

The decree then separately lists a foreign child among those who may apply for
elementary admission or transfer. The door exists. Nobody opens it for you.

## Elementary school: start where the child lives

For elementary school, the parent or guardian may apply to the principal of a
school within the district covering the child's residence. The rule expressly
includes foreign children.

The school may check immigration-entry or foreign-registration information
through administrative data. Where that cannot be checked, or the parent does
not consent to the electronic check, the rules allow documents instead — and
those documents can be evidence of actual residence, such as a lease.

That is the operationally important sentence. The system is not built on the
assumption that every foreign child already holds a Korean registration
record. If you have just landed, the school or the local education office is
the next call, not the immigration queue.

## Middle school: often a different desk

Middle school runs on a related structure with a different starting point. A
guardian may apply to the relevant superintendent of education for the
district or, depending on the local school-zone structure, to the school
itself. Where the education office handles it, that office designates the
school the student is assigned to.

The same alternative-evidence rule applies when standard immigration or
registration information is unavailable.

So do not assume the nearest middle school can complete every step at the
front desk. For elementary, the school is usually the practical entry point.
For middle school, the district education-support office may have to be in it.

## High school is not an extension of either

Do not carry the elementary rule upward. High school admission is not another
version of residential zoning.

Foreign students enter, transfer into or are placed in Korean high schools
under separate rules and criteria set by the superintendent or by school
regulations, and previous schooling, graduation equivalency, admission timing
and the type of high school can each matter.

For a student approaching that age, confirm the route with the metropolitan or
provincial education office first. Being admissible to elementary school
creates no right to a particular high school.

## What to put in the file

| | What it establishes |
| --- | --- |
| Admission, enrollment or transfer application | The request itself |
| Passport or foreign-national identification | Who the child is |
| Entry/departure certificate or foreign-registration information, where available | Status, where it exists |
| Proof of Korean residence — lease or equivalent | Which district, and the fallback where registration is unavailable |
| Previous enrollment or graduation certificates | That schooling happened |
| Transcripts or records of the level completed | Which grade |
| Korean school records, if the child studied here before | Continuity |
| Vaccination or health records, where requested | Local requirement |

The characteristic mistake is arriving with a passport and expecting age alone
to decide the grade. The school also needs to know what the child has already
completed — and where a document genuinely cannot be obtained, say so
explicitly rather than leaving a gap for someone to interpret as an unanswered
question.

## Two separate systems, kept separate

A school decides education. Immigration decides status. Admission does not
legalise a stay, and a school administrator is not the right person to ask
whether a visa is valid.

This cuts both ways, and the useful half is the one families miss: an
education route can exist while an immigration problem is unresolved. Treating
the harder question as a precondition for the easier one keeps a child out of
school for no reason the rules require.

## Moving house is a school question

Enrollment is tied to residence and school districts, so a move between
districts can create a transfer issue with nothing having changed about the
child's status.

Before signing a new lease with a school-age child, check which elementary
district covers the address, which middle-school group applies, whether a
transfer will be needed and what evidence of the new residence will be wanted.
An agent saying a school is "nearby" is not a statement about district
boundaries — distance and assignment are different things. The rest of what a
move triggers is in
[moving in Korea: the filings and their deadlines](/cost-of-living/moving-in-korea-the-filings-and-their-deadlines/).

## Admission is not the whole budget

Public enrollment removes the large number — international-school tuition —
and it does not take the household education line to zero. Depending on the
school and programme there can still be meals, optional after-school
programmes, field activities, supplies, uniforms at some levels, and whatever
private education the family chooses on top.

Access to a school also does not establish eligibility for every scholarship,
municipal benefit or education-support programme. Each has its own residence,
nationality, income or household rules — the same structure that makes
childcare subsidies for foreign children a separate question from childcare
admission.

So the financial question is not *can my child attend*. It is *can my child
attend, and which benefits and charges attach to this child specifically*.

## The order that works

Confirm the Korean address and have credible residence evidence to hand.
Identify the right authority — the district school for elementary, the
education-support office or school for middle, the education office for high
school. Gather identity and residence documents, then the previous education
records. Ask explicitly how the grade will be determined rather than assuming
Korean age settles it. Then get the decision in concrete terms: which school,
which grade, what else is needed.

A family arriving in October with a ten-year-old, a lease and a transcript but
no residence card yet has everything that route actually requires. The missing
entry notice is not the controlling issue. Residence, the foreign-student
admission procedure and the evidence for placement are.

<p class="hand">Ask which school and which grade. An answer without both is not a decision.</p>
