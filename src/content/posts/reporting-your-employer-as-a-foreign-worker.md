The procedure for recovering unpaid wages in Korea is not complicated. What
stops foreign workers using it is a different question: **what happens to me
while I do?**

That question is legitimate, and until recently the honest answer contained a
real risk. One part of it changed in November 2025.

<p class="hand">The labour case and the status are separate files. Keep them that way.</p>

## What your employer says about your rights is not the law

Article 6 prohibits discriminating in working conditions on grounds of
nationality. A complaint to the labour office is an ordinary employee remedy.

So each of these is pressure rather than a legal position:

- "If a foreigner reports us, the visa gets cancelled."
- "E-9 workers have no right to complain."
- "You cannot report us without the company's agreement."

<div class="callout callout-note">
  <p class="callout-title">Keep them if they arrive in writing</p>
  <p>A message telling you that reporting will cost you your status is evidence
  of the relationship you are describing. It costs nothing to save and it can be
  useful later.</p>
</div>

## The protection that changed in November 2025

The fear with the most substance behind it was that approaching a public body
about your employer would surface your own immigration position.

From November 2025, foreign workers who are **victims of unpaid wages** were
included in the exemption from public officials' duty to notify immigration, and
the Ministry of Justice carries this into 2026 as a rights-protection measure.

<div class="callout callout-warn">
  <p class="callout-title">What it does not do</p>
  <p>It does not extend your period of stay, and it does not make an already
  unlawful stay lawful. It removes one specific mechanism — the report on you
  being triggered by your report on them. Everything else about your status
  still has to be dealt with at immigration, and on its own timetable.</p>
</div>

That is a narrower statement than "you are protected", and it is the one that
holds.

## G-1 is a possibility assessed case by case

You will find sources saying that a labour dispute entitles you to G-1 status
while the case runs. That overstates it.

What exists: the Ministry of Justice allows lawful stay, including G-1, for
foreign nationals who are victims of rights violations and need to remain in
Korea to pursue a remedy — assessed on the individual case.

```
overstated   dispute filed → G-1 as of right
accurate     staying is necessary to pursue the case
               → extension or change of status, G-1 among them,
                 considered on the facts
```

Raise it **before** your current period of stay expires. A case in progress is
not a defence to having overstayed, and asking afterwards is a much worse
position than asking in advance.

The first is the one to use when the immigration question and the labour
question are tangled together, which is the situation this guide is about. The
second is the one to use first if you are on E-9 or H-2 and the employment
centre and the labour office are pulling in different directions.

## On E-9, the complaint is evidence for two purposes

An E-9 worker can only change workplace on a statutory ground — and **unpaid
wages, breach of working conditions and unfair treatment are among them**.

So the labour office record does two jobs. It pursues the money, and it
evidences the ground for
[a workplace change](/tracked/e9-workplace-change-application-period/). It may
also determine whether the move counts against
[the limited number of permitted changes](/tracked/e9-workplace-change-limit/),
since moves for reasons not attributable to the worker may fall outside the
count.

<div class="callout callout-note">
  <p class="callout-title">Which changes when you should file</p>
  <p>Treating the complaint as purely a way to recover wages understates it. For
  an E-9 worker it is also the documentary basis for being allowed to work
  somewhere else — and the workplace change deadlines are short, so the two run
  together rather than one after the other.</p>
</div>

## On E-7, the labour case does not hold your status open

A dismissal that is being challenged is still a dismissal for immigration
purposes. Winning at the Labor Relations Commission months later does not
retrospectively supply lawful stay in the meantime.

So run them separately:

| | Labour track | Immigration track |
|---|---|---|
| Question | Was the dismissal justified; is money owed | May I remain, and on what basis |
| Body | Commission; labour office | Immigration |
| Clock | [3 months](/tracked/unfair-dismissal-relief-deadline/) for dismissal relief | Your remaining period of stay |

<div class="callout callout-warn">
  <p class="callout-title">Do not wait for the Commission</p>
  <p>If your period of stay expires while the case is pending, the case does not
  save it. Establish the immigration plan — a qualifying new employer, a change
  of status, or an extension on the facts of the dispute — on its own timetable.
  The options are in
  <a href="/employment/losing-your-job-on-a-korean-work-visa/">losing your job on a Korean work visa</a>.</p>
</div>

## Unpaid wages can also support a benefit claim

Leaving because of unpaid wages is capable of being a justifiable reason even
where the resignation was formally voluntary, which matters for job-seeking
benefit.

But a labour office finding does not by itself produce the benefit: insured
days, the reason for leaving, ability and willingness to work, and lawful stay
are all assessed —
[unemployment benefit as a foreign worker](/employment/unemployment-benefit-for-foreign-workers-korea/).

## The order that keeps both cases alive

```
preserve the records while you still have access
  → check the remaining period of stay
  → unpaid wages: complaint to the labour office
  → dismissal: 3 months, and check the headcount first
  → E-9: workplace change ground and deadlines, in parallel
  → E-7: new employer or change of status, in parallel
  → 1345 (option 1) or 1577-0071 for language and referral
  → confirmation of arrears → substitute payment or civil recovery
```

The real danger in a foreign worker's labour dispute is not the act of
reporting. It is spending three months on the wage claim and finding the
dismissal remedy expired — or winning the dismissal case with no lawful basis
left to be in the country for the outcome.

The two labour procedures themselves, and which body handles which, are in
[unpaid wages and unfair dismissal: two routes](/employment/unpaid-wages-and-unfair-dismissal-korea/).
