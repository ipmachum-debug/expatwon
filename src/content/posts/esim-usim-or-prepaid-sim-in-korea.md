Compare mobile products on data allowance and you will solve the wrong
problem.

What Korea actually asks of a phone line is not bandwidth. It is whether
the line can answer the question **"is this person who they say they
are"** — because banking apps, card companies, insurers and a good deal of
public administration verify identity through the carrier.

And these two sentences are not the same:

```
I have a Korean number.
I can pass Korean phone identity verification.
```

<p class="hand">The number is not the point. What the number is attached to is.</p>

## The example that settles it

KT's foreigner eSIM can come with a number beginning 010. Its own guidance
also states that **personal identity verification is not possible** on a
tourist SIM.

One product, both facts, published by the carrier. Which is why the rule to
carry around is not "get a Korean number" but:

<div class="callout callout-warn">
  <p class="callout-title">A 010 number is not verification</p>
  <p>Korean phone identity verification does not check that a number exists.
  It matches the subscriber information the carrier holds — name, date of
  birth, registration details — against the identity being claimed. A tourist
  line opened on a passport can receive your SMS codes and still fail that
  match.</p>
</div>

## Before you buy anything: check the handset

An eSIM is a profile downloaded to the phone rather than a card pushed into
it, and not every phone can take one.

<div class="steps">
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">1</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>eSIM support, by model number.</strong> iPhones have supported eSIM for years and Galaxy support is common, but it varies by the market a device was sold in. Check your exact model number rather than reasoning from the generation — "iPhone XS or later" and "Galaxy S20 or later" are not reliable rules.</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">2</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>Carrier lock.</strong> A phone bought on contract abroad may be locked to that carrier, and a Korean SIM or eSIM will not work in it. KT's own foreigner eSIM guidance says the device's country lock has to be released first. Sort this out at home, where your original carrier can do it.</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">3</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>Bands and VoLTE.</strong> Some overseas Android models do not support all Korean LTE and 5G bands, or do not handle voice calls properly on Korean networks. For a long stay, "SIM free" is not the end of the check.</div></div>
</div>

## The three kinds of line, by what they are for

| | Data-focused eSIM | Passport voice/SMS prepaid | Registration-card MNO/MVNO |
|---|---|---|---|
| Data | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| 010 number | Varies by product | Usually yes | Yes |
| Voice calls | Varies by product | Commonly yes | Yes |
| SMS | Varies by product | Commonly yes | Yes |
| Opened with | Online details, passport | Passport | Registration card |
| Phone identity verification | Generally not to be expected | Limited or unavailable | The basis for it |
| PASS | Generally difficult | Not assured | Check by carrier |
| Suits | Travel, the first days | Short stays, waiting for the card | Living here |

**The decisive row is not the 010 number. It is identity verification.** A
visitor may never need it. Anyone who will be banking, insuring, or dealing
with administration in Korea for a year builds their whole practical life
on it.

  <p class="callout-title">Data-only does not always mean no number</p>
  <p>Products differ. Some global eSIMs are pure data with no Korean number at
  all; some Korean carriers' tourist data eSIMs do issue an 010. Read the
  specific product rather than the category — and remember that either way,
  the number's presence tells you nothing about verification.</p>
</div>

## One to four weeks: a data eSIM is usually enough

Maps, translation, messengers, search and ordinary travel apps do not need
a verifiable Korean line. A data eSIM is the simplest thing that works, and
it works from the moment you land.

Choose a voice-and-SMS tourist SIM instead if you expect to phone Korean
businesses, make bookings by phone, or use services that text you.

**And ignore the claim that a data eSIM blocks K-ETA.** K-ETA is not built
on having a Korean number — the application runs on passport details and
email verification. That story circulates constantly and it is wrong.

## One to six months: separate the arrival line from the settled line

A D-4 language student, or anyone else arriving for a long stay, has a gap:
you are here, and the registration card is weeks away.

The workable shape is two lines in sequence:

```
land        →  passport-registered tourist SIM or eSIM
card issued →  ask whether this line can move to your registration details
if not      →  open a new MNO or MVNO line in your own name
```

Some products allow the subscriber details to be switched to your
registration card, keeping the number. **Tourist-specific products often
cannot be converted** — the way they are classed and how long they run gets
in the way — in which case you take a new line and a new number.

<div class="callout callout-warn">
  <p class="callout-title">Be careful where you register the temporary number</p>
  <p>Give the arrival number to your bank, your school and your employer, and
  a later switch means changing it in all of them at once — including the
  bank, where a stale number is exactly what breaks logins and transfers.
  Treat the first number as disposable until you know whether it converts.</p>
</div>

The arrival line is also temporary by design: KT's tourist SIM and eSIM run
to a maximum of **90 days from first activation**. Tracked at
[tourist SIM validity](/tracked/tourist-sim-max-validity/).

## Six months and beyond: the registration-card line is infrastructure

Once you live here, price stops being the first question. These come first:

- Can the line be registered in your own name?
- Does it support phone identity verification?
- Is PASS supported?
- International SMS and roaming?
- Number portability?
- Customer service you can actually use?

SK Telecom lists the **alien registration card** as the document a foreign
national brings to open a line. That registration is what connects your
name, registration number and phone number in the carrier's records — and
that connection is the foundation everything else sits on.

```
Registration card
  → line opened in your own name
  → name, registration number and phone number all match
  → phone identity verification becomes possible
  → PASS and similar services can be set up
```

## By length of stay

| Staying | On arrival | After the card | What you are solving |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1–4 weeks | Data eSIM or tourist SIM | — | Data and calls |
| 1–3 months | Voice/SMS prepaid | Consider if needed | A contactable number |
| 3–6 months | Passport prepaid, then wait | Move to a card-registered line | Preparing for verification |
| 6 months + | Temporary arrival line | MNO or MVNO in your own name | Banking and public services |

Anyone on D-4, D-2 or E-7 should plan **two stages from the start** —
something that connects on day one, then something that verifies once the
card exists.

## Verification usually fails on the name, not the line

When a foreign resident cannot pass phone identity verification, the cause
is often not the SIM. It is that the **name does not match across systems**.

Your passport's romanised name, your registration record, the carrier's
subscriber record and the name your bank holds all have to agree — and
foreign names give this endless opportunities to go wrong: spacing, the
order of given and family names, middle names, hyphens.

So when you open the long-term line, check it is registered **exactly as
your registration card has it**, and use that same form at the bank and the
card company. Matching the phone number is not enough; the identity
information has to match too.

Which document the bank wants, and why the first account is limited, is in

## Before you fly, and after you land

**Before:** release the carrier lock, confirm eSIM support by model number,
check Korean band and VoLTE compatibility.

**After:** a temporary line for data, or voice and SMS if you need it. Then,
for a long stay:

Korea's mobile question splits in two: **can I get online**, and **can the
system confirm who I am**. A visitor only needs the first. Anyone living
here finds that the second is where banking and administration actually
begin.
