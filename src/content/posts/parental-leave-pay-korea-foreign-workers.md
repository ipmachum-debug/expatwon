You have been at a Korean company for almost a year, your child is due, and a
colleague tells you Employment Insurance pays for parental leave. Your manager
confirms the company grants it. Then the Employment Center asks a question
nobody warned you about, and it turns out the months you have been working are
not the months that count.

Korea splits this into two legal questions that share a name in English. The
right to take the leave is one Act; the money during it is another. A foreign
worker can hold the first and fail the second, and the second is where the
residence category does its damage.

## Maternity leave and parental leave are not one thing

출산전후휴가 is leave around childbirth; 육아휴직 is leave to care for a child.
Different Acts, different durations, different benefit rules — and, as below,
the same 180-day condition measured at two different moments.

| | Maternity leave | Parental leave |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Korean | 출산전후휴가 | 육아휴직 |
| Main statute | Labor Standards Act | Equal Employment Opportunity Act |
| Duration | 90 days; 100 for qualifying premature, 120 for multiple | Generally up to a year, extendable on conditions |
| Insured-day test | Generally before the leave **ends** | Before the leave **begins** |

For a single birth at least 45 of the 90 days must fall after the birth; for a
multiple pregnancy, 60 of the 120. None of it is restricted by nationality —
but a person genuinely engaged as an independent contractor may not have the
employee entitlement at all, whatever the contract is called.

## The right to the leave, and the six-month trap

An employer must generally permit parental leave for a child aged eight or
under, or in the second grade of elementary school or below. The normal span
is a year, and a further six months — eighteen in total — is available where
the statutory conditions are met: both parents each taking at least three
months for the same child, certain single-parent cases, qualifying
disability-related cases.

Eighteen months is not the default. It is an extension you have to qualify
for.

And there is an exception that catches new hires: an employer may refuse
ordinary parental leave where you have been continuously employed by that
employer for less than six months as of the day before the leave is to start.

Six months with the company and 180 insured-unit days are two different
conditions, measured two different ways. People satisfy one and assume the
other.

## Which visa you hold changes the answer

This is the part that has no equivalent for a Korean colleague. Employment
Insurance does not reach every residence category identically.

| Residence category | General position |
| --- | --- |
| F-2, F-5, F-6 | Generally mandatory coverage for qualifying employees |
| E-7 and other employment-authorised categories | A voluntary enrolment framework may apply |
| F-4 | Voluntary enrolment framework |
| E-9, H-2 | Some components mandatory; unemployment-related coverage may need voluntary enrolment |
| D-7, D-8, D-9 | Special provisions, including reciprocity conditions |

That table orients you; it does not classify you. A D-8 holder may be an
investor rather than a qualifying employee, F-2 covers several sub-categories,
and in every row the actual employment relationship and the actual enrolment
still decide it.

The distinction that matters is between mandatory coverage, voluntary
enrolment and partial coverage — because only the first happens without
anyone choosing it.

## The gap that ends applications

An E-7 professional works legally, draws a salary, has tax withheld, appears
in health insurance and pension records. None of that establishes Employment
Insurance coverage, because the category can sit in the voluntary framework.

So a worker employed ten months may have three months of qualifying insurance,
and the seven that preceded enrolment do not convert retroactively when the
gap is found. Backdating is restricted.

The same applies to F-4: a long residence card and years of payroll deductions
are not the record. For E-9 and H-2, an employer saying "you're covered"
frequently means the mandatory components — employment stabilisation and
vocational skills development — which are not the coverage this benefit needs.

Ask for the insured-person record itself. The Korea Workers' Compensation and
Welfare Service administers enrolment records, and that document is the
answer; a payslip deduction is not.

## 180 insured-unit days is not six months

The requirement is {{fig:employment-insurance-insured-unit-days}} of
피보험 단위기간 — insured-unit days — counted under the statutory rules from
days forming the basis for remuneration. Unpaid days drop out. Coverage under
some other schemes, such as certain self-employed, artist or labour-provider
arrangements, is not automatically combined with ordinary employee days.

At an illustrative 26 recognised days a month, six months is 26 × 6 = 156 —
short. Seven months is 26 × 7 = 182 — clear. The 26 is an assumption for
arithmetic, not a rule; your payroll decides it.

And the moment of measurement differs by benefit. Parental leave benefits take
the position as the leave begins. Maternity benefits are generally assessed by
the time the leave ends, which means paid portions of the maternity leave
itself can count toward the days where the law so provides. A worker short at
the start of maternity leave may still qualify by its end — and still be short
when a separate parental leave begins afterwards.

## What it actually pays

| Leave period | Rate | Monthly ceiling |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Months 1–3 | 100% of ordinary wage | {{fig:parental-leave-ceiling-first-three-months}} |
| Months 4–6 | 100% of ordinary wage | ₩2,000,000 |
| Month 7 onward | 80% of ordinary wage | {{fig:parental-leave-ceiling-month-seven-on}} |

Take a ₩3,000,000 ordinary wage and twelve months on the standard scheme.
Months 1–3 are capped: 3 × ₩2,500,000 = ₩7,500,000. Months 4–6: 3 ×
₩2,000,000 = ₩6,000,000. From month 7, 80% of ₩3,000,000 is ₩2,400,000 but the
ceiling is lower, so 6 × ₩1,600,000 = ₩9,600,000.

Total: ₩23,100,000.

Twelve months of that salary would have been ₩36,000,000. The difference is
₩12,900,000, before anything else about the household enters it. If the
eighteen-month extension applies, the extra six months sit at the same
₩1,600,000 ceiling — 6 × ₩1,600,000 = ₩9,600,000, for ₩32,700,000 in all.

A ceiling is not a salary replacement, and the back half of the year is paid at
well under the front half.

## The schemes with better numbers

The 부모 함께 육아휴직제 — the 6+6 arrangement — raises the first six months
substantially where both parents take leave for the same child within the
child's first eighteen months, with monthly ceilings climbing across those
months. They need not start on the same day, but both parents' leave and
insurance conditions have to hold, and a spouse's leave taken outside Korea
should not be assumed to satisfy a Korean condition.

Eligible single-parent workers have their own higher ceiling for the first
three months. Living apart from the other parent is not the test; the legal
definition and documents are. Ask the Employment Center which family-status
documents it wants and whether foreign-issued ones need translation or
authentication — that answer takes weeks to act on if you get it late.

## Maternity pay has two payers

The government maternity benefit is capped at
{{fig:maternity-benefit-ceiling-30-days}}, which is ₩6,600,000 across a
standard 90-day leave. Premature and multiple births carry their own totals.

That ceiling does not displace the employer's wage obligation over the
statutorily paid portion — generally the first 60 days, or 75 for multiple
births. On a ₩3,000,000 ordinary wage at a priority-support enterprise: the
state's 90 days come to 3 × ₩2,200,000 = ₩6,600,000, while the employer's
first-60-day wage obligation is 2 × ₩3,000,000 = ₩6,000,000, against
₩4,400,000 of benefit covering the same period. The employer's gap is
₩6,000,000 − ₩4,400,000 = ₩1,600,000.

Larger enterprises divide the funded days differently. The point is that the
ceiling is not the whole of what you are owed, and the gap belongs to the
employer rather than to nobody.

## Applying, in the order that works

Request the leave from the employer first, checking the child's eligibility,
the dates, and your continuous service with that employer. Then confirm
whether your category is mandatory or voluntary, and get the insured-person
record. Then count the insured-unit days against the right moment for the
benefit you want. Only then does the application make sense: the employer
submits its leave confirmation, you apply through Work24 or an Employment
Center.

Benefits may generally be claimed from after the first month of leave, and the
application must be made within twelve months of the leave actually ending,
subject to the statutory exceptions. Claiming periodically keeps the household
cash flow legible; waiting to the end concentrates the risk of missing it.
Maternity benefits run on their own deadline — do not borrow one leave's dates
for the other. A missed deadline is a loss even where the leave was granted
properly.

## What the leave protects, and what it does not

Dismissal or unfavourable treatment because you took parental leave is
prohibited, dismissal during the leave is generally prohibited subject to the
business-discontinuation exception, the employer must restore you to the same
or equivalent-paying work, and the period counts as continuous service. If
your employer's response to the request is hostile rather than administrative,
[what counts as workplace harassment](/employment/workplace-harassment-korea-foreign-workers/)
is the neighbouring question.

What none of it does is extend a residence period, authorise work for another
employer, or settle what happens to a fixed-term contract that expires mid-leave.
Nor does leave suspend your residence obligations if the family spends part of
it abroad — confirm benefit-reporting duties with the Employment Center and
re-entry with immigration on 1345, and report changes in circumstances rather
than hoping they go unnoticed.

<p class="hand">Eligibility comes before calculation. A benefit you do not qualify for has no ceiling to look up.</p>
