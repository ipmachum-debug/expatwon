E-9 and E-7 are usually compared as though the difference were skill and
salary — production work against professional work. That framing survives
about one page of the actual rules.

Both statuses carry the same Korean labour law. What differs is **who controls
the employment relationship**: whether you may choose your workplace, how fast
losing a job becomes an immigration problem, whether your family can be here,
and whether the stay leads anywhere.

## Two different statutes, not two tiers of the same one

E-9 non-professional employment runs through the **Employment Permit System**
under the Act on the Employment of Foreign Workers: government-permitted
sectors, a domestic recruitment step first, per-workplace quotas, and a
state-managed process that connects worker to employer.

E-7 specific activities is an **Immigration Act** status permitting employment
in designated occupations, and it subdivides:

| Sub-type | |
|---|---|
| E-7-1 | Professional |
| E-7-2 | Semi-professional |
| E-7-3 | General skilled |
| E-7-4 | Skilled technical |

So "I am on E-7" does not identify a single set of education, career, wage or
employer requirements. The sub-type and the occupation code do.

## E-9 does not mean lower labour rights

There is no separate minimum wage for E-9. Where the Labor Standards Act and
the Minimum Wage Act apply, they apply identically: minimum wage, weekly
holiday pay, overtime, night and holiday premiums, annual leave, retirement
benefits.

The Employment Permit System adds a **standard employment contract** stating
the contract term, workplace, duties, working hours, breaks, wages, payment
date and board and lodging costs.

<div class="callout callout-warn">
  <p class="callout-title">"E-9 is the cheap visa" is not a legal statement</p>
  <p>It describes a labour market, not a rule. Nothing in the status permits a
  lower wage, a longer week or unpaid overtime, and an employer treating it that
  way is in breach of the same provisions that protect everyone else — set out in
  <a href="/employment/your-korean-employment-contract-what-to-check/">your Korean employment contract</a>.</p>
</div>

## The E-7 wage requirement is no longer a GNI calculation

This is the correction most likely to be sitting in a document someone is
relying on right now.

E-7 wage requirements were long explained as a percentage of the previous
year's per-capita GNI. For 2026 the Ministry of Justice announces
[amounts directly](/tracked/e7-annual-wage-requirement/):

| Sub-type | 2026 basic requirement |
|---|---:|
| E-7-1 professional | ₩31,120,000 |
| E-7-2 semi-professional | ₩25,890,000 |
| E-7-3 general skilled | ₩25,890,000 |
| E-7-4 skilled technical | ₩26,000,000 |

Applicable **1 February to 31 December 2026**. Where an occupation-specific
notice sets its own requirement — and some of those are still keyed to a share
of GNI — that requirement governs.

```
E-7 sub-type
  → occupation code
  → the year's announced amount
  → any occupation-specific requirement
```

## Mobility is the real dividing line

**E-9.** A change of workplace requires a statutory ground: the employer
terminating or refusing to renew, suspension or closure of business, breach of
working conditions, unpaid wages, unfair treatment. A better offer is not one.

There is also a count —
[three changes, plus two in the extension](/tracked/e9-workplace-change-limit/).

<div class="callout callout-note">
  <p class="callout-title">"Five moves in a lifetime" gets both halves wrong</p>
  <p>The allowance is split between the base period and the extension, and moves
  forced by circumstances outside the worker's control — closure, breach,
  unpaid wages, unfair treatment — may not count at all. So the reason is
  examined before the tally, and a worker who thinks they are out of moves may
  not be.</p>
</div>

**E-7.** No Employment Permit System change limit. But not free movement
either: under Article 21 of the Immigration Act, changing or adding a workplace
generally requires **prior permission**, with a report **within 15 days after
the change** available to certain holders of professional knowledge, technology
or skills.

<div class="callout callout-warn">
  <p class="callout-title">Two different 15-day rules, and they are not the same rule</p>
  <p>The one here is the E-7 workplace-change report, made by the worker after
  moving. The other — covered in
  <a href="/employment/losing-your-job-on-a-korean-work-visa/">losing your job on a Korean work visa</a> —
  is the employer's duty to notify immigration within 15 days when employment
  ends. Same number, different obligation, different person. Conflating them is
  how people believe they have a 15-day deadline that is actually somebody
  else's.</p>
</div>

The practical E-7 test is not "may I leave" but **"does the new employer meet
the conditions"** — the occupation, the employer requirements, the foreign
employment ratio, the wage requirement, the duties, and your own education and
career record.

## On E-9, the clock after a job ends is short

Where an E-9 contract ends, the worker must apply for a workplace change
through the employment centre within
[one month](/tracked/e9-workplace-change-application-period/), and must obtain
permission for a new workplace within three months of applying — failing which
departure is required. Statutory exceptions including occupational injury,
illness and pregnancy or childbirth can change the counting.

```
E-9   contract ends → 1 month to apply → 3 months to secure a workplace → leave
E-7   employment ends → change of status or a new qualifying employer
```

That is the structural difference, expressed in time. The same event that gives
an E-7 holder a status question gives an E-9 worker a departure deadline.

## Duration: 4 years 10 months is not a lifetime cap

E-9 runs an initial employment activity period plus an extension, commonly
[4 years and 10 months](/tracked/e9-max-continuous-employment-period/) of
continuous work.

But a long-serving worker meeting the conditions can use the **re-entry special
case**: depart, and after a short period outside Korea — currently one month —
return on E-9 and work again.

E-7 has no equivalent fixed ceiling. It renews as long as the employment and
the occupation's requirements continue to be met.

<div class="callout callout-warn">
  <p class="callout-title">Which is not the same as unlimited</p>
  <p>Each extension can re-examine the contract, the wage, the employer, the
  duties and the actual pattern of residence. If the basis for the specific
  activity has gone, the extension can be refused. "E-7 means you can stay
  indefinitely" describes a possibility, not a guarantee.</p>
</div>

## Family is where the two statuses stop resembling each other

F-3 dependent status covers the spouse and minor children of certain long-stay
statuses from D-1 through E-7. **E-9 is not among them.**

So an E-9 worker completing nearly five years in Korea does not thereby acquire
a route to bring a spouse and children. An E-7 holder does — though F-3 is a
dependent status rather than a work permit, and a family member wanting to work
needs their own basis for it.

## Side by side

| | E-9 | E-7 |
|---|---|---|
| Framework | Employment Permit System | Immigration Act, specific activities |
| Scope | Government-permitted non-professional sectors | Professional, semi-professional, skilled, technical occupations |
| Labour law | Identical | Identical |
| Wage | Minimum wage | Minimum wage plus the E-7 requirement for the sub-type |
| Contract | EPS standard contract | Individual contract |
| Changing workplace | Statutory grounds only | New employer must meet E-7 conditions |
| Change count | 3, plus 2 in the extension | No EPS-style limit |
| After a job ends | 1 month to apply, 3 months to secure | Change of status or a qualifying employer |
| Duration | Commonly 4y10m, re-entry special case available | Renewable while conditions hold |
| Family (F-3) | Not available | Spouse and minor children |
| Onward route | Via E-7-4 | F-2 and F-5 pathways exist |

## For employers, this is not a cost menu

A company cannot simply pick whichever status is cheaper for a given hire.

E-9 requires permitted sectors, the domestic recruitment step, employment
permission and per-workplace quotas. E-7 requires the occupation code, the
worker's education and career, the wage requirement, the foreign employment
ratio, employer scale and genuine fit between the person and the duties.

Different gates, and the applicant either passes one or does not.

## What it comes down to

Not what you earn. Whether you can leave, how long you have when the job ends,
whether your family is with you, and whether the years accumulate into
something.

The route by which an E-9 worker moves onto the second list — E-7-4 skilled
technical status, which is rationed and conditional — is the other half of
today's pair.
