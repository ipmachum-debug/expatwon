You meant to send your landlord ₩3,200,000. You checked the account number,
confirmed the amount, and the app told you the transfer succeeded. Then you
looked at the recipient name and it belonged to someone you have never met.

You reopen the transfer screen looking for a cancel button. There is none.

This is a mistaken transfer — 착오송금 — and the thing to understand first is
that it is not one problem with one fix. There is a procedure for money that
went to the wrong person by error, a different procedure for money you were
tricked into sending, and a third for money that left without you touching the
app at all. They are not interchangeable, and starting in the wrong one is how
a recoverable transfer turns into a lost one.

## The first ten minutes

Do not start by hunting for the recipient on social media. Start with the bank
that sent the money, through its official app or customer-service channel, and
say that you need to file a 착오송금 반환 요청 — a request to return a mistaken
transfer.

Before you leave the app, record the sending bank and account, the receiving
bank and account number, the recipient name as displayed, the amount, the date
and exact time, and the transaction reference. A screenshot helps you, but a
transaction confirmation issued by the bank is what a formal application wants
later. Keep the whole record rather than a crop showing the amount.

If your Korean is not up to the call, ask whether the bank can assist in a
foreign language or at a branch. What you must not do, at any point in this,
is give your banking password, one-time password or security-card numbers to
someone who offers to recover the money for you.

## Why your bank cannot simply take it back

The intuition is that a bank that moved the money can move it back. But a
mistaken transfer is a conflict between two account holders. You say the
payment was unintended; the recipient's account has already received it. The
bank cannot pull funds out of someone else's account without a legal basis for
doing so.

So the first stage is not enforcement, it is a request. The bank contacts the
receiving institution, the recipient is asked to return the money, and if they
agree, it comes back. That is the fastest outcome available, and it is also
the cheapest one — a voluntary return through the bank's own procedure does
not carry the recovery costs attached to the later route.

That makes the first attempt worth doing properly rather than treating it as a
formality on the way to somewhere else.

## When the recipient will not return it

If the bank reports that its return procedure did not succeed, the money is
still gone and a second route opens: the Korea Deposit Insurance Corporation's
mistaken-transfer return support programme, 착오송금 반환지원제도.

What KDIC does is acquire the claim associated with the mistaken payment and
pursue it — asking the recipient to repay, and where appropriate using a court
payment-order procedure. It exists because the alternative for an ordinary
person is a civil claim against a stranger whose name they do not know.

It is a recovery mechanism, not a reimbursement scheme. The application has to
qualify, and the recovery has to actually succeed.

## What qualifies

| | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Amount of the mistaken transfer | ₩50,000 up to {{fig:kdic-mistaken-transfer-max}} per transaction |
| Deadline to apply | Within {{fig:kdic-mistaken-transfer-window}} of the transfer |
| Before applying | A return request through the sending bank that did not succeed |
| Disqualifiers to check | Related legal proceedings, the receiving account's status, and other programme exclusions |

Two details inside that table cause most of the wasted applications.

The ceiling applies per transaction, and the programme restricts applications
covering only part of a single claim — a transfer above the limit cannot be
split into qualifying pieces. And the deadline runs from the transfer itself,
not from the day the bank told you its attempt had failed. The preliminary
procedure you are required to complete first takes time you do not control, so
if the anniversary is approaching, ask the bank and KDIC urgently how to
preserve your ability to apply rather than waiting politely.

If you read elsewhere that the ceiling is ₩10,000,000 or ₩50,000,000, you are
reading an older stage of the programme. English-language summaries of this
one are routinely years behind.

## The amount you get back is a third number

What you sent, what is recovered, and what reaches you are three figures, and
only the first is known at the start.

KDIC's route carries recovery-related expenses, deducted from what is
collected. Take a ₩3,200,000 mistaken transfer that is recovered in full, with
₩60,000 of eligible expenses: ₩3,200,000 − ₩60,000 = ₩3,140,000. That ₩60,000
is an illustration of the mechanism, not a published fee — actual expenses
depend on the case.

Recovery is also not always complete. If ₩2,000,000 of that same transfer is
all that can be collected, the deduction applies to what was collected, and
the gap against the original ₩3.2 million is simply a loss. Nothing in the
programme converts an uncollectable claim into cash.

There is no promised processing time either. A cooperative recipient is quick;
an uncontactable one is not, and a payment-order procedure is slower still. If
the money was meant for rent or tuition, tell the person expecting it now and
arrange payment another way. A claim is not a balance.

## If it was fraud, this is the wrong page

| What happened | Where to go |
| --- | --- |
| You mistyped an account number | The sending bank's mistaken-transfer return request |
| An ordinary mistaken recipient refuses to return it | KDIC eligibility check, after the bank's attempt fails |
| You were deceived into paying someone | Your bank's urgent fraud channel, and 112 |
| Money left your account without you | Report unauthorised access to the bank immediately |
| The receiving account is frozen or legally restricted | Ask the bank and KDIC which procedure applies |

The distinction is not bureaucratic. Fraud response is time-critical in a way
that eligibility screening is not, and waiting for a support-programme answer
while a scammer empties an account is the expensive mistake here.

## The person demanding a fee is not the solution

If someone contacts you saying the money can be released once you pay a
processing charge to a private account, treat that as a second attempt on your
money and verify it with the bank or KDIC on a number you looked up yourself.

And if you are on the other side of this — money you did not expect has landed
in your account — do not spend it and do not forward it to anyone who asks.
Contact your own bank and let the proper procedure handle it. Informal
transfers arranged between strangers outside the bank's process are exactly
what both the mistaken sender and the innocent recipient have to avoid.

## Payment apps and transfers out of Korea

Not every payment moves the same way. Some services transfer through linked
bank accounts; others run on prepaid electronic payment balances, and a
balance transfer inside an app is not automatically the same thing as a
bank-to-bank transfer for the purposes of this programme.

Start with the provider you actually sent the money through, and ask whether
the transaction falls inside the current support scope and how to file its
preliminary return request. For money already sent into a foreign banking
system, ask the remittance provider about its own recall and dispute process
instead — that is a different mechanism with different deadlines.

*I entered the wrong account number and sent money to another person. I would
like to file a mistaken-transfer return request. I have the transaction
confirmation, and I would like the return procedure and my case reference.*

If the bank tells you the preliminary procedure failed, ask for that result in
writing. It is the document the next stage is built on.

## The five expensive mistakes

Waiting instead of reporting, while the transaction record is easiest to
obtain. Going to KDIC without the bank's failed attempt, which is the
eligibility condition rather than a suggestion. Relying on an outdated ceiling
and concluding you do not qualify. Assuming an accepted application means the
full amount returns. And treating a fraud as a transfer error, which sends an
urgent problem into a slow queue.

None of those is about the money being unrecoverable. They are all about
starting in the wrong place.
