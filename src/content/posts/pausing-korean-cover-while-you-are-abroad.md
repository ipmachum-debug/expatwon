  - question: 'I am posted overseas but my family stays in Korea. Do I still pay?'

Long absences from Korea create a reasonable question: why pay for cover you are
not using?

There are answers, and they come from three different schemes with numbers close
enough to be confused. None of them starts by itself.

<p class="hand">Nothing here happens because you boarded a plane.</p>

## The national scheme: three months, with a condition

The general period of overseas stay for premium exemption is
[three months](/tracked/nhis-overseas-premium-exemption-period/).

And for a workplace subscriber it carries a condition that decides most real
cases: exemption applies where there is **no dependant residing in Korea**.

<div class="callout callout-warn">
  <p class="callout-title">Which is exactly the posted-abroad situation</p>
  <p>An employee sent overseas while their spouse and children stay in Korea and
  continue using the scheme is not producing a zero premium by being on another
  continent. Summaries that give the three months without the condition give the
  wrong answer to the most common version of the question.</p>
</div>

## One month is an exception, not a shorter rule

A shortened period of
[one month](/tracked/nhis-overseas-work-exception-period/) applies where the
insurance service **recognises** the stay as being for work.

```
"one month abroad → premiums stop"      ✗
"one month, where recognised as work-related"   ✓
```

Recognition is the step. A month-long business trip is not self-executing, and
the dependants condition does not disappear because the shorter period applies.

## Premium exemption is not the same as status

Suspension of benefits and exemption from premiums are connected but distinct.
Re-entering Korea reconnects both, and there are edge cases — returning in a
month, receiving benefits and departing again can create an exception for that
month's premium.

So for a long posting, work from the actual **departure and return dates** and
check the position for each, rather than assuming a clean on-off switch.

## Private indemnity cover: also three months, and unrelated

Korean private indemnity policies have their own arrangement:
[three months of continuous overseas stay](/tracked/private-loss-overseas-suspension-period/)
allows premiums to be suspended, or reclaimed afterwards.

Two routes:

<div class="steps">
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">1</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>Apply to suspend before leaving</strong>, so premiums stop for the period.</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">2</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>Claim a refund after returning</strong>, evidencing the stay — an immigration record certificate typically.</div></div>
</div>

<div class="callout callout-warn">
  <p class="callout-title">Not automatic, and not the same rule as the public one</p>
  <p>Three months abroad does not stop a private premium by itself; the insurer
  applies it on request under your policy's terms. The number matching the public
  scheme's exemption period is a coincidence of drafting, not a shared
  mechanism — different bodies, different conditions, different evidence.</p>
</div>

## Suspending is not free

Suspension reduces the premium and limits the cover for the same period. Before
doing it, confirm what actually protects you while you are away:

- a travel policy, and what its medical component covers
- a global or expat policy, and its area of cover
- an employer's group scheme, and whether it follows you

<div class="callout callout-warn">
  <p class="callout-title">And do not cancel where you could suspend</p>
  <p>For anyone with a medical history this is the expensive move. Coming back
  means fresh underwriting, and a condition that developed while you were away may
  not be insurable at all. The premium saved is visible; the insurability given up
  is not, which is why the trade reads better than it is. Which generation your
  policy belongs to matters here too, since terms differ sharply between them —
  see <a href="/insurance/korean-private-health-insurance-4th-vs-5th-generation/">4th vs 5th generation</a>.</p>
</div>

## The other duplication: individual and group cover

Separately from going abroad, many people hold a personal indemnity policy while
an employer provides group indemnity cover.

Since 2023 the arrangements for suspending one side of that duplication have been
broadened, so the overlap can be reduced without giving up cover entirely.

<div class="callout callout-note">
  <p class="callout-title">Worth checking on joining a Korean employer</p>
  <p>Indemnity cover cannot pay more than the costs actually incurred, so a
  personal policy running alongside a group one is a strong candidate for
  suspension. Foreign employees frequently do not know group indemnity cover is in
  the benefits package at all — it is worth asking, before paying for both for a
  year.</p>
</div>

## Which lever applies to which situation

| Situation | What to check |
|---|---|
| Business trip, under a month | Generally nothing suspends |
| Work-related stay of a month or more | NHIS shortened period, subject to recognition and dependants |
| Personal stay of three months or more | NHIS general period, subject to dependants |
| Long overseas posting | NHIS exemption, plus private suspension or refund |
| Employer group cover overlapping a personal policy | Suspension of one side |

## And on the way back

Re-entry reconnects the national scheme, so check the position for the month of
return specifically. Restart the private policy or complete the refund claim, and
re-check any group cover if you have changed employer.

Which cover overlaps with which in the first place, and the distinction between
the half that is shared and the half that genuinely stacks, is in
[four insurances, one bill: what actually overlaps](/insurance/four-insurances-one-bill-what-overlaps/).

The three periods above look interchangeable and are not. One is public law with
a family condition attached, one is an exception inside it that turns on
recognition, and one is a private policy arrangement you have to ask for. Reading
any of them as "leave the country and the bills stop" produces the same outcome
in every case: a premium still due, and nobody having applied for anything.
