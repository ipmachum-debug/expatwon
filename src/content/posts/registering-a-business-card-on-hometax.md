The point of a business credit card is not the card. It is that the
National Tax Service can collect its transaction data directly from the
card company, so that hundreds of purchases arrive at VAT time as filing
data rather than as a box of receipts to key in.

**How the general and simplified VAT regimes themselves work is a
separate question** —
[Korean VAT for small business owners](/business/korean-vat-for-small-business-owners/)
covers that. This guide is only the card data: how it is collected, and
how deductible and non-deductible are decided.

## Where it is, and what it will not take

In Hometax:

```
Log in → Invoices · Receipts · Cards
       → Credit card purchases
       → Business credit card registration and enquiry
```

An individual business can register up to **50 cards**, and the field on
the screen says exactly which: credit cards and check cards in the name
of the representative or the company. They do not have to be cards opened
specifically for the business — an ordinary personal credit card in the
owner's name can be registered.

A mobile number is required alongside the card numbers. It is not
optional politeness: it is where the SMS goes if a card number is
mistyped, and clearing all the card numbers clears the phone number with
them.

What the scheme will not take:

<div class="check-card">
  <div class="check-row miss"><span class="mark"></span><span><strong>Family cards</strong> (가족카드) — refused</span></div>
  <div class="check-row miss"><span class="mark"></span><span><strong>Gift cards</strong> (기프트카드) and <strong>unregistered rechargeable prepaid cards</strong> (충전식선불카드) — refused. A rechargeable prepaid card converted to a registered name is accepted, but only where it is a gift card.</span></div>
  <div class="check-row miss"><span class="mark"></span><span><strong>직불카드</strong> — refused. This is the older direct-debit product, <em>not</em> a check card. English guides that say "no debit cards" get this wrong.</span></div>
  <div class="check-row miss"><span class="mark"></span><span><strong>Department store cards</strong> (백화점전용카드) — refused</span></div>
  <div class="check-row miss"><span class="mark"></span><span><strong>An employee's personal card</strong> — cannot be registered against your business registration number</span></div>
  <div class="check-result">Registering a spouse's or a staff member's card against one business number is not a supported structure. Where staff need to spend, that is a corporate card or a reimbursement process.</div>
</div>

A card registered this month can be checked for an identity match from
around the 10th of next month, and usage history has been available
monthly for registrations from October 2019 onward. So if a new card is
going to carry business spending, register it **before** you start using
it, not after the first statement.

One card type to keep out deliberately: fuel usage on a freight driver
welfare card is queried through its own menu, and the screen asks you not
to register it here.

<div class="callout callout-tip">
  <p class="callout-title">Get the number right the first time</p>
  <p>The registration list shows each card with a request date, a
  confirmation date and a processing status — and while that status reads
  <strong>확인요청중</strong> (confirmation requested), the entry cannot be
  deleted. A mistyped card number is not a quick fix; it is a wait. Check the
  digits before submitting rather than after.</p>
</div>

## Individual and corporate cards are collected differently

| | Individual business | Company |
|---|---|---|
| Card is in the name of | The business owner | The company |
| Separate Hometax registration | Required | Not normally required |
| How many | Up to 50 credit cards in the owner's name | Company-name cards handled automatically |
| Family cards | Excluded | Different structure |
| Seeing the transactions | In Hometax after registering | Collected as corporate card data |
| Who decides deductibility | The business owner | The company, on the substance |

A card issued in a company's name is already tied to the company, so
there is nothing to register.

**Which is not the same as "paid on the corporate card, therefore
deductible and expensed."** A director's personal purchases, a family
meal, a private trip — the name on the card does not make any of that a
business cost. Corporate cards automate collection. They do not decide
business relatedness.

## The tax office sorts first. You decide last

Registered card data goes from the card company to the National Tax
Service, which analyses it against the counterparty's registration
status and industry classification. Hometax then shows each transaction
in one of three states:

<div class="steps">
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">1</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>Deductible</strong> — an ordinary taxable purchase from a general taxpayer with no statutory bar.</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">2</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>Optionally non-deductible</strong> — industries where business and private use plausibly coexist: restaurants, accommodation, fuel, vehicle upkeep, entertainment venues. Pre-sorted as non-deductible, and you can confirm or change it where the spending genuinely was for the taxable business.</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">3</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>Inherently non-deductible</strong> — barred by statute. Paying for it on a registered business card does not move it.</div></div>
</div>

The confirmation screen for input tax deduction is where you settle
those, and settling them is your job, not the system's. Data that shows
up marked deductible is not a ruling. A meal with no business purpose
does not become deductible under the Value-Added Tax Act because a
classification engine put it in the wrong bucket.

## What never deducts, whatever card paid for it

| Transaction | VAT treatment |
|---|---|
| Ordinary purchases used directly in the taxable business | Deductible where the conditions are met |
| Personal spending unrelated to the business | Not deductible |
| Entertainment and similar consumption-type spending | Not deductible |
| Buying, leasing or running a non-commercial small passenger car | Not deductible as a rule |
| Purchases used in a VAT-exempt business | Not deductible |
| Buying from a VAT-exempt business | No input tax to deduct |
| Purchases from a simplified taxpayer | Pre-sorted as inherently non-deductible |
| Shared across taxable and exempt activities | Apportionment required |

Two of those cost people real money:

**Entertainment.** A meal with a client, a round of golf — genuinely
connected to the business in an ordinary sense, and still treated by the
Value-Added Tax Act as its own non-deductible category. The VAT inside
entertainment spending does not deduct the way VAT on materials does.

**Non-commercial small passenger cars.** Using a car for work does not
make it deductible. Purchase, lease and running costs including fuel and
servicing are, as a rule, outside input tax deduction. Where the vehicle
itself is the business — car dealing, vehicle rental — the analysis is
different, and that is a narrow exception rather than a general escape.

Costs relating to a VAT-exempt business cannot be set against the output
tax of a taxable one either. Where a cost genuinely serves both, common
input tax has to be apportioned.

## VAT deduction and expense deduction are two different tests

<div class="callout callout-warn">
  <p class="callout-title">Non-deductible for VAT does not mean disallowed as an expense</p>
  <p>These are separate statutes answering separate questions. VAT input tax
  not being deductible does not automatically mean the whole payment is
  refused as a necessary expense or a deductible cost for income or corporate
  tax. And the reverse trap is more common: a transaction arriving
  automatically in Hometax is not thereby an allowable expense under the
  Income Tax Act or the Corporate Tax Act.</p>
</div>

Run them as an ordered sequence rather than one decision:

```
Card payment
  → is it business related?
  → is the VAT input tax deductible?
  → is the amount an allowable expense for income or corporate tax?
```

## An unregistered card is not a void card

Not registering does not remove the expense from your business. What you
lose is automatic collection, which means evidencing each transaction
separately — the exact work the scheme exists to remove.

From 2026, validation on the individual electronic VAT return has been
tightened: **with no registration history, amounts cannot simply be
entered into the business card field of the return.** Continuing on
unregistered cards is not an efficient way to manage tax records.

But the claim that non-registration itself attracts penalty tax is not
accurate. The real exposure sits elsewhere: deducting input tax that
never qualified, entering the same transaction twice from different card
statements, or putting private spending through as deductible. The
National Tax Service can assess the input tax back and apply penalty tax
on those.

## Do not meet the classification for the first time in filing week

The whole point is to remove the receipt-gathering sprint before the
deadline. Meeting several hundred pre-classified transactions for the
first time on the day you file recreates it.

Check the Hometax record monthly or quarterly, and look hardest at:

  <div class="check-row miss"><span class="mark"></span><span>Restaurants and accommodation, where business and private use mix</span></div>
  <div class="check-row miss"><span class="mark"></span><span>Fuel and vehicle servicing</span></div>
  <div class="check-row miss"><span class="mark"></span><span>Overseas payments</span></div>
  <div class="check-row miss"><span class="mark"></span><span>Cancellations and refunds</span></div>
  <div class="check-row miss"><span class="mark"></span><span>Purchases from VAT-exempt businesses</span></div>
  <div class="check-row miss"><span class="mark"></span><span>Costs shared across taxable and exempt activities</span></div>
  <div class="check-row miss"><span class="mark"></span><span>Large equipment and fixed asset purchases</span></div>
  <div class="check-result">Pre-classification runs both ways. Deductible business spending can be sorted as non-deductible, and spending with nothing to do with the business can be sorted as deductible.</div>
</div>

## The setup that stays clean

Separate the account, the card and the evidence at the same time, right
after registering the business:

<div class="steps">
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">1</div>
    <div class="step-body">A dedicated business bank account</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">2</div>
    <div class="step-body">A credit card in your own name used only for business spending</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">3</div>
    <div class="step-body">That card registered on Hometax, before it is used</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">4</div>
    <div class="step-body">Monthly review of the collected transactions</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">5</div>
    <div class="step-body">Deductible and non-deductible confirmed as you go, not at the deadline</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">6</div>
    <div class="step-body">The VAT return filed from data you have already checked</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">7</div>
    <div class="step-body">The same records carried into the income tax expense position</div></div>
</div>

A company should build the same control around a corporate account and
corporate cards. Repeatedly paying company costs on the director's
personal card and reimbursing afterwards makes the evidence trail and
the cash flow harder to follow than they need to be.

The value of registering a business card is not that it deducts more
tax. It is that it stops you missing deductions you were entitled to,
strips out the ones you were never entitled to before you file them, and
turns several hundred card payments into something you can actually file
from.
