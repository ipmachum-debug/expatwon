Leaving Korea permanently is not a flight booking and an empty apartment. It is
the termination of a vehicle registration, a lease, a business, an employment
relationship, four insurance schemes, several tax obligations, a bank
relationship and a residence status — each on its own terms.

One rule governs the order: **do not destroy the channel your money arrives
through while money is still owed to you.**

## 1. D-90 to D-60: dispose of the things that stay behind

A car is the first item because it is the one that can remain attached to you
after you have gone.

Handing over the keys is not a transfer of ownership. Under the Motor Vehicle
Management Act the transferee must complete the transfer registration, and where
the sale goes through a dealer there is a structure in which the dealer handles
that registration on the buyer's behalf. If it is never completed, taxes,
administrative fines and attachments keep attaching to the registered owner —
you.

Taking the car out of the country is a different procedure again: not a domestic
transfer but **deregistration** and export. Check the register first for
attachments, security interests or unpaid vehicle tax that would block an export
deregistration, and keep the shipping and export documents aligned with the
registration steps. Transfer registration and deregistration are separate
procedures under the Act, and the detail is in
[selling or exporting your car when leaving Korea](/cars/selling-or-exporting-your-car-when-leaving-korea/).

The car is usually in daily use until the final week. The contract and the
document review are not — those start here.

<div class="callout callout-warn">
  <p class="callout-title">"Two or three months' notice and you can leave" is not a rule</p>
  <p>Two different provisions apply and neither is a general notice period.
  Before expiry, a refusal to renew must reach the landlord by
  <a href="/tracked/lease-nonrenewal-notice-deadline/">two months before the expiry date</a>,
  or the lease can be tacitly renewed. Once it has already been tacitly renewed,
  you may terminate at any time but it takes effect
  <a href="/tracked/tacit-renewal-termination-effective-period/">three months after the landlord receives the notice</a>.
  D-90 is not a recommended lead time — it is the point at which you take out the
  contract and determine which of those you are in, or whether you need an agreed
  early termination instead.</p>
</div>

At the same time, confirm with the landlord when the deposit will actually be
returned, and decide now which account it lands in.

If you run a business, work backwards from the closing date: the last day of
trading, the closure filing, collection of receivables, disposal of stock and the
employees' leaving dates. A final payroll is not the settlement — retirement
benefits, unused annual leave pay, withholding tax and loss of insured status all
belong to the same wind-up, and the sequencing is in
[closing a business in Korea](/business/closing-a-business-in-korea-final-vat-and-stock/).

## 2. D-30 to D-14: pension eligibility and who files for you afterwards

A departing foreign national's national pension contributions do not come back
automatically.

Entitlement rests on **article 126 of the National Pension Act** read with the
social security agreements and reciprocity framework — not article 102, a
citation that appears in many departure checklists and points at the wrong rule.
Three routes exist: the home country recognising a corresponding benefit for
Koreans, an agreement covering lump-sum refunds, or a qualifying residence
status.

So the first question is nationality and status, not paperwork. Which countries
qualify by which route is
[tracked here](/tracked/nps-lump-sum-refund-country-list/).

Those who do qualify can apply before departure. The pension service asks for
proof of the intended departure date such as a ticket, plus passport, alien
registration card and account details — and airport payment at Incheon generally
requires departure
[within one month of applying](/tracked/nps-airport-refund-lead-time/). It also
asks claimants to nominate a domestic or overseas bank account, precisely because
airport payment may not work out.

Which is the first concrete reason not to close the accounts before you fly.

<div class="callout callout-warn">
  <p class="callout-title">A tax manager is a filing, not a hire</p>
  <p>Article 82 of the Framework Act on National Taxes requires a taxpayer with
  no address or place of residence in Korea, or moving abroad, to appoint a
  <strong>tax manager</strong> for national tax matters — and the appointment is
  reported to the head of the competent tax office. Instructing an accountant to
  handle your filings is a private engagement. Whether the statutory appointment
  has actually been reported is a separate question, and it is worth asking in
  those words before you leave.</p>
</div>

If domestic business, employment, rental or investment income remains, or a VAT,
global income tax, corporate tax or capital gains filing may arise after you go,
settle both the appointment and the method of getting documents to your
representative while you are still here.

Health insurance changes with resignation, business closure or departure, so
confirm the final premium and the loss of insured status — and where dependants
are attached to your cover, end those relationships too. The conditions are in
[NHIS dependants](/insurance/nhis-dependants-the-income-and-property-tests/).

## 3. D-7 to departure: collect first, then close

The final week is cash flow.

Take final readings for electricity, gas, water and management fees. Return
internet, TV and any rented equipment. If you have been paying the long-term
repair reserve through the management bill, check with the landlord whether it is
returnable to you — [it is the owner's cost](/cost-of-living/moving-out-in-korea-meters-fees-and-refunds/).

The deposit needs a firm return date **before** you hand over the property.
Moving out and departing while repayment is outstanding makes opposing power and
priority repayment much harder to manage, so where it is late, the leasehold
registration order comes first — and it protects on completion, not on
application. That sequence is in
[ending a Korean lease](/cost-of-living/ending-a-korean-lease-the-notice-that-has-to-arrive/).

Close the banking in three stages, not one:

<div class="steps">
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">1</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>Stop the outflows</strong> — card bills, telecoms, utilities and rental auto-debits.</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">2</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>Keep one receiving account</strong> for the deposit, final salary, tax refunds and any pension lump sum.</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">3</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>Close only after everything has landed</strong> — remit abroad, then cancel the cards and the account.</div></div>
</div>

If you intend to keep a Korean account from abroad, ask the bank in advance about
the registered mobile number, what identity verification works from overseas, and
whether the account converts to non-resident status or becomes transaction
limited. Discovering that after the phone line is cancelled is the common way
this fails.

## 4. On the day: the ARC, and only on a permanent exit

Article 37 of the Immigration Act requires a registered foreign national to
return the alien registration card to an immigration officer on departure.

The exceptions matter as much as the rule. Departing with re-entry permission, or
intending to re-enter within the permitted period of stay under a multiple-entry
visa or a re-entry permit exemption, are treated differently.

<div class="callout callout-note">
  <p class="callout-title">Permanent exit and temporary exit are different events</p>
  <p>Surrendering the card on a trip you are coming back from creates a problem
  rather than closing one. Establish which kind of departure this is before you
  reach the desk — and note that the card is also what your bank, your landlord
  and the pension service have been identifying you by.</p>
</div>

## 5. After the flight: Korea's tax year does not end at the gate

A closed sole proprietorship still has its own VAT filing deadline to meet. Where
domestic global income arose during the year, a filing obligation can remain —
and if you left permanently, that one is due
[the day before departure](/tracked/departure-income-tax-filing-deadline/). A
company's corporate tax continues on its own fiscal clock and on whether it is
being liquidated.

Money also keeps moving in the other direction: post-employment payments, a
deposit settlement difference, the pension payment, tax refunds. All of which is
why the financial connection has to outlive the departure by months, not days.
The tax-side detail is in
[leaving Korea: tax, pension and what you are owed](/business/leaving-korea-tax-pension-and-refunds/).

## The closing sequence

| When | Action | How you know it is done |
|---|---|---|
| D-90 – D-60 | Sell or export the vehicle | Transfer or deregistration completed |
| D-90 – D-60 | Determine and give lease notice | Expiry or tacit renewal identified |
| D-60 – D-30 | Business and employee settlement | Closing date, leaving dates, receivables planned |
| D-30 – D-14 | Pension lump sum | Route, eligibility and receiving account confirmed |
| D-30 – D-14 | Appoint a tax manager | Reported to the tax office, documents handed over |
| D-14 – D-7 | Health and social insurance | Loss of status processed, premiums settled |
| D-7 – departure | Utilities, telecoms, deposit | Balances at zero, deposit received |
| Departure day | Return the ARC | Only on a permanent exit |
| After departure | Final filings and refunds | Nothing outstanding either way |
| Last of all | Close the Korean account | Every payment received and remitted |

The most expensive order is ARC, then account, then deposit, pension and tax.

Build it the other way round: end the assets and contracts, keep the channel the
money arrives through and someone able to file for you, finish the departure
admin, and only then close the financial relationship. The first year in Korea is
about connecting identity, address, banking and tax in that order — and
[the arrival timeline](/cost-of-living/your-first-year-in-korea-the-complete-timeline/)
runs the same logic forwards. Leaving is that sequence in reverse, with one
difference: on the way out, the money is owed to you.
