Ask about changing jobs in Korea on a work visa and someone will eventually
mention a release letter — a document from the previous employer confirming
they have let you go. Then the sentence that follows is usually wrong: that
without it, you cannot move.

The document is real. The veto is not.

<p class="hand">Change the question and the refusal stops being final.</p>

## Why the document is asked for at all

When a workplace change is assessed, part of what has to be established is that
the previous employment relationship genuinely ended, and on what basis.

A letter from the previous employer — a release, a termination confirmation, a
certificate of employment, a resignation acknowledgement — settles that quickly
and in one page. That convenience is why it is requested so consistently, and
why it gets mistaken for a requirement in its own right.

<div class="callout callout-note">
  <p class="callout-title">The distinction that matters</p>
  <p>A consent requirement would mean your former employer holds a right to
  decide. An evidentiary requirement means a fact has to be shown. Facts can be
  shown more than one way — which is the entire difference between "I am stuck"
  and "I need another document".</p>
</div>

## What else evidences the same fact

Where the letter is refused, work the fact rather than the form:

<div class="steps">
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">1</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>The employment contract</strong> — its term, and any fixed end date.</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">2</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>Written notice of dismissal or non-renewal.</strong> Dismissal must be given in writing with the grounds and the date, so if you were dismissed there should be a document.</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">3</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>Resignation correspondence</strong>, including the acknowledgement or anything showing it was requested by the company.</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">4</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>Final pay and severance records</strong> — a final settlement is hard to explain if the relationship continued.</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">5</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>Loss of insured status</strong> on employment insurance and health insurance, with the date.</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">6</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>Labour office records</strong>, where unpaid wages or a breach of conditions was involved.</div></div>
</div>

You can also request a **certificate of employment** — the Labor Standards Act
entitles an employee to one covering matters such as period of employment and
position, on request, and an employer's willingness to issue a routine
certificate is often different from its willingness to sign a letter framed as
permission.

## Your former employer has its own filing to make

Independently of anything you ask for, an employer must report to immigration
within
[15 days](/tracked/foreign-worker-employment-change-report-deadline/) when
employment with a foreign national ends. Missing it exposes them to an
administrative fine of up to
[₩2,000,000](/tracked/employer-employment-change-report-penalty/).

<div class="callout callout-warn">
  <p class="callout-title">Two useful consequences</p>
  <p>First, it confirms whose the 15 days actually are — the employer's, not a
  countdown imposed on you. Second, a company refusing a courtesy letter may
  still complete a filing it is legally required to make, so the fact that you
  left can reach immigration without the document you were denied.</p>
</div>

## Where closure or non-payment is the reason

If the company shut down, stopped paying, or breached the contract, the release
letter was never going to arrive — and that is precisely the case the evidence
route is for.

Business closure records, unpaid wage claims, a labour office complaint,
insured status ending abruptly across a workforce: these establish both that
the employment ended and why, without the cooperation of the party responsible.

## On E-9 the reason does a second job

For a professional status, the reason mainly establishes that the relationship
ended. On E-9 it also decides whether a change is **permitted at all** — the
grounds are limited to statutory ones — and whether the move counts against
[the change allowance](/tracked/e9-workplace-change-limit/).

<div class="callout callout-warn">
  <p class="callout-title">Which is why an E-9 worker should assemble it early</p>
  <p>A departure recorded as voluntary can both fail the grounds test and
  consume one of a small number of permitted moves. A departure evidenced as
  closure, unpaid wages, breach of working conditions or unfair treatment may
  do neither. The difference is documentary, and it is far easier to gather
  while still employed than afterwards.</p>
</div>

## What not to do

<div class="steps">
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">1</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>Do not start work while negotiating for the letter.</strong> The missing document is a paperwork problem; working without permission is an immigration offence — see <a href="/employment/changing-employers-on-a-korean-work-visa/">changing employers on a Korean work visa</a>.</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">2</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>Do not sign something inaccurate to obtain it.</strong> A statement that you resigned voluntarily, given to unlock a letter, can undo a job-seeking benefit claim and, on E-9, a workplace change.</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">3</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>Do not accept a payment condition.</strong> Any demand to repay training costs or a sum for the release is a contract question with its own limits, and a predetermined penalty for leaving is prohibited outright.</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">4</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>Do not let it stall the clock.</strong> On E-9 especially, the application and permission deadlines run whether or not the letter has arrived.</div></div>
</div>

The prohibition on penalties fixed in advance for breach of contract, and what
a training-cost clause can and cannot do, is in
[your Korean employment contract](/employment/your-korean-employment-contract-what-to-check/).

## If you are already in the argument

<div class="steps">
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">1</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>Ask in writing</strong>, naming the document and the facts it needs to confirm. A refusal in writing is itself evidence.</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">2</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>Assemble the alternatives</strong> from the list above, with dates.</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">3</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>Ask immigration what it will accept</strong> — <strong>1345</strong>, in several languages — rather than assuming the letter is mandatory.</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">4</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>Where unpaid wages or a breach is involved</strong>, raise it with the labour office on <strong>1350</strong>. That produces a record, which is what you were short of.</div></div>
</div>

A former employer who will not sign has made your next step harder. Whether
they have made it impossible depends on what else you can show — and the answer
is usually more than people expect, because the fact you need to prove is one
that leaves traces in payroll, in insurance records, and in the employer's own
obligation to report it.
