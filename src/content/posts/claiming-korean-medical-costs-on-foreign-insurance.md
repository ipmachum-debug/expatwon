The difference between a claim that pays and a claim that does not is rarely
the policy. It is what you walked out of the hospital holding — and whether you
asked for one particular document while the doctor was still in the room.

## Two ways the money can move

<div class="steps">
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">1</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>Pay and claim.</strong> You settle the hospital in full and seek reimbursement from your insurer afterwards. The default where nothing has been arranged.</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">2</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>Direct billing.</strong> Your insurer issues a guarantee of payment and the hospital settles with the insurer or its assistance company. Requires an existing arrangement.</div></div>
</div>

Severance's international centre describes direct billing as available where it
has an arrangement with the insurer or organisation, and notes that without one
the patient may have to pay themselves. Seoul National University Hospital lists
arrangements with insurers including Cigna International, Allianz, Aetna and
United Healthcare International.

<div class="callout callout-warn">
  <p class="callout-title">Establish which one applies before the appointment</p>
  <p>Discovering at the payment desk that direct billing is not available turns a
  scheduled treatment into an unplanned large payment. One call or email to the
  international centre, naming your insurer, settles it.</p>
</div>

## A policy is not a promise of full reimbursement

Being insured and being reimbursed in full are different states, and the gap
between them is written into the contract:

- deductible and co-payment
- benefit limits
- exclusions for pre-existing conditions
- conditions specific to emergency treatment
- pre-authorisation requirements
- restrictions to particular hospitals or networks

<div class="callout callout-note">
  <p class="callout-title">Which is why the goal is fewer rejections, not full payment</p>
  <p>You cannot document your way past a deductible or a benefit limit. What
  good documentation does is remove the reasons a claim gets refused or reduced
  that <em>are</em> within your control — an unclear diagnosis, a bill with no
  breakdown, no proof of payment, a missing pre-authorisation. Aim at that, and
  read the policy for the rest.</p>
</div>

For anything expensive, check coverage first, and whether a guarantee of payment
or pre-authorisation is required. Insurers frequently want a cost estimate from
the hospital before issuing one — which the international centres can provide.

## The three documents

| Document | Establishes |
|---|---|
| Medical certificate | What condition or injury was treated |
| Itemised bill | Which tests, procedures and medicines, at what price |
| Official receipt | What you actually paid |

Seoul National University Hospital's international centre states that outpatient
and inpatient receipts and itemised bills can be requested through its billing
records.

Get all three before leaving. From another country, each one becomes an email
thread in a language you may not write, against a hospital that has no
particular reason to prioritise it.

## The English certificate is a request you make in the room

Hospitals differ in how they issue English medical documents, and the procedure
is not always the one people assume.

At Seoul National University Hospital, the English medical certificate is
requested from the treating doctor during the consultation rather than written
up afterwards by the international centre, and it carries a fee of
[₩20,000](/tracked/english-medical-certificate-fee/).

So the useful moment is while you are still with the doctor. Say what the
insurer needs:

<div class="steps">
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">1</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>An English medical certificate</strong>, explicitly.</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">2</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>The diagnosis</strong>, in the form your insurer asks for.</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">3</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>The treatment period</strong>, where the policy turns on dates.</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">4</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>Medical necessity</strong>, where the insurer requires it stated.</div></div>
</div>

Other hospitals set their own fees and procedures; the ₩20,000 is one named
institution's, not a national price.

## Diagnosis codes: ask your insurer, not the internet

Some insurers require a diagnosis code, some do not. "The certificate must carry
ICD-10 and a hospital seal or the claim will be rejected" circulates widely and
is not a rule.

The reliable method runs the other way:

```
download the insurer's claim checklist
  → show it to the hospital before the consultation
  → the certificate is written to the spec that will be assessed
```

That takes ten minutes and removes the most common cause of a document being
returned.

## The pharmacy is a second claim

Korean outpatient care generally separates prescribing from dispensing: the
doctor issues a prescription and you buy the medicine at a pharmacy outside the
hospital. Seoul National University Hospital notes that outpatient prescriptions
are normally not included in the hospital's outpatient charge.

```
hospital claim   consultation, tests, procedures
pharmacy claim   the medicine
                 ── two receipts, often two submissions ──
```

Without Korean insurance there is no benefit contribution to the prescription
either, so the pharmacy cost is yours in full. What that comes to depends on the
medicine, the number of days prescribed and what is dispensed — there is no
ratio against an insured patient's cost worth quoting.

<div class="callout callout-warn">
  <p class="callout-title">Keep more than the card slip</p>
  <p>Ask the pharmacy for its official receipt and, where available, an itemised
  breakdown. The medicine bag usually shows the drug names, dosage instructions
  and number of days — keep it with the prescription. Where an insurer wants the
  drug details in English, ask what the pharmacy can issue and submit the
  prescription and drug names alongside.</p>
</div>

## Over-the-counter medicine is a different question

Some medicines — cold remedies, painkillers, digestive aids — are bought without
a prescription at the listed price. Prescription-only medicines require a
doctor's prescription and are dispensed against it.

Foreign policies often exclude over-the-counter purchases, or require a
prescription before they will pay for medicine at all. So do not file something
you bought on your own initiative in the same mental category as a dispensed
prescription.

## What the international centre saves you

Navigating booking, consultation, tests, payment and insurance paperwork
separately, in Korean, is five problems. An international centre consolidates
them, provides interpretation — Severance offers English, Japanese, Russian and
Chinese as standard, with other languages arranged on request — and may bill your
insurer directly.

Compare on the total, not on the consultation fee alone.

abroad are slow to re-issue.

Being uninsured in Korea is not primarily a treatment problem. It is a
documentation problem — and the documents are cheap on the day and expensive
from another continent.
