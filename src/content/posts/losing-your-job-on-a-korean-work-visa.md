The Employment Insurance Act asks whether you are able and willing to work.
The Immigration Act asks whether you may. For a foreign worker who has just
lost a job in Korea, the second question is usually the urgent one, and it is
the one surrounded by the most confidently repeated misinformation.

## The 15 days belong to your employer

Where an employment relationship with a foreign national ends, the **employer**
must notify immigration within
[15 days](/tracked/foreign-worker-employment-change-report-deadline/).

That is the whole rule. It is not a grace period granted to you, and it is not
a deadline for changing your status.

<div class="callout callout-warn">
  <p class="callout-title">Reading it as your deadline causes both errors at once</p>
  <p>People rush a change of status they may not need on that timetable, and
  people who miss "day 15" conclude they are already out of options and stop
  acting. Your obligations exist, but they are decided by your current status,
  your remaining period of stay, and whether you have a new contract — not by
  your former employer's filing.</p>
</div>

Track the two separately. Your employer's notification is their compliance
item; your next status is yours.

## What actually happens to a status tied to a job

An E-7 was granted for a specified activity at a specified place. So:

- the period on your card does not disappear the moment the job ends
- and having a remaining period is not permission to stay on indefinitely doing
  something else

Both halves are true simultaneously, which is why the confident versions in
either direction are wrong. You have some time. You do not have unlimited time,
and the time is for arranging what comes next.

<div class="steps">
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">1</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>A new employer.</strong> Depending on the status type, this needs a workplace change permit or a report — which is a real procedure with conditions, not a formality.</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">2</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>No employer yet.</strong> Then the question is whether you can change to a status that permits looking for one, D-10 being the usual candidate.</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">3</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>Neither available.</strong> Establish that before your remaining period runs down, not after.</div></div>
</div>

## D-10 is a status you qualify for

D-10 exists for job-seeking and permitted internships with Korean companies and
institutions. It is not an automatic consequence of an E-7 ending.

Eligibility runs on education, career history, a Korean degree and points
criteria depending on the track, and a change of status has to be permitted.
Where it is granted, the current framework runs to
[up to one year per grant and three years in total](/tracked/d10-max-stay-period/)
— a figure that moved as recently as October 2025, which is a reason to check
it rather than trust a remembered number.

<div class="callout callout-note">
  <p class="callout-title">Do the eligibility check before your last working day</p>
  <p>If you qualify, you have a plan. If you do not, you have just discovered it
  while you still have time to do something else — a new employer, a different
  status, or a considered departure. Discovering it afterwards costs you exactly
  the period you would have used.</p>
</div>

The sequence that works is: **E-7 ending → check D-10 eligibility → apply for
the change → then job-search.** Not: leave, wait, and apply when the search
stalls.

## D-10 does not create insured periods

Changing status does not generate employment insurance history. D-10 is an
immigration permission; job-seeking benefit is an insurance benefit. Neither
produces the other.

What D-10 can do is provide the lawful basis on which you continue to look for
work in Korea — which is one of the things the employment centre needs to be
true. So where you were properly covered during the E-7 employment and meet the
insured days, reason-for-leaving and job-search conditions, holding D-10 supports
the claim rather than creating it.

<div class="callout callout-warn">
  <p class="callout-title">And the benefit clock is already running</p>
  <p>Job-seeking benefit is generally payable within
  <a href="/tracked/unemployment-benefit-claim-period/">12 months of the day after
  you leave</a> — which includes every week spent on the status change. That is
  the reason to run the immigration process and the employment centre in
  parallel rather than in sequence.</p>
</div>

## Not everyone carries the same risk

| Status | What losing the job does |
|---|---|
| F-5 permanent residence | Stay is not tied to a particular employer; the benefit question is close to a Korean national's |
| Certain F-2 and F-6 | Similar in structure, subject to the specific sub-type |
| E-1 to E-7 | Two problems at once — the insurance claim and the status |
| E-9, H-2 | A different regime again (below) |

Two people can file the same job-seeking benefit claim and be carrying entirely
different immigration exposure while they wait for it.

## E-9 and H-2 are not on this map

The Employment Permit System has its own structure for changing workplace,
employment permits, permitted sectors and periods, under the Act on the
Employment of Foreign Workers.

<div class="callout callout-warn">
  <p class="callout-title">Do not apply the professional roadmap to EPS work</p>
  <p>"Leave, move to D-10, look for a professional job" is an E-7 pathway. For
  E-9 and H-2 the workplace change rules, the permit requirements and the sector
  restrictions are decided elsewhere, and being covered by employment insurance
  does not loosen any of them. Two statutes, two answers, and the insurance one
  does not govern the immigration one.</p>
</div>

The related exit question for EPS workers — departure guarantee insurance and
the gap between it and statutory severance — is in
[departure guarantee insurance for E-9 and H-2 workers](/employment/departure-guarantee-insurance-for-eps-workers/).

## The order to work in

```
current status, and remaining period of stay
  → is there a new employer?
      yes → workplace change permit or report
      no  → check eligibility for D-10 or another status
  → file the change of status
  → in parallel: insured status history, insured days, separation report
  → job-seeking registration and the employment centre
```

The employer's 15-day notification sits outside this list, because it is not
your task.

For status questions the Immigration Contact Center is **1345** and operates in
several languages; for the insurance side the Ministry of Employment and Labor's
line is **1350**. Two numbers, because they are two systems — and calling only
one of them is how people end up with a valid claim they cannot lawfully pursue,
or a lawful stay with no claim left in the window.
