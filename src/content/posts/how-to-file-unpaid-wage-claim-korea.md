Your complaint was accepted. The inspector has the file. Somewhere in the
paperwork there is a number — 14 days, or 25 days — and it is tempting to read
it as the date the money arrives.

Neither one is that date. Three clocks run on an unpaid wage claim and they
measure three different things:

This guide is about that third stage. For how to file the complaint, what

Routes](/employment/unpaid-wages-and-unfair-dismissal-korea/). For what a

Worker](/employment/reporting-your-employer-as-a-foreign-worker/). Those two
answer the question most people arrive with. This one answers the question
they arrive at a few weeks later.

## Clock one: 14 days is the employer's deadline, and only after you leave

Labor Standards Act **art. 36** says an employer must settle wages and other
money within **14 days** of the cause for payment arising — that is, after
someone retires, leaves or dies. The parties may extend it by agreement where
special circumstances exist. Agreement: not an employer's announcement.

The regular monthly payday is a different duty, under **art. 43**. This matters
in both directions, and people get it wrong both ways:

| If you are | The clock that applies | The mistake to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Still employed, payday missed | Art. 43 — the agreed regular payday | Waiting 14 days for a deadline that does not apply to you |
| Leaving or already left | Art. 36 — 14 days from departure | Reading day 15 as the date the state starts paying |
| Left with an extension agreed | The agreed date, if the agreement is real | Treating a unilateral "we'll pay next month" as an extension |

So fix your own date first. For a current employee it is the agreed payday and
the pay period. For someone who has left it is the final working day, the
evidence of how employment ended, any genuine written agreement to a different
date, and a separate line for each unpaid period.

**Art. 37** adds statutory late-payment interest for specified categories.
Note what the article does and does not contain: it describes a delegated
rate, not a fixed percentage written into the provision. Do not quote a figure
for it without checking the currently applicable decree rate and its
exclusions.

## Clock two: 25 days is how long the office works, not how long you wait for money

The Labour Portal's complaint directory lists the wage-arrears petition —
진정서 (임금체불) — with a **25-day** administrative processing period.

<div class="callout callout-warn">
  <p class="callout-title">What the 25 days covers</p>
  <p>It is the period the office allots to handling the petition. It is not a commitment that the employer will have transferred the money by then, and it is not a cap on the case. Further inquiries, corrections, contested facts and any enforcement that follows all sit outside the figure.</p>
</div>

The useful thing to do with the number is to treat it as the point at which you
should know *something* — whether the arrears are acknowledged, disputed, or
still being examined — rather than as the point at which you are paid. Save the
petition number, the submission date and the exact version of the statement you
filed, so that what you said in week one can be compared with what the employer
says in week four.

## Clock three: there is no clock, because collection is a separate event

This is the part that surprises people. A verified debt and a received payment
are two different records, and every intermediate step establishes less than it
appears to.

| Stage | What it establishes | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Complaint accepted | The matter is before the office | That the employer is liable |
| Documents examined | The evidence is being assessed | That any money has been collected |
| Amount acknowledged or verified | A supported figure for the arrears | That the employer can pay it |
| Direction to pay, or an agreement | A route toward settlement | That a transfer has happened |
| Funds received | Payment of that amount | That separate claims are waived |

Keep all three records — the amount you claim, the amount officially found, and
the amount that actually arrived. They diverge more often than they match, and
reconciling them at the end is how you know whether the case is finished.

## Who actually owes the money may not be whose sign is on the door

Collection depends on identifying the right legal entity, and a shop name is
not an employer. Workers are hired through staffing companies, subcontractors
and affiliated entities; the party giving daily instructions may differ from the
party on the wage records.

Check the entity named in the employment contract, the payroll statement and any
employment-insurance documents. Where those three disagree, **preserve the
disagreement and describe it in the filing** rather than picking the one that
looks most solvent. The law does attach additional responsibilities in some
contracting chains, but that is for the inspector to apply — it should not be
inferred from the relationship alone, and confidently naming a parent company or
a client as the debtor before the relationship is established tends to slow a
case rather than speed it.

## The state's backstop has a ceiling, and the ceiling has a shape

Where the employer does not pay confirmed arrears, the simplified substitute
payment (간이대지급금) under the Wage Claim Guarantee Act can step in. The
scheme's existence is covered in the [two-routes
guide](/employment/unpaid-wages-and-unfair-dismissal-korea/); what matters here
is how much of a debt it can actually absorb.

| Claimant | Ceiling | The catch |
|---|---|---|
| Former employee | {{fig:substitute-payment-cap-former-employee}} combined | Component limits of ₩7m for covered wage arrears and ₩7m for covered retirement benefits — they cannot be added to make ₩14m |
| Still employed | {{fig:substitute-payment-cap-current-employee}} for wage arrears | A separate door with extra eligibility restrictions, including a wage test set against the minimum wage |

<p class="hand">A worker owed ₩12 million cannot infer that the state will pay ₩10 million of it. The ceiling caps what the scheme may pay; it does not describe what any particular claim qualifies for.</p>

Amounts depend on which categories and periods are covered, on confirmation of
the arrears, on the employer's qualifying history, on the filing route and on
the time limits. The portal distinguishes a route built on a **court
determination** from one built on a **labour office confirmation**, and the
windows are not the same. For the labour office route a former employee must as
a rule have started the petition within [one year of the day after
leaving](/tracked/substitute-payment-claim-period/).

One more thing worth saying plainly: when the state pays, it may pursue the
employer for that money. That is the state's claim against your employer, not a
second entitlement for you on top of what you received.

## Insolvency is a different door with the same name

If the employer is failing, the distinction between confirming a debt and
collecting it becomes the whole problem. Korea has a separate
insolvency-related route — 도산대지급금 — with its own eligibility tests and
its own ceilings.

<div class="callout callout-note">
  <p class="callout-title">Two schemes, one word</p>
  <p>간이대지급금 and 도산대지급금 are both 대지급금. They are not interchangeable. Establish which one your employer's actual legal status puts you in before counting on either ceiling.</p>
</div>

A company in severe cash-flow trouble is not the same as a company in formal
rehabilitation or bankruptcy, and the dates matter. Claims can be treated
differently inside insolvency proceedings. Do not assume a missed salary is
erased by insolvency, and do not assume it is paid ahead of everything else
regardless of procedure. Ask the labour office or the Korea Workers'
Compensation and Welfare Service which route applies and which documents
establish the employer's status — and ask early, because these windows are
time-sensitive.

The right to a debt and the logistics of receiving it are two different
problems, and departure separates them hard.

## Reconcile at the end, not at the acknowledgment

The case is not closed when the employer admits the debt, when the office issues
a finding, or when a payment is promised. It is closed when you have compared
what arrived against what was owed and know what, if anything, is still
outstanding.

A partial payment is a good sign and nothing more. Update the balance, keep the
bank record, and be explicit about which items it settled — a receipt for part
of the wages does not resolve the rest by implication, and it does not waive
claims you have not identified.

<p class="hand">Scope: this guide covers the gap between a wage claim being accepted and the money arriving. It is general information about the procedure, not advice on your claim, and it does not determine your employment status, the amount owed, or your immigration position.</p>
