**The money in your bank account is not necessarily the income a lender will recognize.**

A software consultant receives ₩70 million in annual client payments. A salaried engineer receives ₩55 million from a Korean employer. A company director controls a business with ₩500 million in revenue but draws a relatively small salary. The consultant might be the highest earner after expenses; the director might have the strongest company balance sheet. Yet, for an ordinary personal loan, the engineer could be the easiest applicant to underwrite.

The difference is not whether the other income exists. It is whether the bank can document the **applicant's own recurring income**, understand what remains after legitimate costs and obligations, and apply its current product rules consistently. Korean lenders do not necessarily use the same definition of income as employers, accountants, immigration officers or business owners.

For foreigners, the evidentiary problem may be amplified by recent arrival, different names across accounts, income from overseas clients or the short time between starting a business and applying for credit. The solution begins by identifying what each piece of paper actually proves.

## An evidence ladder, not a single magic certificate

Banks can request different combinations of documents according to borrower type, product and verification channel. These records answer different questions.

| Document | What it mainly demonstrates | What it does not independently prove |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Certificate of employment (재직증명서) | Current employment relationship | Total taxable income or uninterrupted deposits |
| Employment contract (근로계약서) | Agreed position and compensation | That promised salary has already been earned |
| Earned-income withholding receipt (근로소득 원천징수영수증) | Employer-reported taxable salary for a period | Future employment or automatic acceptance of every allowance |
| NTS income amount certificate (소득금액증명) | Tax-authority record of reported/assessed income | Current-month cash flow when the tax year lags |
| Payslips (급여명세서) | Payroll calculation for specific months | Annual earnings independently verified by tax authority |
| Salary-account transactions | Money received on specific dates | Legal character or taxable amount of every deposit |
| Business registration certificate (사업자등록증) | Registered business identity | Profit or personal spending capacity |
| Business tax/financial records | Declared activity, income, expenses and obligations | A guarantee that the bank recognizes all stated profit |

The strongest files are not necessarily the thickest. They are the files where identity, period, employer, deposits and tax records tell a coherent story.

## Salaried employees: consistency is the main asset

For an E-7 or other working resident employed by a Korean company, the usual starting point is straightforward: passport and residence document for identity, employment certificate for current relationship, recent payslips or salary-account records, and annual tax evidence.

A monthly deposit from an employer helps verify that a paycheck was actually paid. The employment certificate shows the individual is still employed. The withholding statement reflects tax reporting for the relevant period. A National Tax Service document provides an independent income record. Banks can use these in different combinations rather than treating one as an all-purpose replacement for another.

Suppose an employee receives a raise from ₩45 million to ₩60 million annualized in June. The most recent full tax year may still show ₩45 million. If the bank uses that completed year, a letter stating “current salary ₩60 million” might not change the limit immediately. If the product allows consideration of current payroll, recent pay evidence may matter. Ask which basis the lender uses before arguing about the correct figure.

Allowances, bonuses, overtime and irregular compensation require care. A payslip showing an exceptional performance bonus does not prove it will recur every year. The bank may use an average, exclude some elements, require history or apply a conservative figure. There is no responsible way to quote a universal bonus-recognition percentage without a named product's rules.

## The year mismatch that catches new employees

Korean tax documentation often follows a reporting calendar. A person who starts work late in the year may have a strong current contract but a limited prior-year income certificate. That is an evidentiary timing problem, not necessarily proof of low earning capacity.

The applicant should prepare a clear timeline: starting date, prior employment if any, current contracted salary, monthly deposits, withholding document period and the income year reflected in the NTS record. If you changed employers, disclose the change instead of combining documents without explanation.

Some lenders may accept a different method for new employees, using current payroll or estimated annual earnings; others may require minimum tenure. Product policy determines the answer. A foreigner with good prospective earnings should not claim that a future salary has already been tax-certified.

It is particularly important to use the correct Korean term. **소득금액증명** is an income-amount certificate. **원천징수영수증** is a withholding receipt. **재직증명서** is an employment certificate. An applicant who asks for the wrong document can spend days collecting papers that answer the bank's wrong question.

## Freelancers: revenue must survive the expense test

Freelancers often experience the largest gap between money received and income accepted. Invoices may show ₩100 million of billings. But the amount deposited can include taxes collected, reimbursements or business funds, and taxable business income can be much lower after allowable costs.

Banks may look at income reported through the National Tax Service, business tenure, the nature of recurring contracts and the stability of receipts. Each product can have its own treatment of business income and acceptable tax years. A newly established consulting business with several strong months may be less predictable under those rules than a smaller operation with years of consistent filed income.

Foreign clients create additional complexity. A payment arriving by international transfer proves receipt, but it does not automatically establish how the bank should classify or recognize the amount. Contracts, invoice history, currency conversion records and Korean tax reporting may be relevant. The applicant should work with a qualified tax professional when cross-border reporting is involved, rather than invent a domestic salary narrative.

A practical method is to prepare three figures: **gross billings, tax-declared income and cash available for debt service**. These may be different. Explain why. The lender decides which one its rules recognize.

## Sole proprietors: a business registration is only the first page

A Korean sole proprietor can present a valid business registration certificate and still fail an income test. The certificate confirms registration; it does not state annual taxable business profit or how much cash remains after suppliers, taxes and operating expenses.

Depending on the loan, banks may request business tenure, NTS income certificates, tax filing records, sales evidence and business-account transactions. VAT sales documents can demonstrate turnover, but turnover is not the same as profit. A shop selling ₩300 million annually while paying ₩280 million in expenses cannot be analyzed as if the owner personally earns ₩300 million.

For a seasonal business, one impressive month cannot stand in for a full year. Describe seasonality with records rather than asking the reviewer to assume every month will repeat peak sales. If the business is newly operating, some facilities may be unavailable until a minimum period of documented earnings exists.

This is also where keeping a separate business account matters. The more personal and business funds are mixed, the harder it becomes to show recurring business performance and the individual's reliable repayment capacity.

## Company directors: company turnover is not private income

A corporate chief executive might oversee substantial sales and cash balances. For a **personal** mortgage or unsecured loan, that turnover belongs to the corporation, not automatically to the executive. The lender may recognize the individual's documented salary, taxable dividends or other acceptable personal income. It may ask for additional evidence when the executive controls the payer company.

A company with ₩1 billion in annual revenue can pay its director ₩30 million in salary. Unless the lender has a separately approved income-assessment method, ₩1 billion is not the director's annual personal income. Nor does an irregular shareholder loan become employment earnings because the transaction appears in the director's bank account.

For a **corporate** loan, the analysis changes: company financial statements, cash flow, debt, business tenure, tax compliance, security and guarantees become central. Confusing corporate underwriting with personal borrowing is a serious mistake. If an executive needs personal housing finance, resolve the personal income file first. Do not move money artificially to create a misleading pattern.

This distinction is especially relevant to foreign founders. Immigration permission to manage a company, corporate registration and a company's revenue do not automatically establish an individual's ability to obtain retail credit.

## Overseas salary: genuine does not mean recognized

Some applicants work for an employer abroad while living in Korea, or receive consulting payments from another country. The funds can be legitimate and fully reported under the appropriate tax rules. But a Korean retail lender may still restrict recognized income to documents and categories its verification process can accept.

Documents issued abroad may require translations, authentication, or evidence of their tax treatment. None of those steps forces a bank to count the overseas income. The bank can decline to use it even if the applicant provides convincing proof of receipt.

Ask about overseas-income recognition *before* commissioning expensive document translations. If the product cannot use that category of income, more paperwork will not alter its eligibility. In some cases a different lender or secured structure may be available; compare conditions rather than assuming a universal path.

## Why bank statements are supporting evidence, not the whole file

A recurring deposit is persuasive when it aligns with an employer and the tax records. The same deposit is ambiguous when it could be a loan from a friend, reimbursement, transfer between personal accounts or sale of an asset. Lending staff are expected to assess repayment capacity, not simply total account inflows.

For a newly arrived foreigner, bank statements may initially be the easiest documents to obtain. Use them to establish history, but organize the source of funds clearly. Label salary, client receipts, owner transfers and personal remittances accurately. Avoid circular transfers intended to inflate apparent income.

A good loan file allows a third party to reproduce the key figure without trusting the applicant's personal description. That is why independent NTS records carry a different evidentiary weight from an unsourced spreadsheet.

## What to do when the documents disagree

A discrepancy is not automatically misconduct. It may reflect timing, tax adjustments, net versus gross pay, allowances or a changed employer. The right response is a short reconciliation explaining period, source and reason.

For example: “The 2025 income certificate covers January–December at my former salary. My increase began July 2026. Attached are the revised contract, July–September payslips and corresponding salary deposits.” This does not guarantee acceptance of the higher figure; it simply tells the truth in a way the reviewer can verify.

Correct genuine mistakes with the original issuer. Ask the employer to reissue an erroneous certificate; follow NTS procedures for errors in filed tax records; use proper name-change evidence for identity differences. Altering a PDF yourself can turn an ordinary documentation problem into a serious credibility issue.

## Build the package for the product, not for the internet

Before submitting, ask the lender: which income categories are accepted, which tax year governs, whether current payroll can supplement past-year records, how variable pay is treated, and whether overseas income or proprietor income is eligible. Ask whether there is a minimum employment or business-history period.

Then prepare a concise index. Page one should say who the applicant is, what type of earnings they receive, the figure reported to tax authorities, the figure claimed for current earnings, and where each proof appears. Do not include unrelated client data, sensitive employee files or unnecessary corporate bank details. Evidence should be sufficient and proportionate.

For a mortgage, request the bank's actual recognized income calculation rather than a loose “looks fine.” The recognized number determines how much debt service the lender can attribute to you under applicable rules. See [DSR, LTV & Stress DSR](/loans/dsr-ltv-korea-lending-rules-explained/) for the affordability side of the decision.

## Bottom line

Banks are not asking how much money passed through your hands. They are asking how much **reliable, personally attributable and verifiable income** can support repayment under the chosen product. Salaries, freelance earnings, business revenue, shareholder distributions and foreign remittances must not be collapsed into one figure.

Make the records agree where they should, explain legitimate differences and ask the lender which figure it is authorized to use. Better evidence can change the quality of a loan application. It cannot make an excluded income source automatically eligible.
