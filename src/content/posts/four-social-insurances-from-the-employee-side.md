The gap between the salary in your contract and the money in your account is
mostly four insurances and two taxes. On the 2026 rates the insurance side is
about **9.7%** of pay — but that number is where the explanation starts, not
where it ends.

The four schemes are not one deduction split four ways. They insure different
risks, they are governed by different statutes, and — the part that breaks
most attempts to check a payslip — **they do not share a calculation base.**

<p class="hand">They overlap heavily. They are not identical, and the difference is where checks fail.</p>

## The 2026 rates

| Scheme | Total | Employee | Employer |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| National pension | 9.5% | **4.75%** | 4.75% |
| Health insurance | 7.19% | **3.595%** | 3.595% |
| Long-term care | 0.9448% of pay | **≈0.4724%** | ≈0.4724% |
| Employment insurance, job-seeking benefit | 1.8% | **0.9%** | 0.9% |
| Industrial accident | By industry | **0%** | All of it |

Long-term care is charged as **13.14% of the health insurance contribution**
rather than as a rate on pay, which is why it comes out as the odd 0.9448%
figure — [the long-term care rate](/tracked/long-term-care-rate/).

The employee side adds to roughly **9.7%**, before income tax and local income
tax.

## The pension rate is on a path, not at a level

The national pension rose for the first time since 1998, and 2026 is the first
step rather than the destination —
[the national pension rate](/tracked/national-pension-rate/) records the
legislated path to **13% in 2033**, half a point a year.

<div class="callout callout-warn">
  <p class="callout-title">4.5% is not slightly out of date. It is a widening error</p>
  <p>Most English-language material on Korean payroll still quotes 9% total and
  4.5% for the employee, which was correct through 2025. From 2026 it is wrong,
  and it will be more wrong each January for the next seven years. If a guide
  does not say which year it describes, that alone tells you something about
  the rest of it.</p>
</div>

## Where 9.7% stops being the answer

Each scheme applies its rate to its own base.

The pension uses **standard monthly income**, bounded at
[₩410,000 and ₩6,590,000](/tracked/pension-standard-income-limits/). So a
₩10,000,000 salary does not attract 4.75% of ₩10,000,000 — the contribution
stops climbing at the ceiling, and the effective share of actual pay falls as
pay rises.

Health insurance works on remuneration with its own limits. Employment
insurance uses its own statutory remuneration concept. And none of the three
is the same thing as taxable employment income under the tax code, even though
the non-taxable items overlap heavily.

<div class="callout callout-note">
  <p class="callout-title">Which is why payslip checks fail</p>
  <p>Taking one gross figure and multiplying it by four rates produces four
  numbers that are each close and none of them exactly right. The reconciliation
  has to be done scheme by scheme, on the base each one actually declared. How
  those bases appear line by line is in <a href="/employment/reading-your-korean-payslip/">reading your Korean payslip</a>.</p>
</div>

## A worked month at ₩3,000,000

Assuming every scheme applies and all four bases are ₩3,000,000 — a
simplification, and a useful one:

```
national pension       ₩3,000,000 × 4.75%    = ₩142,500
health insurance       ₩3,000,000 × 3.595%   = ₩107,850
long-term care         ₩3,000,000 × 0.4724%  =  ₩14,172
employment insurance   ₩3,000,000 × 0.9%     =  ₩27,000
industrial accident                              ₩0
                                                ─────────
                                                ₩291,522
```

Income tax and local income tax then come off separately, from the withholding
table rather than from a rate. Treat ₩291,522 as a check, not a target: the
declared bases and rounding will move it.

## What each one actually insures

The framing that costs people money is treating these as a savings scheme with
a poor return. They are four insurances against four different risks.

<div class="steps">
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">1</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>National pension</strong> — old age, disability, and death. Not only retirement: a disabling illness during coverage, or death leaving surviving family, are both within it.</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">2</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>Health and long-term care</strong> — medical costs, the out-of-pocket ceiling on very large bills, national health screening, and long-term daily-living care for those who receive a care grade.</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">3</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>Employment insurance</strong> — unemployment, but also parental leave benefit, maternity-related benefits and employment stability schemes.</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">4</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>Industrial accident</strong> — occupational injury and illness, and commuting accidents that meet the conditions.</div></div>
</div>

## The pension needs ten years to become a pension

An old-age pension in pension form generally requires
[ten years of coverage](/tracked/nps-minimum-contribution-years/).

Below that the contributions are not lost, but they are dealt with under the
refund rules — and those turn on nationality, a social security agreement or
your status of stay, not on the amount paid in. For anyone whose Korean stay is
measured in years rather than decades, that is the question, and it is the
subject of the afternoon guide.

## Long-term care is not a benefit you draw on next year

You pay it monthly; it pays for care for people with a long-term care grade,
through home and facility services. Contributing does not entitle you to an
equivalent sum. That is what makes it insurance rather than saving, and the
same is true of the other three in different measures.

## Industrial accident is never your deduction

The employer pays the entire premium. If a line for employee-borne industrial
accident insurance is reducing your net pay, that is the one item on a Korean
payslip worth querying the same day.

What it buys is concrete: medical care benefits, disability benefits,
survivors' benefits, and — for time off work through an occupational injury —
[70% of average wage per day](/tracked/injury-leave-benefit-rate/) where the
conditions are met.

<div class="callout callout-warn">
  <p class="callout-title">Cover you have never seen deducted is still cover</p>
  <p>Because nothing appears on the payslip, employees frequently do not know
  they are insured at all — which is exactly the situation in which someone can
  be told, wrongly, that they are not covered. Industrial accident insurance is
  judged on the workplace and the employment relationship.</p>
</div>

## The order to check in

```
which schemes you are actually enrolled in
  → the base each one declared
  → the rate for the year, not for 2025
  → the employee share
  → then the tax lines, separately
```

The first line is doing the most work, and it is where foreign employees
diverge from their Korean colleagues — differently for each of the four, under
four different tests. Two people in the same job on the same salary can
correctly have different deductions, and that is the subject of the other half
of this pair.
