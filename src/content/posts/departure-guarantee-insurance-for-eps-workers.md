  - question: 'Do all foreign workers in Korea have departure guarantee insurance?'
    answer: 'No. It is an instrument of the Employment Permit System, so it attaches to E-9 and H-2 employment — not to E-7, F-2, F-5 or foreign employment generally. If you are not an EPS worker, there is no departure insurance to claim and your retirement entitlement runs through the ordinary route: statutory severance, or your workplace’s DB or DC plan. Being told to "wait for the departure insurance" when you were never in the scheme is a sign that somebody has misidentified your status.'
  - question: 'The insurance paid out less than my severance calculation. Is that normal?'
    answer: 'Yes, and it does not mean either figure is wrong. The employer accrues the insurance at 8.3% of your monthly ordinary wage, while statutory severance is computed on average wage — the wages of your last three months divided by the calendar days in that period, with regular bonuses entering separately. Two different bases produce two different numbers. Where the payout is smaller, the difference is still owed to you by the employer; the insurance discharges the policy, not the statutory obligation.'
  - question: 'I already left Korea and never claimed. Have I lost it?'
    answer: 'Not automatically. Claiming before departure is the straightforward route and lets you confirm the amount while you are still here, but a claim can also be made after departure through the prescribed procedure. The harder half is usually the other one: any shortfall between the payout and your statutory severance is owed by your former employer, and pursuing that from abroad is considerably more difficult than raising it before your last day. Deal with the gap first, the policy second.'
  - question: 'Can I collect it at the airport?'
    answer: 'Airport payment after immigration is one of the operating routes, alongside remittance to an overseas account, but treat it as a collection method rather than a plan in itself — you apply first and collect afterwards. Two things decide whether it works: whether you filed in time, and whether your employer completed its side. Confirm both a few weeks before your flight rather than at the counter, and have an overseas account ready as the fallback.'

Departure guarantee insurance is one of the most misdescribed things in
Korean employment. It is called the foreigners' severance, treated as a
pension, and assumed to settle the account on the way out.

It is none of those. It is an insurance policy an employer takes out under
the **Employment Permit System**, and its relationship to your statutory
severance is arithmetic — not identity.

<p class="hand">Two claims, two counterparties, two timetables.</p>

## Who is actually in the scheme

Departure guarantee insurance sits under the Act on the Employment of
Foreign Workers, which means the **Employment Permit System** — principally
**E-9** and **H-2**.

| Status | Departure guarantee insurance |
|---|---|
| E-9, H-2 (EPS) | The employer takes out the policy |
| E-7 and other work statuses | Not in the scheme |
| F-2, F-5, F-6 | Not in the scheme |

If you are outside it, nothing has gone missing. Your entitlement runs
through the ordinary route — statutory severance, or your workplace's DB or
DC plan — which is set out in
[Korean severance pay: what you are actually owed](/employment/severance-pay-and-retirement-pensions-korea/).

## The accrual, and why the number comes out different

The employer accrues monthly at
[8.3% of your monthly ordinary wage](/tracked/departure-guarantee-insurance-rate/).
A payout event can arise where you have been employed a year or more and
depart, or on certain changes of status.

Statutory severance is not computed that way. It uses **average wage** —
total wages over the final three months divided by the calendar days in that
period, with regular bonuses entering as three twelfths of the prior twelve
months.

| | Departure insurance | Statutory severance |
|---|---|---|
| Base | Monthly **ordinary** wage | **Average** wage |
| Accrual | 8.3% monthly, as you go | Computed at the end |
| Paid by | The insurer | The employer |
| What it settles | The policy | The statutory entitlement |

Two bases, two answers. Nothing has gone wrong when they differ — but
something goes wrong when nobody compares them.

## The shortfall is the whole point

```
statutory severance        ₩10,000,000
departure insurance payout  ₩8,700,000
                            ──────────
employer still owes          ₩1,300,000
```

<div class="callout callout-warn">
  <p class="callout-title">"We took out the insurance, so severance is settled"</p>
  <p>This is not a defence, and it is the sentence to listen for. The policy
  discharges the policy. Where the payout falls short of the statutory figure,
  the employer owes the difference — and where the accrual was never properly
  maintained, it owes more than the difference.</p>
</div>

Ask for the accrued amount **before** your last day, not after. Once you have
the statutory calculation and the policy figure side by side, the shortfall
is a number rather than an argument.

## Claiming: before departure is the easy version

You can claim before you leave and confirm the amount while you are still in
the country. Operating routes include remittance to an **overseas account**
and payment at the **airport** after immigration.

<div class="steps">
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">1</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>Confirm the accrued amount</strong> while you are still employed, or immediately after.</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">2</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>Compute your statutory severance separately</strong>, and raise any gap with the employer before your last day.</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">3</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>File the claim and choose the payment route</strong> — overseas remittance or airport collection.</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">4</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>Keep an overseas account ready</strong> regardless. Airport collection is a method, not a guarantee.</div></div>
</div>

If you have already flown without claiming, a claim can still be made after
departure through the prescribed procedure. What becomes genuinely hard from
abroad is the other half — pursuing an employer for the shortfall.

## The two timetables do not line up

Statutory retirement benefits are payable within
[14 days](/tracked/severance-payment-deadline/) of the entitlement arising,
extendable by agreement. The insurance claim runs on its own process, with
its own timing and its own payer.

<div class="callout callout-note">
  <p class="callout-title">Track them as separate items</p>
  <p>An insurance payout arriving is not evidence that the employer has paid
  what it owes, and an employer settling a shortfall is not evidence that the
  policy has been claimed. Two lines in your own notes, each with a date and an
  amount, is the whole method.</p>
</div>

Fourteen days can also outlast your flight. If you leave a week after your
last working day, the statutory deadline expires while you are already home —
which is a reason to fix the destination account and the shortfall before you
go.

## Before your last day

- the accrued departure insurance amount, in writing
- your statutory severance calculation, done properly on average wage
- the difference between them, raised with the employer while you are here
- an overseas account that can receive both
- the employer's loss-of-coverage and departure filings, confirmed done

The rest of the exit — the tax return that can fall due the day before you
fly, and the national pension lump-sum refund, which is a different scheme
again with its own country conditions — is in
[leaving Korea: tax, pension and refunds](/business/leaving-korea-tax-pension-and-refunds/).

For a shortfall an employer will not pay, the Ministry of Employment and
Labor's counselling line is **1350**, and it is far easier to use from inside
Korea than from outside it.
