Two policies from the same insurer can pay completely differently for the same
treatment. The variable is not the company — it is the **generation** of the
contract.

And the generation on sale changed this year, which resets what a new applicant
should be reading.

<p class="hand">The insurer's name tells you almost nothing about the cover.</p>

## The generation is the product

Indemnity policies are grouped into generations, and cover, excesses and limits
are set at that level. The generation
[on sale to new applicants](/tracked/private-health-insurance-generation-on-sale/)
moved from the fourth to the fifth on **6 May 2026**.

So the first thing to establish about your own policy is when it was taken out
and under which generation's terms.

## The 4th generation: covered and non-covered, separated

Sold from July 2021, it split the contract:

| | |
|---|---|
| Main contract | Benefit-covered treatment |
| Rider | Non-covered treatment |

Excesses of broadly **20% on covered** and **30% on non-covered** treatment.
Which is the first correction to a common assumption: even on covered
treatment, what you paid is not fully reimbursed.

### Three non-covered categories with their own limits

The rider carried separate ceilings, of which the best known is
[₩3,500,000 a year and 50 sessions](/tracked/gen4-therapy-limit/) for manual
therapy, extracorporeal shockwave and prolotherapy — with non-covered injections
at ₩2,500,000 and 50 sessions, and non-covered MRI and MRA at ₩3,000,000.

<div class="callout callout-warn">
  <p class="callout-title">Track the sessions as well as the money</p>
  <p>Either can end cover for the year, and a course of therapy consumes the
  session count faster than the ceiling in many cases.</p>
</div>

### Claiming has a price attached

The mechanism that makes the 4th generation unlike ordinary insurance: from July
2024, the **non-covered rider premium** is banded on the previous twelve months
of non-covered claims.

| Non-covered claims, previous year | Next renewal |
|---|---|
| Nothing | Discount |
| Under ₩1,000,000 | Unchanged |
| ₩1,000,000 to under ₩1,500,000 | +100% |
| ₩1,500,000 to under ₩3,000,000 | +200% |
| ₩3,000,000 or more | [+300%](/tracked/gen4-noncovered-premium-tier/) |

Two things routinely lost in the telling:

<div class="steps">
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">1</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>It applies to the non-covered rider premium</strong>, not to the whole policy.</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">2</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>It is reassessed every year.</strong> A heavy year followed by a quiet one moves you back down; it is not a permanent mark.</div></div>
</div>

<div class="callout callout-note">
  <p class="callout-title">Serious illness is carved out</p>
  <p>Treatment relating to conditions under the health insurance special
  calculation scheme, and to long-term care grades 1 and 2, is excluded from the
  banding. So a year of cancer treatment does not put someone in the top band as
  though they had been buying routine therapy.</p>
</div>

## The 5th generation: severe and non-severe

From 6 May 2026 the structure changes again. Non-covered treatment is no longer
one category:

| Rider | Covers |
|---|---|
| Rider 1 | **Severe** non-covered — cancer, cerebrovascular and cardiac disease, rare and intractable conditions, tied to the special calculation scheme |
| Rider 2 | **Non-severe** non-covered — everything else |

The non-severe side is where the reform bites:

- annual ceiling of
  [₩10,000,000](/tracked/gen5-nonsevere-noncovered-limit/)
- inpatient excess of
  [50%](/tracked/gen5-noncovered-copay/)
- outpatient excess of 50% **or ₩50,000, whichever is greater**
- several treatments excluded outright, manual therapy, extracorporeal
  shockwave, prolotherapy and certain non-covered injections among them

<div class="callout callout-warn">
  <p class="callout-title">So the 4th generation therapy limit does not transfer</p>
  <p>Quoting ₩3,500,000 a year for manual therapy to someone buying a policy now
  is not a small inaccuracy — it describes cover that is not in the product they
  can buy. Several of those treatments are outside the non-severe rider
  altogether.</p>
</div>

The trade is explicit: a lower premium — the reform was designed around roughly a
30% reduction — against a much larger share of non-essential non-covered costs
staying with the patient.

## Side by side

| | 4th generation | 5th generation |
|---|---|---|
| On sale to new applicants | Until 5 May 2026 | From 6 May 2026 |
| Non-covered structure | One rider | Severe and non-severe riders |
| Excess, covered | ~20% | Per terms |
| Excess, non-covered | ~30% | Non-severe: 50%, outpatient floor ₩50,000 |
| Therapy limits | ₩3.5m / 50 sessions | Several excluded from non-severe |
| Non-severe annual ceiling | Category limits | ₩10,000,000 |
| Claim-based premium banding | Yes, up to +300% | Differential mechanism retained |
| Premium level | Higher | Lower |

Whether one is better than the other depends entirely on what you use. Someone
who has repeated outpatient therapy each year and someone insuring against a
serious diagnosis are not choosing between the same two products.

## Insurance does not make a non-covered decision free

A doctor recommending non-covered treatment is not a statement about who pays
for it. Across both generations the direction of travel is more patient
responsibility and tighter management of repeated use.

Before starting a course of treatment:

```
is it medically necessary?
  → how many sessions, at what price each?
  → is it covered by my generation's terms?
  → what is my excess and the annual ceiling?
  → what will it do to next year's premium?
```

The last line is the one that does not appear on any hospital estimate.

## Foreign residents: underwriting, not nationality

Long-staying foreign residents can often buy indemnity cover, subject to the
insurer's underwriting. There is no single national rule and no reliable visa
table.

Insurers assess status of stay, length of residence, whether you are in the
national scheme, occupation, age, medical history and recent treatment,
medication and investigations.

<div class="callout callout-warn">
  <p class="callout-title">Do not publish, or trust, a visa eligibility table</p>
  <p>"E-7 yes, F-5 yes, D-2 no" is not a national standard — it is one insurer's
  appetite, generalised. Ask the insurers you are considering.</p>
</div>

And the part that matters more than any of it: **the pre-contract disclosure
duty**. Recent admissions, surgery, recommended further tests, ongoing
medication and chronic conditions have to be answered accurately. A breach does
not surface at application — it surfaces years later, as a rescinded contract or
a refused claim on the treatment you bought the policy for.

## Claiming: prove the reason and the amount

For a small outpatient claim, a receipt and a prescription showing the diagnosis
code often suffice. For larger non-covered amounts, expect more:

- the medical bill and official receipt
- the itemised statement of charges
- a prescription or medical certificate carrying the diagnosis code
- admission and discharge confirmation
- test results or a physician's opinion

<div class="callout callout-note">
  <p class="callout-title">There is no fixed set of four documents</p>
  <p>Nor is a medical certificate always required, and a bank passbook copy is
  usually unnecessary once an account is registered in the insurer's app. The
  real rule is: evidence of the cost, evidence of the diagnosis and treatment,
  and whatever the insurer asks for on top. Ask for the list before assembling
  it.</p>
</div>

What the underlying bill is made of — covered, non-covered, and your statutory
share — is in
[what Korean health insurance does not cover](/insurance/what-korean-health-insurance-does-not-cover/).

The question to answer before buying or claiming is never which company. It is
which generation, what it excludes, what the excess is, and what this year's
claims do to next year's premium.
