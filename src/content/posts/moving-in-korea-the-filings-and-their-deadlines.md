Three numbers govern a Korean move, and they come from three different statutes.

Fourteen days is the Resident Registration Act. Fifteen days is the Immigration
Act. The day after is the Housing Lease Protection Act. Merging them into one
"address deadline" produces advice that is wrong for whoever is reading it.

## 1. Korean nationals: 14 days, and why not to use them

Under article 16 of the Resident Registration Act, a person who changes their
place of residence must file a move-in report within
[14 days](/tracked/resident-move-in-report-deadline/) of moving into the new
address. It can be done at the community centre or through Government24.

But if you have rented the housing, there is no reason to wait.

Opposing power under the Housing Lease Protection Act arises from the day after
the tenant has been delivered the property and completed resident registration.
Every day of delay is a day the deposit sits outside the Act's protection, in
exchange for nothing.

If you can move in on the balance payment date, file that day.

## 2. The move-in report is not the fixed date

These are two different instruments for two different rights.

The move-in report, combined with possession, forms the requirement for
**opposing power** — the right to assert the lease against a third party.

The **fixed date** is what adds **priority repayment** — the right to be paid
ahead of junior creditors in an auction or public sale.

So "I filed the move-in report" does not mean "and the fixed date is handled".

For a jeonse contract, or a monthly lease with a substantial deposit, the
practical order is to obtain the fixed date after signing, and then complete
delivery and the address filing promptly on the balance date. The clause-level
detail is in
[lease clauses that actually protect a tenant](/cost-of-living/lease-clauses-that-actually-protect-a-tenant/).

## 3. Foreign residents: 15 days, and a different filing

Article 36 of the Immigration Act requires a registered foreign national who
changes their stay address to report it within
[15 days](/tracked/foreigner-address-change-deadline/) of moving in.

The filing can be made at the si/gun/gu or eup/myeon/dong community centre for
the new address, or at the competent immigration office, and electronic filing
through Hi Korea is available in some cases. An alien registration card and
something evidencing the actual address — usually the lease — are generally
needed.

## 4. The penalty is a criminal fine, not an administrative one

<div class="callout callout-warn">
  <p class="callout-title">Nearly every English checklist gets this wrong</p>
  <p>A registered foreign national who does not report the change of stay address
  is liable to a fine of
  <a href="/tracked/foreigner-address-change-penalty/">up to ₩1,000,000</a> under
  article 98(2) of the Immigration Act. That is a <strong>벌금</strong>, a criminal
  fine — not a <strong>과태료</strong>, an administrative one. Describing it as
  "up to a ₩1,000,000 administrative fine" misstates the legal character.</p>
</div>

The amount is not the part that matters. The classification is.

For someone whose extension or change of status is assessed partly on their
compliance record, a criminal fine sits differently from a parking-ticket-style
administrative penalty.

It is also worth keeping separate from the rules on failing to report other
changes to alien registration particulars — those sit under different provisions
with a different enforcement structure. Two different obligations, not one.

## 5. A Korean spouse's filing does not cover the foreign spouse

This is the most common way the fifteen days gets missed, and it happens to
people who thought they had done everything.

A Korean household head files the move-in report at the community centre. The
family has moved. The address is updated.

But a registered foreign national's stay address in the immigration record is not
changed by that act. The foreign national has to file their own report, and it is
worth confirming it was actually processed rather than assuming.

For a foreign resident this address is not a postal detail. It is a statutory
entry in the residence management record, and it is also the filing that carries
a foreign tenant's opposing power.

## 6. If the old deposit has not come back, do not file yet

Here is where the pieces collide.

Filing a move-in report at the new address moves your registration away from the
old one. Where the old deposit has not been returned, that can affect the
opposing power and priority repayment you built there.

The instrument for this is the leasehold registration order, and the order of
operations is the entire protection:

  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">1</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>Apply</strong> for the leasehold registration order at the court with jurisdiction over the old property.</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">2</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>Pull a fresh certified copy of the register</strong> and confirm the leasehold entry is actually recorded.</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">3</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>Then vacate</strong> the old property.</div></div>

</div>

  <p class="callout-title">The 2025 position, stated plainly</p>
  <p>The Supreme Court confirmed in 2025 that where possession is given up before
  the leasehold registration is completed, the opposing power already acquired is
  <strong>not retroactively restored</strong> by the later registration. There is no
  fixing it afterwards.
  <a href="/tracked/leasehold-registration-order-effective-point/">The application gives no protection</a> —

</div>

## 7. Mail forwarding is not an address change

The post office's residential relocation service will forward certain mail from
the old address to the new one for a period.

That is redirection. It does not change the address held in any institution's own
records.

So banks, card issuers, insurers, securities firms, online retailers, the mobile
carrier and your employer's HR records all still need doing separately — and
forwarding expiring is how people discover which ones they forgot.

## 8. There is no public service that does them all

<div class="callout callout-note">
  <p class="callout-title">금융주소 한번에 closed in August 2020</p>
  <p>It still appears in moving checklists, and it is
  <a href="/tracked/address-change-bulk-service/">no longer available</a>. Do not
  plan an afternoon around it.</p>
</div>

What exists now is private. KT Moving's bulk address and contact change service
is free, usable on identity verification regardless of which carrier you are
with, and covers partner financial, insurance and retail companies.

The limit is in the word partner. Institutions outside the arrangement are not
reached, so the major banks, card issuers and insurers are still worth opening
individually and checking.

## 9. Moving a business is a separate filing again

If you are moving an office rather than a home, changing your personal address
does not change the address on the business registration.

Sole proprietors and companies both need to look at correcting the business
registration for the change of place of business, and at whether corporate
registration entries or any licences need updating too. What the accounts
themselves need is in
[business vs personal bank accounts in Korea](/business/business-account-vs-personal-account-in-korea/).

## The timeline that works

| When | What | What to keep |
|---|---|---|
| D-14 | Book the internet and TV relocation | Reference number |
| D-14 | Book the gas disconnection and connection | Appointment record |
| D-7 | Check management fees and the repair reserve | Management office certificate |
| D-Day | Read water, electricity and gas | Meter photographs |
| D-Day | Interim management settlement | Settlement statement |
| D-Day | Balance payment and keys | Transfer record, handover note |
| D-Day | File the move-in report if possible | Resident registration |
| Within 15 days | Report the change of stay address | Filing confirmation |
| Just after | Change addresses at banks, cards, insurers | Confirmations |
| Just after | Apply for mail forwarding | Application record |

A statutory deadline tells you the last lawful day. It does not tell you the
right day.

Fourteen days and fifteen days are ceilings; the day after possession is when
protection begins. Which means the whole of a well-run Korean move is closing the
old property's costs and rights cleanly, and connecting possession, address and
fixed date at the new one in a single motion — on the day, not across the
fortnight the law would tolerate.
