Dependant status on Korean health insurance means a family member is covered
without a separate premium. Because of that, the law does not hand it out on
family relationship alone.

Four conditions run in order, and most refusals happen at the third and fourth
rather than the first.

<p class="hand">"They're my parent" answers gate one and nothing else.</p>

## Who is in scope at all

**Spouse.** The clearest case. A marriage concluded abroad may not be visible in
Korean records, so overseas evidence of the relationship can be needed. A de
facto spouse can be recognised where conditions are met, but not automatically —
it takes evidence of the relationship and shared life.

**Parents and grandparents**, including a spouse's lineal ascendants in defined
circumstances. Living apart, or the existence of another relative able to
support them, can be examined.

**Children and grandchildren.** For an adult child, marital status and their own
income and property become decisive.

**Siblings** — the narrow case, below.

<div class="callout callout-warn">
  <p class="callout-title">"Parent" is not a qualification by itself</p>
  <p>Support is a real condition, not a formality attaching to the relationship.
  Cohabitation, whether other lineal descendants exist, and the person's own
  means are all weighed.</p>
</div>

## Income: one combined ceiling

Combined annual income must be
[₩20,000,000 or less](/tracked/nhis-dependent-income-threshold/), combining
interest, dividend, business, employment, pension and other income as the health
insurance rules define them.

<div class="callout callout-note">
  <p class="callout-title">Which is why "no salary" is not the test</p>
  <p>A retired parent with an overseas pension and some investment income has
  income for this purpose. People who have never thought of themselves as earning
  are a large share of the refusals here.</p>
</div>

### Business income has its own rule

A dependant must in principle have **no business income**. Where there is no
business registration, income at or below
[₩5,000,000](/tracked/nhis-dependent-business-income-exception/) is treated as
none.

```
no business registration, ₩4,000,000  →  treated as no business income
business registration, ₩4,000,000     →  a different question entirely
```

So being comfortably under the ₩20,000,000 combined ceiling does not preserve
dependant status for a registered sole trader. Both conditions have to hold.

### If they are married, both spouses are tested

Where the prospective dependant is married, the rules require **both spouses**
to satisfy the income condition.

<div class="callout callout-warn">
  <p class="callout-title">The step most often missed when registering a parent</p>
  <p>You check your mother's income and register her; the application fails
  because of your father's business income. Check the couple, not the individual.</p>
</div>

## Property: not what it would sell for

The property test uses the **property tax assessment base** under the Local Tax
Act — materially lower than market value, and not a number most people know
offhand.

| Combined assessment base | Property condition |
|---|---|
| ₩540,000,000 or less | Met |
| Over ₩540,000,000 to ₩900,000,000 | Met only if annual income is ₩10,000,000 or less |
| Over ₩900,000,000 | Cannot be met |

The [general ceiling](/tracked/nhis-dependent-property-threshold/) is the first
row; the middle band is where applications fail for reasons the applicant cannot
see, because the income ceiling inside it is half the general one.

<div class="callout callout-note">
  <p class="callout-title">Look up the assessment base before concluding anything</p>
  <p>"They own a flat worth a billion won" is not an answer to this test. Find
  the assessment base first — the conclusion frequently reverses.</p>
</div>

## Siblings are a separate regime

Sibling dependants are confined to narrow categories — broadly those under 30,
those 65 or over, persons with disabilities, and certain veterans with
service-related injuries.

And the property ceiling is
[₩180,000,000](/tracked/nhis-sibling-property-threshold/), not the general
figure.

```
relationship category  →  support  →  income  →  ₩180,000,000 property
```

Living in the same home is not a qualification. Applying the general property
threshold to a sibling gets it wrong twice: once on the category, once on the
number.

## Approval is not permanent

The insurance service re-checks dependant status against income and property
data obtained from the tax authorities and local government.

Status can be lost when:

<div class="steps">
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">1</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>New employment or business income</strong> arises.</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">2</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>Property crosses a threshold.</strong></div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">3</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>The family relationship or status of stay changes.</strong></div></div>
</div>

Where status was obtained improperly, it can be removed retroactively to the
date of acquisition — which is a different and worse outcome than losing it
prospectively.

## Losing dependant status is not losing insurance

It generally moves the person to **regional enrolment**, with premiums assessed
on them directly.

So if a change in income or property is coming, work out three things in
advance: when status would be lost, whether regional enrolment follows, and what
that premium would be. Discovering it from a bill is the expensive version. The
three enrolment positions are set out in
[NHIS health insurance for foreigners](/insurance/nhis-korean-health-insurance-for-foreigners/).

## The order to check in

```
relationship category
  → support in fact
  → combined income ≤ ₩20,000,000
  → business income rule, separately
  → both spouses, if married
  → property assessment base, and the middle band
  → siblings: the narrower ceiling
```

A relationship that is beyond doubt still fails on income or property. And for a
foreign family member, all of the above can be satisfied and registration still
not be possible yet — because of a residence condition that arrived in 2024, and
a set of documents the service cannot look up. That is the other half of today's
pair.
