The alien registration card is not the last piece of arrival admin. It is
the first one, and everything else is stacked behind it.

A language student who treats the card as something to sort out "once
settled in" spends six weeks unable to open a proper bank account, unable
to get a phone contract, paying for things in cash, and unable to sign a
lease. The order is not negotiable.

## The deadline, and the deadline behind the deadline

You register **within 90 days of entry**. That is the legal deadline and
it is generous.

The real deadline is the one nobody states: **the appointment slots run
out.** Immigration offices work by reservation through HiKorea, and in the
weeks around a term start, offices near universities fill up. A student
who books on day sixty may find the first free slot falls after day
ninety.

<div class="callout callout-warn">
  <p class="callout-title">Two separate things, two separate dates</p>
  <p>Making the booking and attending the appointment are different events.
  Make the booking in your first week, whatever date it lands on. That is the
  action that protects you; the appointment can be weeks out.</p>
</div>

## What the office wants

The exact list varies by office and by status, so confirm yours on HiKorea
or by calling **1345** before you go. Broadly, expect to bring:

| | What it is | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| Application form | Completed on the day or in advance | Immigration office / HiKorea |
| Passport and visa | The original | You |
| Photo | Passport-standard, recent | Photo booth or studio |
| Fee | Payable at the office | You |
| Proof of study | Certificate of enrolment or admission | Your institute |
| Proof of address | Lease, dormitory confirmation, or equivalent | Landlord or dormitory office |

**The address proof is the one that surprises people.** A student staying
in a friend's room, a short-let, or a place rented informally can arrive
with everything else in order and be turned away because there is no
document tying them to an address. Sort out where you live, and the paper
that proves it, before the appointment.

## The address on the card is a live obligation

Once you are registered, the address is not a historical fact. It is a
declared one, and changing it is reportable within a deadline.

This is the rule language students break most often, and always for the
same reason: the first room is temporary. You take a dormitory place or a
short-term room for the first weeks, then move somewhere real. That move
feels like part of arriving. Administratively it is a change of registered
address with a clock on it.

```
Moved rooms  →  report it  →  within the deadline
```

Two consequences of getting this wrong. The obvious one is the penalty.
The less obvious one is that **official mail goes to the registered
address** — including notices you cannot afford to miss and correspondence
about your own status.

## What opens up, in order

### Bank account

The first thing worth doing with the card. Without it, some banks will
open a limited account, and the limits are strict enough that it does not
really function — the practical ceiling is low enough that you cannot pay
a deposit or a term of tuition through it.

What the branch asks for, and why the first account is restricted, is in
[opening a bank account in Korea as a foreigner](/banking/how-to-open-a-bank-account-in-korea-as-a-foreigner/).

### Phone

A monthly contract needs identity verification, and identity verification
for a foreign resident runs on the registration number. Prepaid SIMs work
before that and are the sensible bridge; they are also more expensive per
month, so it is a bridge rather than a destination.

Plan structures, prepaid against contract, and what a foreign resident can
actually sign up for are in
[Korean phone plans for foreigners](/cost-of-living/korean-phone-plans-for-foreigners/).

### Health insurance

Long-term foreign residents are inside the national health insurance
system, and students have their own treatment within it. Enrolment and
billing follow from being a registered resident, so this too begins moving
after the card.

The system, what it covers and how contributions work is in
[NHIS health insurance for foreigners](/insurance/nhis-korean-health-insurance-for-foreigners/).

The card takes time to be produced after the appointment, which is the
last thing people mis-plan. Counting from landing to a working bank
account, the realistic answer is weeks, not days — and it is weeks longer
for anyone who waited to make the booking.

The visa is what let you in. This is what lets you live here, and the two
are separated by an appointment you should already have booked. What the
visa itself turns on is in
[the D-4 visa for studying Korean](/study/d-4-visa-korean-language-study/).
