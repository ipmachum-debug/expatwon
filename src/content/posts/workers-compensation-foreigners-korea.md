A workplace injury creates more than one problem. The patient needs medical
treatment, the employer must manage workplace safety and records, and the
worker may need a statutory insurance determination and income during
recovery. For a foreign worker, language and immigration uncertainty add
another layer. None of these should be reduced to *"the boss pays the hospital
bill."* Korea's industrial-accident insurance has its own claim and benefit
structure, and a private settlement may not replace that structure.

## Prioritize treatment and factual records

In an emergency, obtain appropriate care first. Record the time, place,
equipment, task, witnesses and immediate symptoms as soon as practical,
without delaying care. Preserve photos of the scene where safe and authorized,
accident reports, shift records, medical notes, diagnoses, receipts and any
discussion about the circumstances. Do not alter the scene or fabricate a
witness statement. A later injury dispute often turns on whether the event and
its connection to work can be reconstructed reliably.

Explain to medical staff that the injury occurred during work and ask how they
record occupational circumstances. The employer's accident report, the
hospital record and the insurance application are not necessarily the same
document. Check that dates, job tasks and injury description match, and
explain any corrections transparently. Missing a reporting form should not
cause a worker to hide symptoms or postpone medically necessary attention.

## What statutory treatment benefits cover

Article 40 of the Industrial Accident Compensation Insurance Act establishes
medical-care benefits for a work-related injury or disease and identifies a
range that includes examination, medicine, treatment, rehabilitation,
hospitalization, nursing and transport subject to the legal framework. It also
states that medical-care benefits are not provided where the injury can be
cured with treatment within three days. Do not translate that limited
threshold into the false claim that every three-day absence creates full
compensation rights. Benefit categories have separate conditions.

Ask the treating institution and the Korea Workers' Compensation & Welfare
Service how the case should be filed, which medical facility arrangements
apply and what bills, if any, fall outside statutory coverage. Private health
insurance, ordinary national health insurance and industrial-accident
insurance should not be conflated. A worker may need to explain the
work-related nature of the event and obtain a formal determination.

## Income replacement is not salary continuation

Article 52 provides temporary disability benefits for eligible time during
which a worker cannot work due to treatment for a work-related injury or
disease. Its stated general daily rate is 70% of average wages, with an
exception where the period unable to work is three days or less. The
definition and calculation of average wages, applicable floors or adjustments
and the period recognized for payment may change the actual outcome. Do not
promise 70% of the worker's last monthly paycheck without doing the statutory
calculation.

A separate question is whether the company pays wages or other benefits under
the labour contract, a collective agreement or applicable law. The existence of
an insurance benefit does not itself explain every employer obligation. Keep
wage slips, work calendars and medical certificates so the relevant period and
earnings basis can be established. Avoid entering a private settlement that
purports to resolve unidentified future care or disability rights without
independent review.

## Foreign status is not a reason to hide the accident

Korean industrial-accident protection and immigration compliance are different
legal systems. A worker may be worried about employment authorization,
residence expiry or a change in job. The specific facts require competent
advice, but the worker should not assume that reporting an injury
automatically removes all protection, or that an employer's willingness to
cover a small bill is an adequate substitute for investigating statutory
coverage. Obtain support from the relevant agency or a qualified adviser,
ideally with language assistance.

A company may discourage reporting because of perceived compliance
consequences. That is precisely why the worker should preserve independent
medical and employment records. If there is a dispute about employee status or
which business controlled the work, document the real chain of supervision,
payroll and job assignment rather than relying on labels such as subcontractor
or freelancer.

## What happens after initial treatment

Recovery may involve further treatment, rehabilitation, a determination about
lasting impairment and decisions on return to work. The initial claim and
later benefits are distinct stages; eligibility for one should not be casually
described as approval of every future benefit. If the condition worsens after
being considered healed, the Act separately addresses additional medical care
in defined circumstances. Keep medical follow-up records and ask the agency
which applications are needed.

For the employer, a defensible incident file should include immediate
response, objective investigation, corrective actions and legally required
reporting. The goal is not to coach a statement to protect the company. It is
to establish how the event happened and prevent recurrence. For the employee,
the file establishes the injury and the treatment chronology. The two
interests overlap in the need for accurate facts, even where compensation is
disputed.

## The employer's role and the worker's own evidence

An employer can help by supplying a truthful accident description, employment
records and wage information. But an employee should not let the entire
evidentiary record exist only in the employer's office. Keep copies of
employment terms, attendance records, messages showing the task assignment,
photographs that were lawfully taken and medical certificates. A missing
written employment contract does not mean there was no work relationship,
though establishing the relevant facts may take more evidence.

Where an employer disputes that the accident occurred in the course of work,
separate the disputed propositions. Was the worker performing a task? Who
directed it? Was the location authorized? When did symptoms appear? What did
contemporaneous witnesses observe? Present a coherent timeline rather than
repeating *"it happened at work"* without supporting facts.

## The foreign-worker question

Immigration status, workplace insurance administration and occupational-injury
compensation interact, but they are not the same inquiry. Do not tell every
foreign worker that their specific visa guarantees every benefit, or that a
paperwork problem automatically bars compensation. Ask the responsible
institution to apply the current law to the employment arrangement. Where
someone faces an immediate residence or employment-status issue during
recovery, seek immigration guidance separately and promptly.

A transfer, dismissal or termination after injury can also create a distinct
labour issue. Preserve the dates of medical certification, employment changes
and insurance communications. The validity of a termination and eligibility
for injury-related benefits should not be collapsed into one question.

## Rehabilitation, return to work and lasting impairment

An injured employee and employer should document medically appropriate
return-to-work restrictions, follow-up appointments, modified tasks and any
workplace adjustments. The ability to perform one light task does not
necessarily resolve the question of recovery or permanent impairment. Avoid
making medical predictions in a financial guide. If symptoms persist, ensure
treatment notes describe them accurately and request the relevant official
assessment process when indicated.

A useful financial plan distinguishes wages or statutory income replacement
during recovery, medical expenses, potential rehabilitation expenses and any
longer-term effect on earning capacity. These are different cash flows with
different evidence. The objective is not to maximize a dramatic claim but to
ensure the worker understands each decision and can document the real loss.

## Five statutory figures that change the compensation decision

The Act contains concrete numerical rules, but they belong to different
benefit categories. Treating them as one percentage of monthly salary is a
mistake.

| Claim issue | Statutory figure | What the figure does not tell you |
|---|---|---|
| Temporary disability (휴업급여) | 70% of legally calculated average daily wages, for qualifying inability to work during treatment | Not 70% of any stated monthly salary, and not an approval of the work-related injury |
| Short inability to work | No temporary-disability benefit where the inability to work is three days or fewer | Not proof that a four-day absence guarantees a benefit; the other statutory requirements remain |
| Permanent disability | Grades 1 to 14, the benefit form depending on grade and statutory exceptions | A grade cannot be inferred from the name of an injury or from a photograph |
| Ordinary limitation on benefit claims | Three years | Not a promise that every claim accrues on the injury date, or that every kind of claim shares one limit |
| Listed exceptions to that limitation | Five years for disability, survivor, funeral, pneumoconiosis compensation pension and pneumoconiosis survivor pension | Not a general five-year limit for all wage losses or every civil claim |

Temporary disability is Article 52; disability compensation Article 57;
limitation periods Article 112.

## Calculate the temporary-disability base, not the monthly salary

Article 52 expresses the temporary-disability benefit per day, based on
statutory average wages, for a worker unable to work because of treatment for
a recognized occupational injury or disease. If a legally determined average
daily wage were ₩120,000, the simple Article 52 starting point would be
₩84,000 per eligible day (₩120,000 × 70%). For a hypothetical twelve eligible
days, that is ₩1,008,000 before considering any adjustment or special payment
rule.

<p class="hand">Those three figures are an arithmetic illustration. They are not an agency decision, a monthly-salary conversion, or a suggestion that twelve days will be approved.</p>

An absence of three days or fewer does not attract temporary-disability
benefit under the article. The medical-benefit question under Article 40
remains a different assessment.

## Grade 1–14 is an evaluation system, not an injury price list

Under Article 57, disability compensation is considered after healing where a
work-related injury or disease has resulted in a qualifying remaining
impairment. The statute refers to disability compensation pensions or lump
sums in Appendix 2, while Enforcement Decree Article 53 and its Appendix 6
establish the grading criteria. Grade 1 is not interchangeable with Grade 14,
and there is no responsible method for converting a short diagnosis into a
grade. Request the formal assessment and identify which benefits the resulting
grade actually supports.

Article 57(3) specifically requires a lump-sum disability benefit for a
non-Korean national residing abroad at the time the disability-benefit claim
arises. Read that for what it is: a defined rule about the **form of payment**,
not a rule denying industrial-accident coverage to foreigners in Korea. Other
statutory grade-based restrictions on payment form also apply.

## A three-year deadline is not the whole story

Article 112(1) states a three-year limitation for the rights listed there,
with an express five-year limit for disability benefits, survivor benefits,
funeral expenses, pneumoconiosis compensation pensions and pneumoconiosis
survivor pensions. The starting point of a particular claim depends on when
its right arises, so neither figure should automatically be counted from the
day of the accident.

Article 113 addresses interruption of limitation upon a statutory benefits
claim, and extends that effect to other statutory benefit types in the
specified first-claim situation requiring a decision on whether an accident
was work-related. That is a reason to document the filing date, not a reason
to wait.

For a real case, put the treatment dates, the unable-to-work dates, the date
of healing, the type of benefit sought and the date filed on separate lines.
That is how the statutory numbers become decision-useful, instead of sounding
precise while describing the wrong right.

<p class="hand">Scope: statutory benefit rates, the disability-grade framework and the limitation periods were checked against the Act effective 1 July 2026. Occupational causation, your average wages, an impairment grade, immigration consequences and a claim-specific accrual date are all agency determinations, not things this guide can settle.</p>
