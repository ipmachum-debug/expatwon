You open the banking app to pay rent. The balance says ₩4,100,000. The
transfer fails, twice, and the bank tells you the account is under a payment
suspension connected to suspected voice phishing.

You did not lend your account to anyone. You did not agree to receive anything
from a stranger. But ₩100,000 you never asked for arrived, and now ₩4.1
million you do depend on is out of reach.

The ₩100,000 is about 2.4% of what is showing. The restriction is on all of
it. That gap — between the size of the suspect payment and the size of the
problem it creates — is the whole shape of this situation.

## Ask the right first question

Not *why can't I withdraw my money*. Ask what the legal or contractual basis
for the restriction is, and then: when it started, whether it is a payment
suspension under the telecommunications-financial-fraud legislation, whether a
separate electronic-banking restriction also applies, whether a
creditor-extinguishment procedure has been announced, what the objection
procedure and required documents are, which department holds the case, and
what reference you can quote.

You are not entitled to everything in someone else's fraud report. You are
entitled to what you need to exercise your own procedural rights.

If the answer is that this is an identity-verification problem rather than a
fraud suspension, stop and go down that route instead. Those get confused
constantly — an expired residence card and a suspected phishing deposit
produce the same error message and need completely different paperwork.

## What the objection actually argues

The Act provides an objection for the holder of an affected account, and the
grounds are narrower than "I am innocent". In practice they come in three
shapes: that the account was not used for the alleged fraud at all; that the
money was received on a legitimate basis, such as payment for goods or
services; or that the account was not used to obtain victims' assets.

The second one carries a limitation worth reading twice. It is available to a
holder who did not know, and was not grossly negligent in failing to know,
that the account was being used for financial fraud. An invoice proves a
transaction happened. It does not by itself prove the holder's state of mind
about where the money came from.

So the evidence has to explain three things, not one: where the money came
from, why it was paid, and what happened afterwards.

| If the payment was | Assemble |
| --- | --- |
| A sale of goods | Listing, contract or order, buyer messages, handover or delivery proof, the transfer record |
| Salary | Employment contract, payroll statement, employer confirmation of the payment |
| Rent or a deposit | The lease, prior payment history on the same account |
| Loan repayment | The original loan agreement and earlier transfers between the same parties |
| Paid by a third party | An explanation of the relationship, plus whatever documents the arrangement generated |

Labelling an incoming transfer "salary" in the app proves nothing. Altering a
record or writing a contract after the fact to fit the payment is a far worse
problem than the freeze.

## The deadline is not counted from the freeze

This is the detail that quietly costs people the objection.

The statutory window ends relative to the public announcement of the
creditor-extinguishment procedure — not the date the suspension began, not the
date you discovered it, and not the date of the bank's first text message.
Those dates differ, sometimes by a week.

| | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Payment suspension begins | 10 November |
| Creditor-extinguishment procedure announced | 18 November |
| Objection deadline | Runs from the announcement, under the Act's period |

Ask the bank for the announcement date and the deadline it produces. If no
announcement has been made yet, file your evidence anyway rather than waiting
for a clock you cannot see.

## What five business days means

In May 2026 the Financial Supervisory Service announced standardised handling
of objections from account holders who were not involved in the fraud, after
cases where small suspicious deposits left innocent people locked out for
months. The package covered common minimum documents for the main objection
categories, requests for more evidence where a first submission fell short,
simplified treatment of qualifying small-payment cases, and notification of
the review result within {{fig:phishing-objection-review-days}} where the
application is complete.

Read that last item precisely. It is a standard for *answering* the objection.
It is not a deadline for unfreezing the account, and the two get quoted
interchangeably. A bank can meet it by telling you, on day five, that it needs
more evidence.

## Why winning the objection may not reopen the account

The Act also governs when a suspension actually ends, and it carries
exceptions. Related litigation, the protection of the alleged victim's assets,
and a notification period following an objection can all keep a restriction in
place after the objection itself has been accepted. Separate restrictions —
anti-money-laundering, identity verification, a court order — survive on their
own terms regardless.

So ask the bank to separate four things: the objection result, the status of
the fraud-related suspension, any continuing electronic-banking restriction,
and anything imposed under a different law. Restoring one function does not
restore the account.

## Partial release, and the ₩4 million question

Where the statutory conditions are met, terminating a suspension for part of
an account or part of the funds is contemplated — and this matters most in
exactly the situation at the top of this page, where a small suspect deposit
has immobilised a much larger balance.

The Financial Services Commission has described this publicly in the context
of 통장협박, the extortion pattern where criminals deposit money into an
innocent person's account specifically to trigger a freeze and then demand
payment. A holder who can objectively demonstrate they were not involved may
seek relief for funds unrelated to the suspected victim's money.

What that does not mean is that ₩4 million is automatically released because
₩100,000 arrived later. Ask whether partial release is available on your
facts. Do not assume it, and do not attempt a workaround through someone
else's account.

## While you wait, the rent is still due

A legitimate objection takes time the calendar does not grant. Rent, utilities
and living costs do not pause, and a restricted balance is not available
money, whatever the app displays.

Tell the landlord and the service providers now rather than after a missed
payment. If salary is normally deposited into the restricted account, discuss
future payroll arrangements through legitimate channels. What you must not do
is route disputed funds through a friend or an intermediary to get around the
restriction — that converts a dispute you are likely to win into conduct you
will have to explain.

## Two things that make it much worse

Lending, renting or selling access to a bank account is not a harmless favour;
the Financial Services Commission has warned that it can breach the Electronic
Financial Transactions Act and expose the holder to criminal liability. The
objection grounds above distinguish innocent holders precisely from those who
knew, or were grossly negligent in not knowing. Commission offered for
receiving money and passing it on is the shape of that offence.

And if the freeze followed unauthorised access rather than an unexpected
deposit — credentials stolen, transfers you did not make — report it to the
bank's fraud department and the police immediately, preserve device and
notification records, and check for unfamiliar accounts or loans opened in
your name. Deleting messages to tidy the account history destroys the evidence
that would have cleared you.

## What to say at the bank

> 제 계좌가 보이스피싱 관련 지급정지 상태라고 안내받았습니다. 저는 사기에
> 가담한 사실이 없으며, 지급정지 사유와 관련 법적 절차를 확인하고 싶습니다.
> 이의제기신청서와 필요한 소명자료, 접수기한을 안내해 주세요.

*I have been told my account is under a payment suspension related to
suspected voice phishing. I had no part in any fraud, and I would like to
confirm the reason for the restriction and the applicable legal procedure.
Please explain the objection form, the required evidence and the filing
deadline.*

Say the second sentence only if it is true.

## This is not the mistaken-transfer process

| | Mistaken transfer | Fraud-related freeze |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who needs help | The sender | The account holder |
| The problem | Money reached the wrong person | The account is restricted |
| Where it starts | The sending bank | The bank holding the restricted account |
| Key evidence | Transfer confirmation, failed return attempt | Source of funds, lawful account activity |
| The deadline | One year from the transfer | Tied to the announcement, under the Act |

If you are the one who sent money to the wrong account, the route runs through
[your bank's return request and then KDIC](/banking/wrong-bank-transfer-korea-kdic-recovery/),
not through an objection. A frozen account is not evidence that a mistaken
transfer arrived, and a mistaken transfer is not an accusation of fraud.

<p class="hand">Being unable to use your account does not prove wrongdoing — but saying so is not evidence either.</p>
