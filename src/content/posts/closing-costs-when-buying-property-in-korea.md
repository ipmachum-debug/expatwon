The cash a Korean property purchase needs is not the price minus the
mortgage.

On completion day, alongside the balance owed to the seller, several
things fall due at once: acquisition tax, local education tax, sometimes
rural special tax, the estate agent's fee, the loss on discounting
national housing bonds, the registration costs, the judicial scrivener's
fee, and stamp duty.

**How much you can borrow, and how LTV and DSR are calculated, is a
separate question** — that is
[getting a mortgage in Korea](/loans/korean-mortgage-for-foreigners/).
This guide is only the cash the loan will not cover.

## Acquisition tax is the big one

For an individual acquiring housing for consideration, the base rate
moves with the price:

| Acquisition price | Rate |
|---|---|
| ₩600,000,000 or under | 1% |
| Over ₩600M to ₩900M | 1–3%, on the statutory sliding formula |
| Over ₩900,000,000 | 3% |

Local education tax comes on top, and rural special tax applies in some
cases. For a standard home in the 1% band, local education tax normally
adds 0.1% of the price.

Rural special tax is where floor area decides it. At an **exclusive
floor area of 85㎡ or under**, an ordinary home is not charged it. Above
85㎡, an ordinary single-home acquisition can pick up a further 0.2%.

So the same ₩500,000,000 home is two different tax bills:

<div class="check-card">
  <div class="check-row ok"><span class="mark"></span><span><strong>85㎡ or under</strong> — ₩5,000,000 acquisition tax + ₩500,000 local education tax = <strong>₩5,500,000</strong></span></div>
  <div class="check-row miss"><span class="mark"></span><span><strong>Over 85㎡</strong> — the same, plus ₩1,000,000 rural special tax = <strong>₩6,500,000</strong></span></div>
  <div class="check-result">₩1,000,000 turns on the floor area alone, on an otherwise identical purchase.</div>
</div>

Owners of more than one home, and companies, are on a separate and
heavier schedule. A company acquiring housing, or an additional home in a
regulated zone, can leave the 1–3% range entirely.

<div class="callout callout-note">
  <p class="callout-title">Being foreign does not change the rate</p>
  <p>There is no foreigner surcharge in the acquisition tax schedule. What
  decides the rate is the acquiring party, the number of homes held after
  the purchase, the price, the region and the nature of the dwelling —
  nationality is not one of the inputs.</p>
</div>

## The scrivener's invoice is not the scrivener's fee

Most buyers hand the ownership transfer registration to a judicial
scrivener, to be done at the moment the balance is paid.

That is not a tax, and the total you transfer to them is not their
charge. A typical invoice combines:

<div class="steps">
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">1</div>
    <div class="step-body">The scrivener's own professional fee</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">2</div>
    <div class="step-body">Registration application fees</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">3</div>
    <div class="step-body">Certificate and document issuance costs</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">4</div>
    <div class="step-body">The national housing bond discount</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">5</div>
    <div class="step-body">Acquisition tax and other taxes paid on your behalf</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">6</div>
    <div class="step-body">VAT, and sundry disbursements</div></div>
</div>

Ask for those separated before completion, and keep the tax receipts,
the bond calculation and the registration fees as distinct documents
afterwards. On an ordinary apartment around ₩500,000,000 the fee and
disbursements often land in the several-hundred-thousand-won range,
varying with difficulty and region — **there is no single national
statutory scale**.

## The agent's fee is a ceiling, not a price

Housing sales have maximum brokerage rates by transaction band, and the
actual fee is agreed within that maximum between the client and the
agent. In Seoul:

| Transaction value | Maximum |
|---|---|
| Under ₩50,000,000 | 0.6%, capped at ₩250,000 |
| ₩50M to under ₩200M | 0.5%, capped at ₩800,000 |
| ₩200M to under ₩900M | 0.4% |
| ₩900M to under ₩1.2B | 0.5% |
| ₩1.2B to under ₩1.5B | 0.6% |
| ₩1.5B and above | 0.7% |

A ₩500,000,000 apartment sits in the 0.4% band, so the ceiling is
₩2,000,000. You can negotiate below it — the number is a limit on what
may be charged, not a tariff.

Where the agent is a standard-regime taxpayer, VAT can be charged on top.
Settle **whether the quoted figure includes VAT** at contract stage: a
₩2,000,000 fee with 10% added is ₩2,200,000 in actual cash.

## The housing bond does not cost you its face value

This is the line most misread on a completion statement.

Registering ownership of certain property requires you to buy First-Class
National Housing Bonds. The purchase amount is calculated from the
**published assessed value**, not the price you paid, at a rate that
moves with that value and with where the property is:

| Assessed value | Metropolitan | Elsewhere |
|---|---|---|
| ₩20M – under ₩50M | 13 / 1,000 | 13 / 1,000 |
| ₩50M – under ₩100M | 19 / 1,000 | 14 / 1,000 |
| ₩100M – under ₩160M | 21 / 1,000 | 16 / 1,000 |
| ₩160M – under ₩260M | 23 / 1,000 | 18 / 1,000 |
| ₩260M – under ₩600M | **26 / 1,000** | 21 / 1,000 |
| ₩600M and above | 31 / 1,000 | 26 / 1,000 |

A Seoul home assessed at ₩500,000,000 sits in the fifth band:

```
₩500,000,000 × 26/1,000 = ₩13,000,000 of bonds to buy
```

**That ₩13,000,000 is not a cost.** In practice buyers rarely hold the
bonds; they sell them the same day at the registration counter. What you
actually lose is the discount on that immediate sale:

```
₩13,000,000 × 8.5% ≈ ₩1,105,000
```

**Where that discount comes from** is the part nothing explains. The bond
pays **1.0% a year over five years**, compounded annually and repaid in
one sum at the end. When the market wants more than 1.0% to hold
five-year paper, nobody buys it at face value — and the gap between what
it pays and what the market wants is the discount. So the discount is not
a fee anyone charges you. It is the price of getting out of a
below-market bond immediately instead of waiting five years.

That coupon has moved a great deal: 3.00% until mid-2012, down to 1.00%
by August 2019, up to 1.30% in December 2022, back to 1.00% from April
2025. [Its full history is tracked here](/tracked/national-housing-bond-coupon-rate/).

<div class="callout callout-tip">
  <p class="callout-title">Buying jointly lowers the rate</p>
  <p>Where a property is bought in more than one name, the assessed value is
  apportioned by each buyer's share and <strong>each buyer's rate comes from
  their own share's band</strong>, not from the whole. Two people splitting a
  home 50/50 can each land a band or two lower than one person buying it
  alone. On a couple's purchase that is worth checking before the registration
  is drawn up, because the saving is real and it is not recoverable
  afterwards.</p>
</div>

One thing to ignore: if a guide mentions a Second-Class National Housing
Bond, it is out of date. Issuance of those stopped on 31 May 2013.

Two things follow, and both matter on the day:

<div class="check-card">
  <div class="check-row miss"><span class="mark"></span><span><strong>The discount rate moves daily</strong>, with bond market yields. The figure on a quote you received a few days before completion is not the figure you will pay on the morning.</span></div>
  <div class="check-row miss"><span class="mark"></span><span><strong>The assessed value is not the purchase price.</strong> For an apartment it is the published multi-family housing price, which has to be looked up separately — and is often lower, which reduces this cost.</span></div>
  <div class="check-result">Purchase price → not the input. Assessed value × statutory purchase rate × the day's discount rate → the actual loss.</div>
</div>

## Stamp duty, and who is supposed to pay it

The Stamp Tax Act treats a document transferring ownership of real
property as taxable. Where the stated amount is over ₩100,000,000 and up
to ₩1,000,000,000, the stamp duty is **₩150,000**. A ₩500,000,000
apartment contract sits in that band.

Where two or more parties draw up the document together, liability is
joint. In practice buyer and seller often split it, but the law does not
prescribe ₩75,000 each. **Budget the full ₩150,000 on the buyer's side**
and treat any split as a saving rather than an assumption.

## What a ₩500,000,000 purchase actually needs in cash

Assuming: Seoul, one home after purchase, 85㎡ or under, agent fee at the
cap, agent on the standard VAT regime, assessed value taken as
₩500,000,000 for illustration, a discount rate around 8.5%, and no
first-time-buyer relief.

| Item | Expected cash |
|---|---|
| Acquisition tax | ₩5,000,000 |
| Local education tax | ₩500,000 |
| Rural special tax | ₩0 |
| Agent fee | ₩2,000,000 |
| VAT on the agent fee | ₩200,000 |
| Housing bond discount loss | ≈₩1,105,000 |
| Stamp duty | up to ₩150,000 |
| Scrivener fee and registration | ≈₩500,000–800,000 |
| **Total** | **≈₩9,455,000–9,755,000** |

If the assessed value is below the purchase price the bond cost falls. If
you negotiate the agent's fee below the cap it falls again. Go **over
85㎡** and rural special tax adds roughly ₩1,000,000, taking the total to
about ₩10,455,000–10,755,000.

The same headline price does not produce the same closing cost twice.

## Leave a margin in the account on completion day

Structure the completion money as its own set of buckets rather than one
figure:

```
Your share of the purchase price
+ acquisition tax and the taxes with it
+ agent fee
+ housing bond discount
+ scrivener and registration
+ stamp duty
+ a reserve
```

Matching the loan drawdown and your own funds to the last won, and
running the balance down to nothing, is the mistake. The bond discount
rate can move, a floor-area or homes-held judgement can change the
acquisition tax, and registration disbursements can be added.

Get an estimate from the scrivener before contract, then recalculate
acquisition tax, the bond cost and registration two or three days before
completion.

<div class="callout callout-tip">
  <p class="callout-title">Keep the receipts separated, for two later reasons</p>
  <p>Ask for acquisition tax, local education tax, rural special tax, the bond
  purchase amount, the bond discount loss, the scrivener's fee and the
  registration disbursements as individual lines with their own receipts, not
  as "registration costs: ₩7,000,000".</p>
  <p>They matter twice more. If you brought money in from abroad, the trail
  showing which account paid the price, the tax and the scrivener is what
  answers a source-of-funds question later. And when you eventually sell,
  acquisition-related costs are the material for the necessary-expense side of
  the capital gains calculation.</p>
</div>

For a ₩500,000,000 apartment — one home, 85㎡ or under, Seoul, no relief
— plan on roughly **₩9,500,000 to ₩10,000,000 in closing cash**, held
separately from the loan and from your share of the price.
