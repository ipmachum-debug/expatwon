**The hard part can be finding an appointment—not understanding the extension form.**

Your period of stay expires in six weeks. You have a passport, a residence card and every employment or school document you expect to need. But HiKorea shows no suitable appointment at your local immigration office before the deadline. The website accepts your search, displays a calendar and gives you no workable time slot. Now the real question appears: does having tried to book count as having applied?

No. **Checking a calendar, holding an appointment and submitting a valid extension application are three different things.** Confusing them can turn an ordinary scheduling problem into a serious immigration problem.

Korea's Ministry of Justice explains that many in-person stay applications require an advance reservation and that eligible extension applications can begin **four months before the authorized-stay expiry date**. It also explains that some categories can use an electronic application rather than an in-person visit. The exact available route and final filing conditions should be verified against current HiKorea guidance.

## An appointment is access to the counter, not immigration permission

HiKorea's advance reservation system manages visits to immigration offices. It does not, by itself, extend lawful stay. A booked visit is evidence that you intend to attend an office on a particular date; an extension application is a separate legal filing subject to substantive review.

Imagine a person whose stay expires October 30 and whose appointment is November 12. A screenshot of the future booking should not be treated as automatic authorization to remain through November. The fact that the website offered or displayed a date is not a substitute for an official determination about timely filing and permitted stay.

The opposite is also important. A timely accepted electronic application may create a procedural record without an in-person appointment, where the applicant and status category qualify for that channel. Whether the applicant can use it depends on the service and current requirements.

**Your objective is to file an eligible application on time, not merely to reserve a visit.**

## Work backward from the authorized-stay end date

Start with the expiry date shown in immigration records, not the planned end of your employment contract or the date you bought a plane ticket. These can differ.

The Ministry of Justice's published guidance states that an extension can generally be requested from **four months before expiry**. It also explains in a common FAQ that an in-person extension needs to be requested by expiry, while eligible electronic petitions have had an earlier cutoff—described there as **the day before** the expiry date. Because online system hours, categories and rules can change, check the current service screen instead of treating an old deadline summary as a substitute for instructions.

That four-month window is not a recommendation to wait until the last week. It is a planning opportunity. Employers and universities may need time to produce documents; tax and residence records may require corrections; popular immigration offices can have booking pressure. The earlier the file is prepared, the more choices remain.

For a worker, set reminders around four months, two months, one month and two weeks before the authorized date. For a family, track each person's status independently. One adult's appointment does not necessarily cover every dependent's filing.

## The booking wall has several different causes

“No slots available” might mean the local office is heavily booked, the selected service or applicant category has limited slots, the requested period is outside the available booking window, or the website has a temporary issue. Do not assume a hidden technical trick will produce lawful priority access.

A recurring mistake is choosing the wrong office. Immigration jurisdiction generally relates to the person's registered residence or applicable case type, so an appointment in a more convenient district may not be usable. Check the jurisdiction before chasing an open time at a distant branch.

A second mistake is treating the **visit reservation date** as the **last day to act**. If the booked date comes after the authorized-stay end, the applicant needs procedural advice before the deadline. Do not rely on an online booking confirmation as an implicit extension.

A third mistake is assuming every service requires the same booking process. Some electronic applications or authorized alternatives may be available for particular statuses, while cases requiring interview or original-document review may need an office visit. The menu should be read for the actual service being requested.

## Check electronic petitions before refreshing the calendar

HiKorea has an **전자민원** electronic civil-petition system. Official guidance states that eligible stay-extension applications can be submitted through it; the availability depends on status and case conditions. Some older government materials have also described a reduction in fees for eligible electronic petitions, but fees and service availability should be checked in the current system.

The first question when the appointment calendar is empty is therefore not “How frequently should I refresh?” It is “Can my specific extension be filed electronically today?”

Log into the official site, locate the relevant petition, read the eligibility requirements and verify which supporting documents must be uploaded. A service may appear on the menu but still reject an ineligible case based on status, previous compliance or required in-person verification.

Do not submit a false or incomplete filing simply to obtain a receipt. An application must satisfy the procedural requirements to have the intended effect. Where the screen is ambiguous, contact **1345**, the immigration contact center, and ask which channel is accepted for your status and deadline.

## If there is no suitable appointment, act before expiry

First, record the expiry date and the failed booking attempts. Screenshots can help explain what happened; they do not replace filing. Second, verify whether electronic application is possible. Third, contact 1345 or the competent office about a timely application where no visit can be reserved. Ask specifically whether there is an official alternative procedure for deadline cases, not whether “a screenshot is enough.”

Do not assume there is a nationwide walk-in exception. Offices can operate different intake arrangements under official instructions, and ad hoc reports from social media do not establish a right to be seen without reservation. Nor should you assume an intermediary can create a lawful slot unavailable to everyone else.

If an authorized immigration administrative agent or other proper representative is used, understand which functions that representative may perform and what remains the applicant's responsibility. A paid service does not guarantee an extension approval or excuse false documents.

If you are close to expiry, treat the issue as time-sensitive. Waiting for a more convenient appointment while the filing deadline passes is a very different risk from preparing for an ordinary visit.

## Build the file before securing the slot

The standard documents described by immigration guidance include the **integrated application form**, passport, residence card and proof of residence; additional documents depend heavily on the underlying status. Employees may need employer and income documents, students school records, and family-category residents relationship evidence or other supporting materials.

Use the status-specific manual available through HiKorea rather than a universal “visa extension checklist.” An E-7 applicant and an F-6 applicant have different grounds for staying. The officer needs evidence that the relevant grounds continue to be satisfied, not merely evidence that the applicant wants to stay.

Organize the file by claim. For work status: current employment, permitted occupation, employer information, compensation and tax compliance as required. For studies: continued enrollment or academic situation under the relevant status. For family residence: current relationship and residence evidence. For F-2: the exact subtype and its extension requirements.

Names, dates, addresses and identification numbers should match. A recent move may require a separate address-change report; do not treat the extension as an automatic cure for unreported changes. If a document contains an error, request correction from the issuer.

## What happens at the appointment

A reservation gets you to the appropriate process, but it does not determine the substantive result. The officer may verify identity, review documents, request additional evidence or process a filing according to the case. Approval, additional-document requests and other outcomes must be distinguished from merely attending.

Bring original identification and the required supporting papers, including appointment confirmation where specified. Arrive according to official instructions rather than assuming the counter will accept a late arrival. Ask how any further-document request affects the case's procedural status and deadline.

After filing, keep the application receipt, case number or system confirmation, as applicable. These records matter if an employer, bank or landlord asks what is happening. They do not justify claiming that approval has been issued before the decision is communicated.

## Pending applications and lawful stay: do not generalize

Immigration procedure can recognize properly filed applications pending decision in ways that differ from a person who never applied. But the exact consequence depends on the type, timing and validity of filing. It would be irresponsible to tell every reader that uploading a file one minute before midnight automatically permits indefinite stay.

Confirm the legally relevant filing date, any defect-correction requirements and whether the current status can continue while the petition is considered. If the deadline has already passed, obtain direct guidance immediately about the person's situation rather than relying on a blog comment.

A bank may ask for an updated residence document during the same period. A filing receipt may explain the delay; whether it satisfies the bank's separate identity or loan process is institution-specific. Do not confuse the bank's acceptance with immigration approval.

## Changing employers and other facts while awaiting extension

Employment, residence and study categories can have obligations that operate independently of the extension itself. If a job ends or changes, there may be separate permission or reporting requirements. Filing an extension based on employment that has already ended, without proper explanation, can create more than a scheduling problem.

Before filing, check whether the category requires prior approval for a new workplace, how a resignation affects the basis of stay, and whether another status such as job-seeking may be the correct route. An applicant who needs a **change of status** should not automatically book the menu intended only for an **extension of the same status**.

For students, graduation can change the correct immigration pathway. A D-2 holder moving into D-10 is not simply asking for another year of D-2 as if enrollment continued. Select the procedure that matches the actual facts.

## Overseas travel does not solve the booking problem

Some residents assume they can leave Korea briefly and return to reset the situation. Re-entry rules and period-of-stay rules are separate. Leaving with an expiring status may create a different entry problem; a visa waiver does not necessarily restore the prior residence category.

The Ministry's overseas-Korean guide also explains that HiKorea visit reservations may not be available from outside Korea through the ordinary process and notes restrictions on how soon after entry a booking can be made. Do not schedule international travel around an assumption that the appointment system will work the same abroad as it does at home.

Before any trip close to expiry, confirm current re-entry eligibility and immigration status. The companion re-entry permit guide addresses that separate issue.

## A practical calendar example

Suppose your authorized stay ends December 20. A four-month planning window opens around August 20 under the general guidance. In August, identify the exact status-specific documents. In September, request employer or school evidence and verify whether electronic filing is allowed. In October, reserve a suitable appointment if an in-person route is required. In November, reconcile any missing records. By December, the objective should be a valid submission—not the first visit to the booking calendar.

This is a planning illustration, not a statement that every applicant can or should file on August 20. Availability, category-specific restrictions, calendar calculations and the current service rules still control. The earlier preparation matters because the problems are often documentary, not technical.

## Bottom line

A scarce appointment should not decide whether a foreign resident lawfully stays in Korea. The actionable distinction is **reservation versus application**. Use the four-month preparation window, check electronic filing, seek official guidance when slots are unavailable and obtain proof of what was actually submitted.

If you are facing the booking wall, stop treating refresh attempts as immigration progress. Find the lawful filing route for your precise status before the authorized-stay deadline.
