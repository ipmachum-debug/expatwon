A passport gets you an 010 number quickly. SK Telecom states that a foreign
national can open a prepaid phone on a valid passport, and KT runs foreigner
prepaid plans that sign up on a passport alone, with voice and text
allowances on 30-day cycles.

So the number is easy. The two hard questions come afterwards: **how far
that line goes**, and **whether you can keep it** once your registration
card exists.

<p class="hand">A real number. An incomplete record. Those are different problems.</p>

## Two things are both called "SMS verification"

This is where the confusion starts, and it is worth separating properly.

| | What happens | Passport prepaid |
|---|---|---|
| **A code by text** | A service texts a number, you type it back | Works |
| **Phone identity verification** | The carrier's subscriber record — name, date of birth, registration number — is matched against the identity being claimed | May be restricted |

The first only needs a working number. The second needs the carrier to hold
identity information it can vouch for.

```
Receiving SMS  ≠  passing phone identity verification
```

**PASS** is the clearest illustration. Its own guidance is that a foreign
national can use it — provided the line supports phone identity
verification and the app can be registered. Holding a 010 number is not
that condition.

## Treat the passport line as the bridge it is

D-4, D-2 and E-7 arrivals all have the same gap: you are here, the card is
weeks away, and you need to be contactable now.

A passport prepaid line closes that gap properly. What it should not be is
your permanent arrangement.

```
land
  → passport prepaid, 010 number in hand
  → apply for alien registration
  → collect the card
  → ask whether this number can stay
  → re-register the subscriber details under the card, or open a new line
```

It is also temporary by design: tourist SIM and eSIM products run to a
maximum of **90 days from first activation**. Tracked at
[tourist SIM validity](/tracked/tourist-sim-max-validity/).

Which product to buy on arrival, and what to check on your handset before
flying, is in
[eSIM, USIM or prepaid SIM in Korea](/cost-of-living/esim-usim-or-prepaid-sim-in-korea/).

## The first question after the card is not "can I change the name"

It is: **can this number stay, on this product, while the subscriber
details are re-registered against my card?**

Because the answer varies, and the routes are genuinely different things:

- re-registering the passport sign-up under your card
- staying prepaid while updating the identity information
- porting from prepaid to postpaid
- opening a new line entirely

## Why keeping the number matters more than it looks

From your first week, one number quietly accumulates: the school, the
landlord, deliveries, a Kakao account, shopping and delivery apps, the
hospital, the bank's records, every booking you make.

Lose it and you do not just change a contact detail. Some of those services
send one-time codes **to that number**, so recovery after it is gone gets
complicated — and PASS states that a certificate cannot continue to be used
after a number change and has to be reissued.

Keeping the number is easier to manage. It is not a legal requirement, and
if the product structure makes it impossible, the answer is a new number and
an orderly migration — not a fight.

## What to bring, and why prepaid and postpaid differ

Go in person; it is the simplest version. Bring:

- passport
- alien registration card
- the phone and the SIM you are using
- your current number and plan details

Carriers may ask for more. SK Telecom's guidance for a foreign national
opening a new line in person lists the **registration card**. KT's terms
distinguish by product: for foreigners, **prepaid takes a passport or a
registration card, postpaid takes the registration card**.

That is the whole structure in one line — the identity document a carrier
will accept changes at exactly the point the card arrives, which is why
this is the moment to sort the line out rather than months later.

A general retail assistant may rarely handle all five. So a flat "foreigners
cannot change the account holder" is a reason to **call the carrier's
foreign customer service** — KT runs one with English, Chinese, Vietnamese
and Russian support — not a reason to open a new line on the spot.

Carrier-operated stores, and official dealers in university or
international districts, are usually easier. And **MVNOs set their own
procedures**, so an answer that is true at an MNO may not apply to one.

## Check the name, on the screen

Verification failures often trace back to the name rather than the line.

A passport shows SURNAME and GIVEN NAME. Carrier systems can end up with a
different order, different spacing, or a middle name handled differently.
When the romanised name on your card, at the carrier, at the bank and at
the card company disagree, verification breaks.

So when the line is re-registered, check three against each other:

```
passport romanisation  →  registration card  →  carrier subscriber name
```

And check it **on the screen or the contract**, not by being told it is
done.

## Do not over-top-up before the card arrives

This is the part almost no SIM guide mentions.

KT states that when you port from its prepaid to its postpaid, **the
remaining prepaid balance is neither carried over nor refunded.**

So if your card is two weeks away, a 90-day top-up is not thrift — it is
money you will probably never see. Top up to the changeover you expect,
and no further.

## A passport line left alone will close itself

Prepaid needs managing in a way postpaid does not.

KT's foreigner prepaid terms describe outgoing and incoming service being
suspended after the balance runs out or the validity or permitted stay
expires, followed by automatic termination — and service being suspended
past a point if identity confirmation is not completed.

SK Telecom's passport-opened prepaid customers face restrictions on further
top-ups when the permitted stay expires, and extend the period by attending
a store or branch with the passport for identity confirmation.

Which is the practical argument for not postponing the switch: **the number
you are trying to keep can be terminated while you delay.**

## The order that works

| Stage | What happens |
|---|---|
| On arrival | Passport prepaid line opened |
| First weeks | Calls, texts, data |
| Registration applied for | Keep the number |
| Card collected | Ask the carrier whether the number can stay |
| Store visit | Passport, card, current phone and line |
| Details re-registered | Subscriber information under the card |
| Long-term line chosen | Stay prepaid, or move to MNO/MVNO postpaid |
| Confirm | Test phone identity verification, and PASS |
| Follow up | Update the number at the bank, school, employer and apps |

The important step is not buying a SIM. It is **deciding how the number you
already use connects to your new registration details.**

## If a new number is unavoidable, migrate before you cancel

Do not cancel the old line first.

Where you can, run both for a while and move your registrations across:

In Korea a phone number is an account recovery mechanism as much as a
contact detail. Cancelling first and updating afterwards is the order that
locks people out.

## Two stages, planned as two stages

Before flying: handset compatibility and carrier lock. On landing: a
passport prepaid line, so there is no gap. Then registration, the card, the
question about the number, the re-registration, and a verification test on
something real.

The difference between a passport 010 and a card-registered 010 is not
visible in the digits. It is in what the carrier's system knows about who
holds it — and until that is sorted, the banking and administrative half of
living in Korea stays shut.
