**Eighty points is a threshold, not an invitation to add numbers from the wrong table.**

If you search for the Korean F-2-7 residence status in English, you can find point tables that disagree about almost everything except the words “80 points.” Some assign far more weight to degrees, others to income. Some describe 120 total points; others describe 170. Some suggest completing a Korean course always produces a separate bonus in addition to language points. An applicant who mixes those tables can be confident of qualifying on paper and still be missing the legal basis for an application.

The first task is therefore not calculating your age or adding your TOPIK score. It is identifying **which official regulation was in force when your application is filed** and **which F-2-7 applicant category you belong to**.

For applications under the presently published rule, the primary legal source is the Ministry of Justice notice titled *출입국관리법시행령 별표 1의2 중 24. 거주(F-2)자목에 해당하는 자의 연령, 학력, 소득 등에 관한 기준 고시*, Notice No. **2025-408**, effective **October 24, 2025**. Article 3 requires **at least 80 points** under its Annex 2. The notice's transitional provision says applications received before its effective date follow the former rule. The current scoring framework is described as up to **170 points** including applicable bonuses, not the older 120-point table still published on some government education pages.

Official legal notice: [National Law Information Center, MOJ Notice 2025-408](https://law.go.kr/LSW/admRulLsInfoP.do?admRulSeq=2100000266296).

## Step zero: 80 points does not establish basic eligibility

The official notice defines applicant categories before the scoring requirement. This is the crucial detail missing from many calculators: a person may earn enough theoretical points while not meeting the conditions for any applicable category.

The notice identifies several groups, including qualifying listed-company personnel, workers in designated promising industries, specified professional-status residents, certain graduates of Korean master's-degree programs who secure eligible employment, and potential high-level talent from designated science and technology institutions. Each group has its own threshold conditions.

For example, the **professional worker** route in the notice requires a qualifying status and generally **three years of continuous lawful residence** in the specified professional categories, subject to the exact regulation and current immigration manual. Certain E-7 subcategories are expressly excluded from that professional route. Do not assume every person who sees E-7 printed on a card can use the same F-2-7 pathway.

The Korean master's-degree graduate route is separate. The notice discusses graduates who obtain an eligible Korean degree and secure qualifying professional employment within a specified period. A government Study in Korea page describes a student-to-residence pathway but still displays an older points grid; its broad description of that pathway should not replace the current notice and annex.

Ask first: “Which paragraph of Article 2 describes me?” Only then calculate points. This prevents a good-looking score from obscuring an application category you cannot establish.

## What the current scoring architecture measures

The revised framework groups core scoring around **age, education, basic competency and annual income**, then applies qualifying bonuses and deductions. Public explanations of the current notice identify maximum core allocations of **25 for age**, **25 for education**, **20 for language/basic competency**, and **60 for annual income**—a **130-point core**, with up to **40 additional bonus points** under the applicable schedule.

| Core element | Maximum under the current framework | Evidence to identify before scoring |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Age | 25 | Passport/date of birth at assessment |
| Education | 25 | Highest eligible credentials, field and verification |
| Korean/basic competency | 20 | TOPIK or KIIP evidence under the notice |
| Annual income | 60 | Acceptable income documentation and relevant tax period |
| Applicable bonus points | Up to 40 | Qualifying items and documentary proof |
| **Threshold** | **At least 80 after applicable rules** | Legal eligibility and disqualifications still apply |

The table above describes structure, **not every individual bracket**. For the exact point score assigned to your age, degree combination, language level and income range, use the live Annex 2 of Notice 2025-408. Older ministry or university brochures cannot safely replace the appendix merely because they have an official logo.

## Age: the date of application can change the answer

Age scoring is intended to reward particular stages of working life. The exact bracket comes from the official schedule; it is not something to estimate from a blog's rounded age categories. A birthday near an immigration filing date may change the assigned points. Calculate using the legally relevant date and the passport information the authority uses.

Consider an applicant who sits only one point above the threshold in a spreadsheet. If they enter a different age bracket by the time documents are submitted, the result may no longer pass. This is a reason to re-run the score immediately before filing—not a reason to rush an incomplete application.

There is a second timing issue. Score tables change. If you saved a spreadsheet a year ago, its age or education weighting may be from another regime. Write the notice number and effective date at the top of the calculation so the source is traceable.

## Education: prove what the regulation recognizes

A degree is not just a line on a CV. The scoring schedule can distinguish degree level, subject area and particular combinations of credentials. The exact current annex determines which category applies. Overseas-issued documents may require an apostille or consular confirmation and translation under current immigration-document practice.

Do not add points for each degree unless the official rules specifically allow it. Two bachelor's degrees do not necessarily equal the sum of two bachelor's scores. Likewise, a science/engineering designation must match the authority's criteria, not the applicant's informal description of a mixed-major program.

Prepare the actual degree certificate and supporting verification before relying on the score. A provisional transcript can be useful in some applications, but it is not necessarily interchangeable with an awarded qualification. If graduation is pending, confirm how the specific eligible route handles anticipated degrees.

## Korean language and KIIP: avoid double counting

Applicants commonly have a TOPIK score, a Korea Immigration & Integration Program (KIIP) completion record, or both. The scoring question is whether these credentials occupy the same basic-competency category, whether the schedule chooses the stronger result and whether a **separate** KIIP-related bonus is available.

Under the current explanations of Notice 2025-408, the core language/basic-competency item is capped at **20 points**. TOPIK and KIIP are alternative evidence in that capped category; possessing both does not mean earning two sets of base language points. The schedule may also award a distinct KIIP completion bonus when the specified conditions are satisfied. That should be counted only in the bonus section and only with the required completion proof.

A person with TOPIK Level 5 cannot simply add “20 for TOPIK” and “20 for KIIP” to claim forty language points. The correct calculation depends on two legally defined line items rather than the number of certificates held.

For an applicant who is short of the threshold, the right strategy may be improving language competency or completing KIIP, but only after verifying the score increase available under the current annex. Never pay for a course based solely on an outdated blog chart.

## Income: the most consequential document may arrive late

Income carries the highest maximum weight among the core categories in the revised framework. That makes its definition and timing central. The relevant measure is not automatically the salary in an offer letter, the gross amount transferred into your bank, or the revenue of the company you own.

Immigration may require income evidence issued by the National Tax Service, such as **소득금액증명**, assessed for the period and category specified in the current regulation and manual. The applicant must establish what income the notice permits, whose income is counted, the relevant tax year and whether the declared amount meets the exact bracket.

Someone who starts a high-paying job in September may expect the next month's payslip to increase the F-2-7 score. That is not a safe inference. If the score uses an already reported annual-income measure, the timing of tax documentation can control when the new income is recognized.

For freelance or business income, deposits and invoices cannot simply be placed into the salary bracket. Reported income and legal form matter. Company revenue belongs to the company; a director's personal immigration income claim must be based on the evidence allowed for the applicant.

## Bonuses are not a free second scorecard

The current scheme includes limited bonus items. Candidate categories described in recent summaries include specified Korean educational or integration achievements and qualifying public-interest activities. The point allowance, time period and proof vary by item. A useful working practice is to document each bonus as a separate line: legal provision, activity, period, issuing authority and claimed point amount.

Volunteering is a good example of why precision matters. “I volunteered many times” is not evidence that an activity meets the current required conditions. Records may need to establish an eligible organization, number of sessions, hours and time span. Requirements that applied to an earlier version may have been rewritten.

The same applies to universities or recommendations. Do not assume a school listed in a commercial ranking today satisfies the immigration regulation's eligible-ranking window. When a bonus refers to a particular list or period, reproduce the criteria from the official appendix rather than the school's promotional materials.

## Deductions and disqualifications are a separate decision

A theoretical eighty-five points can be irrelevant if the applicant has a legal disqualification. Article 4 of Notice 2025-408 sets out grounds including certain criminal sentences, specified immigration violations and fines, submission of false documents and other security or public-interest concerns. These are not merely a few negative points to subtract at the end.

The notice specifically refers to categories of serious convictions and a **₩3 million-or-more fine within three years** among its disqualifying grounds, subject to the exact provision. Anyone with a relevant history should obtain qualified immigration advice and review the primary law before relying on a calculator. A generic online point score cannot adjudicate those legal facts.

There may also be penalty points under the annex for certain immigration or criminal records. Treat **scoring deductions** and **application-disqualifying grounds** as two different checks. Passing one does not waive the other.

## Work an example without pretending the score is approved

Imagine a professional applicant who has correctly established an Article 2 eligibility route. Their current documents, checked against the official annex, yield the following hypothetical worksheet:

| Component | Illustrative result |
| --- | ---: |
| Age | 20 |
| Education | 17 |
| Language/basic competency | 15 |
| Annual income | 30 |
| Supported bonus | 0 |
| Supported deduction | 0 |
| **Illustrative total** | **82** |

This worksheet shows **arithmetic only**. It does not assert that the specific person's age, degree or earnings would in fact receive those scores under a verified annex. The applicant must match each value to the actual schedule and documentary evidence. At 82, even a small scoring error could matter, so the file deserves a fresh check immediately before submission.

Now imagine a second applicant with excellent language results and a higher degree but no documented income under the applicable rules. Their personal self-assessment may still appear impressive, yet the missing income evidence or basic route requirement may be decisive. Scores do not excuse missing eligibility conditions.

## Build an application file that an officer can audit

Prepare a one-page claim sheet with six columns: claimed score, official annex line, evidence, issuing body, document date and any translation or authentication required. Attach the passport and valid residence documents, application form, qualifying employment material and relevant tax/education/language records as directed by the current immigration manual.

Use the exact legal names and dates across documents. If a foreign degree contains a different name spelling, include the lawful supporting explanation. Do not assume every foreign document can be submitted as an English photocopy without additional formalities.

Before filing, confirm the route and document list through **HiKorea** or the **1345 Immigration Contact Center**. Ask specifically about the current version of the residence-status manual and the Annex 2 scoring criteria. The published notice governs the architecture; the operational manual explains submission and review requirements.

## F-2-7 is not the same as permanent residency

F-2-7 is a residence status. F-5 is permanent residence. A person who obtains F-2-7 has not automatically qualified for every F-5 category, and the time credited toward a particular permanent-resident route depends on the applicable rules.

Plan the transition as a separate project: identify the F-5 subtype, count only the qualifying years and establish good conduct, economic-support and basic-knowledge requirements as applicable. See Korea F-5 Permanent Residency: Who Actually Qualifies in this series for that next decision.

## The outdated official-page trap

One surprising detail is that an apparently authoritative web page can be obsolete. Korea's government-run **Study in Korea** employment-system page still displays a **120-point** F-2-7 framework in its explanatory table, whereas the more recent MOJ Notice 2025-408 establishes the controlling current route and 80-point criterion under its newer annex.

This is not permission to dismiss government sources. It is a reminder that a **later regulation with an effective date** should be checked against undated or outdated overview pages. Never mix an older age score with a new income score and publish the combined number as “2026 points.”

## Bottom line

F-2-7 is not a shopping list where you collect eighty points in any combination. First establish the exact qualifying route. Then use the current legal annex, count the correct income and credentials, apply supported bonuses or deductions, and check disqualifying grounds separately.

The winning file is not the spreadsheet with the largest total. It is the file where every point can be traced to **the correct law and a real document**.
