Departure is an immigration date. It is not a tax date, a pension date or a
payment date, and treating it as all four is how people leave money in
Korea.

Some of what is owed to you arrives weeks after you fly. Some of what you
owe is due **the day before**.

<p class="hand">The real end date is when the last won arrives. That is usually after you have left.</p>

## There is no single "tax clearance" for leaving

A recurring assumption is that every departing foreign worker collects some
certificate from the tax office before they can fly. That is not how this
works.

What actually exists are four separate procedures, and which apply to you
depends on your income:

| | Who handles it |
|---|---|
| Mid-year settlement on final salary | Your employer |
| Global income tax return before departure | You, if you have the obligation |
| Unclaimed refunds | Employer, tax office, or local authority |
| Tax agent appointment | You, before you go |

So the first question is not "what do I need to get stamped". It is
**whether you have a filing obligation at all**:

```
Employment income only        →  the employer settles it
Business, rental, multiple    →  your own return, and a hard deadline
employments, other global
income
```

## The employer settles employment income with your final pay

Leave mid-year and your employer normally runs a **mid-year settlement**
against that year's employment income when it pays you for the last time.
Over-withheld tax is refunded; under-withheld tax comes out of the final
payment.

Before you leave, secure three documents:

<div class="steps">
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">1</div>
    <div class="step-body">Final payslip</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">2</div>
    <div class="step-body">Withholding tax receipt for employment income</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">3</div>
    <div class="step-body">Severance pay statement</div></div>
</div>

If annual deductions — medical, donations, card spending — were not fully
reflected at that point, a further refund can be pursued through a return
or a claim for correction later. Which is a reason to keep the paperwork
rather than a reason to delay leaving. How those deductions work is in
[year-end settlement for foreign employees](/business/year-end-settlement-for-foreign-employees/).

## If you must file, it is due the day before you fly

<div class="callout callout-warn">
  <p class="callout-title">Not the following May. Not the month after you go</p>
  <p>A resident with a global income filing obligation who is leaving to
  relocate their domicile or residence abroad files and pays for that tax
  period <strong>by the day before departure</strong>. "I leave in September,
  so I will file next May" is not available to that person. Tracked at
  <a href="/tracked/departure-income-tax-filing-deadline/">the departure filing deadline</a>.</p>
</div>

This catches people with business income, rental income, several
employments or other global income. It does **not** need applying
mechanically to someone whose single employment was settled by their
employer — and misapplying it in that direction wastes a week of everyone's
time.

Establish which group you are in first. If you are unsure, that uncertainty
itself is a reason to ask before booking the flight rather than after.

## The pension refund is not "you paid in, you get it back"

Contributing does not create an entitlement to a lump-sum refund. Three
routes exist, and two of them turn on nationality:

<div class="steps">
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">1</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>Reciprocity</strong> — your country pays equivalent benefits to Korean nationals.</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">2</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>Social security agreement</strong> — Korea's agreement with your country provides for the refund.</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">3</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>Status of stay</strong> — periods contributed on <strong>E-8, E-9 or H-2</strong> qualify regardless of nationality.</div></div>
</div>

On the current list, **24 countries** qualify by agreement — the United
States, Canada, Germany, Australia, France, India, Türkiye and the
Philippines among them — and **26 by reciprocity**, including Thailand,
Malaysia, Indonesia and Kazakhstan.

<div class="callout callout-warn">
  <p class="callout-title">China is not on the current list</p>
  <p>Which is worth stating plainly, because a great many workers in Korea
  hold Chinese nationality and a great many blog posts imply the refund is
  universal. Chinese nationality alone does not establish entitlement — the
  E-8, E-9 and H-2 route is separate and applies regardless. Minimum
  contribution conditions also differ by country, so check the current list
  at <a href="/tracked/nps-lump-sum-refund-country-list/">countries paying the refund</a>
  and confirm with the service rather than from an old table.</p>
</div>

### Applying before you go

Where you qualify, apply at an NPS branch before departure. Typically:

- passport
- alien registration card
- a flight ticket showing the departure date
- the claim form
- bank account details

For an overseas transfer you need the receiving bank's name, the account
number and the SWIFT or BIC code, accurately. Additional documents vary by
country and by claim route, so confirm at the branch.

## Airport cash exists, and it is not applied for at the airport

The pension service will pay a qualifying refund in foreign currency cash at
Incheon on the day you fly. It is genuinely useful and it is widely
misunderstood.

```
apply at an NPS branch, within a month of departure
  → select airport payment
  → former employer files loss of coverage by the day before you fly
  → collect at the airport counter
```

<div class="callout callout-warn">
  <p class="callout-title">Two conditions you do not control</p>
  <p>Your <strong>former employer</strong> must complete the loss-of-coverage
  report by the day before departure — a deadline you cannot see and they may
  not be tracking. And the service runs on weekdays with conditions on flight
  times, so a weekend or very late departure cannot use it.</p>
  <p>Anyone flying the day after their last working day should confirm the
  employer's filing explicitly. Tracked at
  <a href="/tracked/nps-airport-refund-lead-time/">the airport refund lead time</a>.</p>
</div>

## "My refund" is three different refunds

Money owed to a departing employee comes from three payers on three
timetables.

| Refund | Paid by |
|---|---|
| Mid-year settlement over-withholding | Your employer |
| Global income tax return or claim for correction | The National Tax Service |
| Local income tax | The local authority |

So nothing showing in Hometax does not mean your employer owes you nothing.
Before leaving, check **both** the withholding receipt from your company and
your filing and refund record with the tax office. They are different
systems and neither knows about the other.

## Close the bank account last

The instinct to tidy up before flying is the wrong one here.

Still to arrive, often after departure:

- final salary
- severance pay
- tax refunds
- the pension refund
- your rental deposit

The tax office lets you register an account to receive refunds, and the
taxpayer support centre at Incheon handles account-registration business.
Where keeping a Korean account is genuinely impossible, ask **each payer
separately** whether it can remit overseas — the pension service can, and
that is not the same procedure as a tax refund.

How a deposit comes back, and why it often lands after you have gone, is in
[protecting a rental deposit in Korea](/cost-of-living/rental-deposit-protection-korea/).

<div class="callout callout-note">
  <p class="callout-title">Keep a way to log in, too</p>
  <p>An account you cannot access is nearly as unhelpful as no account.
  Cancelling your Korean number and your certificates on the way out leaves
  you unable to check Hometax, confirm a transfer or deal with your former
  employer. Keep something working for a few months. What breaks without a
  verifiable number is in
  <a href="/cost-of-living/what-breaks-without-a-korean-phone-number/">what breaks without a Korean phone number</a>.</p>
</div>

## If anything is still open, appoint a tax agent

The Framework Act on National Taxes requires a taxpayer who has no domicile
or residence in Korea, or who is relocating theirs abroad, to appoint a
**tax agent** to handle national tax matters.

This is not a forwarding address. A tax agent can file returns, make
applications and claims, receive documents from the tax office, pay tax
**and receive refunds** on your behalf.

Which makes it the practical answer for anyone leaving with a return still
to file, a claim for correction outstanding, a refund not yet paid, or
Korean property or business filings continuing.

Appointing one before you go is far easier than arranging it from abroad
once the tax office has already written to an address you no longer live at.

## A timetable that works

| When | What |
|---|---|
| D-30 | Check pension refund eligibility — country and status route |
| D-21 | Confirm final salary, severance and tax settlement dates with HR |
| D-14 | Apply for the pension refund; decide the receiving account |
| D-7 | Establish whether a pre-departure tax return is required |
| D-3 | Collect withholding receipt, severance statement, insurance loss-of-coverage confirmations |
| D-1 | File and pay if required; confirm the employer's loss-of-coverage filing |
| After | Track every payment in; keep the account and login alive until they land |

If you were on the 19% flat rate, check its application state and period at
the final settlement as well —
[the 19% flat rate: when it beats the brackets](/business/the-19-percent-flat-rate-when-it-wins/).

## The date that actually matters

```
resignation
  → employer's mid-year settlement
  → is a pre-departure return required?
  → pension refund application
  → national and local refunds identified
  → tax agent appointed if anything remains
  → receiving account kept open
  → departure
  → payments confirmed
```

Immigration is finished with you at the gate. Tax and pension are not.

The real **leaving settlement date** is the day the last of your salary,
severance, refunds and pension has arrived and no filing obligation remains
— and for most people that is somewhere between a month and a year after
the flight.
