There are two lawful ways to reduce what a first hire costs, and neither
involves paying anyone less.

One is a national subsidy built for precisely this situation and routinely
missed. The other is designing the pay package so that a component which is not
taxed is actually structured as one.

<p class="hand">The cheap version of both of these is the one that gets unwound later.</p>

## The subsidy is designed for a first hire

Support covers **80% of national pension and employment insurance contributions
— both the employer's and the employee's** — for up to **36 months**, at
businesses with fewer than ten employees, for employees newly enrolled and
earning under
[₩2,700,000 a month](/tracked/small-employer-insurance-subsidy-threshold/).

"Newly enrolled" generally means no national pension or employment insurance
record in the preceding year.

<div class="callout callout-warn">
  <p class="callout-title">It is not "fewer than ten employees, therefore supported"</p>
  <p>Headcount is one gate. Pay level, newness of enrolment, the support period,
  and the employee's own property and income are others: support is excluded where
  the employee's prior-year property tax base reaches ₩600,000,000 or global income
  reaches ₩43,000,000. Several of those are facts about the employee that you will
  not know at the offer stage.</p>
</div>

## The ceiling and the calculation base are different numbers

Eligibility runs to under ₩2,700,000 a month. But support for anyone earning
**₩2,300,000 or more** is calculated on a ₩2,300,000 base.

```
eligible up to    ₩2,700,000
calculated on     ₩2,300,000 where pay is at or above it
```

So the value tapers rather than scaling with salary, and the difference matters
when comparing two candidates on either side of it.

## What it does to a ₩2,500,000 hire

From the costing in the morning guide, a ₩2,500,000 salary carries about
₩285,935 of employer social insurance and ₩208,333 of retirement accrual — about
₩2,994,268 a month.

Where every condition is met, the employer support works out at up to about
₩108,560 a month on the official example basis:

```
monthly total          ₩2,994,268
employer support         −₩108,560
                        ──────────
                       ₩2,885,708
```

Roughly ₩1,300,000 a year, on a hire at this level — and it also reduces the
employee's own pension and employment insurance deductions, which is a real
improvement in their take-home that costs you nothing.

<div class="callout callout-note">
  <p class="callout-title">₩3,000,000 and ₩4,000,000 hires are outside it</p>
  <p>Both are above the pay ceiling, so the ordinary support does not apply. This
  is worth knowing before setting a salary at ₩2,750,000: a small increase can
  cost the business more than it gives the employee.</p>
</div>

## Do not budget the subsidy before it is approved

The conditions include facts about the employee's history, property and income
that you cannot verify yourself.

<div class="callout callout-warn">
  <p class="callout-title">Budget at the unsupported figure</p>
  <p>Plan the hire at the full cost, apply, and treat approval as a reduction when
  it arrives. A business that has already spent the subsidy in its cash flow and
  then fails a condition has a shortfall in the month it can least absorb one.</p>
</div>

## The meal allowance, done properly

Where the conditions are met — including the employer not also providing meals —
a meal allowance of up to **₩200,000 a month** can be outside taxable employment
income. The national pension also excludes the non-taxable meal allowance from
standard monthly income.

So the same total package can produce better take-home than a straight salary
increase, and slightly reduce some contribution bases.

<div class="steps">
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">1</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>Write it into the contract or pay rules</strong> as a defined component, not as an informal arrangement.</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">2</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>Actually pay it.</strong> A line on a payslip for money that does not move is not an allowance.</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">3</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>Do not also provide meals</strong> and treat the allowance as exempt at the same time.</div></div>
</div>

<div class="callout callout-warn">
  <p class="callout-title">"Non-taxable" printed on a payslip is a claim, not a fact</p>
  <p>The exemption depends on the statutory conditions being satisfied, and it is
  the employee's tax position that is affected when they are not. Splitting a
  salary on paper to manufacture an exempt component is the version of this that
  gets unwound, with the employee carrying the consequence. How these lines read
  from the other side is in
  <a href="/employment/reading-your-korean-payslip/">reading your Korean payslip</a>.</p>
</div>

## Two things that are not cost reduction

**Understating the reported wage.** The wage reported to the insurance schemes
is not a dial. It is reconciled, and the correction arrives with the gap owed.

**Calling an employee something else.** A contractor arrangement over work that
is really employment does not remove the obligations; it defers them until
someone examines the relationship, at which point they arrive together and
backdated. What the contract has to contain either way is in
[your Korean employment contract](/employment/your-korean-employment-contract-what-to-check/).

## The order that works

<div class="steps">
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">1</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>Cost the hire in full</strong>, at roughly 120% of gross plus everything outside it.</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">2</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>Check the subsidy conditions</strong> against the actual candidate, including pay level and enrolment history.</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">3</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>Design the package</strong>, including a properly constituted meal allowance if it fits.</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">4</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>Register on time</strong>, on each scheme's own deadline.</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">5</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>Apply for support</strong>, and treat it as a reduction once approved.</div></div>
</div>

The filing deadlines diverge between the four schemes, and missing them costs
more than the penalty suggests —
[registering employees for the four insurances](/employment/registering-employees-for-four-insurances/).

The full cost this is being applied to is in
[what your first employee actually costs](/employment/what-your-first-employee-actually-costs/).
Both levers here are real and both are conditional; the failure mode is not
using them, it is assuming them.
