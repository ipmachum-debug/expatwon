A first year in Korea is not a visa and a flat. It is the construction of an
administrative system in which residence status, stay address, phone-based
identity verification, a bank account, a tenant's opposing power, business
registration, four insurance schemes, VAT and income tax all reference each
other.

Get the order wrong and the failures cascade. A late alien registration makes
identity verification and banking difficult. A missed stay-address report creates
an immigration problem. A tenancy without possession and the address filing has
weak deposit protection. A business trading before its registration and
bookkeeping exist makes the following year's tax settlement expensive to
reconstruct.

The order that holds is: **arrival → identity → address → mobile → banking →
lease protection → business → employment → tax settlement.**

## 1. Arrival to month 1: settle identity and address first

A foreign national intending to stay longer than 90 days must in principle
complete **alien registration**
[within 90 days of entry](/tracked/alien-registration-deadline/). Where status is
granted or changed inside Korea, a separate registration point can apply.

Read that as a ceiling. Appointment availability and document preparation both
run ahead of it, and everything downstream is blocked until the registration
number exists — so a long-stay arrival should be booking and assembling documents
in the first weeks, not planning around day 89.

If you have already registered and then move, the rule changes. Under article 36
of the Immigration Act you report the change of stay address
[within 15 days](/tracked/foreigner-address-change-deadline/) of moving in, at the
si/gun/gu, eup/myeon/dong office for the new address or the competent immigration
office. A document evidencing the actual residence, usually the lease, may be
required.

<div class="callout callout-warn">
  <p class="callout-title">The address filing is not only immigration admin</p>
  <p>Opposing power under the Housing Lease Protection Act rests on delivery of
  the housing and resident registration, and a foreign national's alien
  registration and stay-address report have been recognised as performing the
  equivalent function. So the address filing is simultaneously an immigration
  obligation and the thing protecting your deposit. Move in, file the address, and
  obtain the fixed date as close together as possible —
  <a href="/cost-of-living/lease-clauses-that-actually-protect-a-tenant/">the contract-stage detail is here</a>.</p>
</div>

Once the registration number and long-stay status exist, build a domestic mobile
number as an identity verification method. Before that, a foreign credit card and
roaming will bridge the gap — but banking apps, delivery, shopping and most
public and private services all assume domestic verification, so it is a bridge
rather than an arrangement.

Which operators actually work for a foreign resident is in
[the MVNO deep dive](/cost-of-living/choosing-an-mvno-that-will-verify-you/),
and the interim card question in
[paying in Korea with a foreign card](/banking/paying-in-korea-with-a-foreign-card/).

## 2. Months 2 to 3: separate the money before there is much of it

With registration and verification in place, build the banking layer — and build
it already separated. Personal living expenses and business funds do not share an
account.

Banks apply their own checks to foreign customers, and a new account is often
opened with transaction limits. Lifting them means evidencing a genuine purpose:
salary transfers, an employment certificate, business contracts, tax invoices. It
is a review, not a form.

If you are actually trading, business registration has its own clock: applied for
at each place of business
[within 20 days of commencing business](/tracked/business-registration-application-deadline/),
with advance registration also available — usually the better course where costs
are being incurred before the first sale.

<div class="callout callout-warn">
  <p class="callout-title">The business-use account report is not a startup task</p>
  <p>The Income Tax Act obligation to report and use a business-use account
  applies to <strong>double-entry bookkeeping obligors</strong>, with the deadline
  set by reference to the start of the taxable period in which the obligation
  arises. Registering a business does not create it. Separate the accounts from
  day one as practice; test the statutory obligation against your industry,
  revenue and bookkeeping category separately —
  <a href="/business/business-account-vs-personal-account-in-korea/">the two are different things with the same name</a>.</p>
</div>

Home and office internet belongs here too, and the question at signup is not
installation but the term, whether the line can be relocated, and what early
termination costs — [the contract traps](/cost-of-living/korean-home-internet-the-36-month-total/)
outlive the installation by three years.

## 3. Months 4 to 6: employment, insurance and the residency question

A first hire is not a salary calculation. The employment contract, withholding
tax, retirement benefits and the four insurance schemes are one labour cost
structure — [what a first employee actually costs](/employment/what-your-first-employee-actually-costs/).

And the deadlines do not agree with each other.

| Scheme | Deadline |
|---|---|
| Health insurance, workplace application | 14 days from becoming subject to employee coverage |
| Health insurance, employee acquisition | Generally 14 days from the acquisition date |
| Employment insurance, acquisition of insured status | [15th of the following month](/tracked/employment-insurance-acquisition-report-deadline/) |

"All four within 14 days" is the version everyone repeats and it is not accurate.
The practical answer is not to memorise the divergence but to **file all four
together immediately after the hire** — that satisfies the earliest deadline and
makes the rest moot.

Dependants are a separate test again. Family membership does not produce
dependant status by itself: income and property conditions apply, so a family
member with foreign income or Korean property needs checking against
[the income and property tests](/insurance/nhis-dependants-the-income-and-property-tests/).

This is also the point to establish your own **tax residency**. It is not decided
by visa category. Address in Korea, occupation, family, assets and duration of
stay are weighed together, with
[183 days](/tracked/tax-residency-day-threshold/) an important element rather than
the test itself. Anyone with foreign income should reach a view around the
six-month mark and read the applicable treaty alongside the domestic rule — not
discover the question in May.

<div class="callout callout-note">
  <p class="callout-title">VAT is not one calendar</p>
  <p>A company generally files preliminary returns in April and October and final
  returns in July and the following January. An individual general taxpayer
  generally files final returns in July and the following January, and individual
  general taxpayers along with certain small companies can instead receive
  preliminary assessment notices in April and October. "Everyone makes an interim
  payment in July" describes neither structure.</p>
</div>

## 4. Months 7 to 12: close the year as data

The second half is less about filings than about what will exist in December.
Payroll records, withholding, business expenditure, rent, overseas remittances,
asset acquisitions and sales and purchase tax invoices organised monthly are what
make the following year's returns a transcription rather than an investigation.

The year-end filings themselves:

| Filing | Deadline |
|---|---|
| VAT, second final return | Generally 25 January |
| Corporate tax, December year-end company | Generally by the end of March |
| Global income tax, sole proprietor | 1–31 May |

Housing gets recalculated at the twelve-month mark too. Extend, keep renting, or
buy is a capital allocation question with a holding period attached, not a
forecast — run it through
[the break-even model](/cost-of-living/buying-vs-renting-the-real-break-even/)
rather than the instinct that rent is wasted.

If you move, the fifteen-day stay-address deadline applies again, alongside the
deposit recovery sequence —
[ending a Korean lease](/cost-of-living/ending-a-korean-lease-the-notice-that-has-to-arrive/)
and [the moving admin](/cost-of-living/moving-in-korea-the-filings-and-their-deadlines/).
If you wind up a business instead, that has its own order —
[closing a business in Korea](/business/closing-a-business-in-korea-final-vat-and-stock/).

## The twelve-month map

| When | Milestone | What goes wrong if missed |
|---|---|---|
| Arrival – 30 days | Documents, address, lease, mobile line | Identity verification, banking and deposit protection all delayed |
| Within 90 days | Alien registration, long-stay | Breach of a statutory immigration deadline |
| Within 15 days of a move | Stay-address change report | A criminal fine, and mismatched address records |
| Months 2–3 | Personal and business accounts, business registration | Mixed funds, missing tax evidence |
| Months 4–6 | Hiring, four insurances, residency determination | Backdated premiums, withholding errors, foreign income exposure |
| July and January | VAT final returns | Non-filing and late-payment penalties |
| Year-end – March | Company accounts and corporate tax | Closing and tax adjustment errors |
| Following May | Global income tax | Errors in aggregating domestic and foreign income |

A first year in Korea is not a twelve-month administrative calendar. It is a
sequence: **establish identity, attach an address to it, connect banking and
contracts, then close employment and tax against both.**

With that order in place, each of the procedures below stops being a separate
problem and becomes one settlement process.
