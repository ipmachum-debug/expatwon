Korea's tax refund is not duty free. A duty free shop sells to you without the
tax in the price; a tax refund shop sells at the ordinary price and gives part
of it back **on the condition that the goods leave the country**.

That condition is what makes the rest of it complicated — and it is also why
the amount is smaller than most people expect.

## Who is actually eligible

The scheme is for **non-resident foreign tourists**, and for foreign nationals
the stated basis is a stay in Korea of
[under six months](/tracked/tourist-tax-refund-stay-period/). Korean nationals
resident abroad for two years or more can qualify on a temporary return where
the conditions are met.

<div class="callout callout-warn">
  <p class="callout-title">The residence card is not the test — in either direction</p>
  <p>"I registered as a foreign resident, so I am out" is not right, and neither
  is "I hold a foreign passport, so I am in." What matters is whether you meet
  the non-resident condition and the stay period. Merchants check passport
  details at the till, which is where the question is decided in practice — so a
  long-stayer who has been claiming by habit should find out where they actually
  stand.</p>
</div>

## The threshold is per purchase

The minimum is [₩15,000](/tracked/tax-refund-minimum-purchase/) in a **single
qualifying purchase at a single licensed merchant**.

```
₩8,000 at shop A + ₩8,000 at shop B  →  no refund
₩16,000 at shop A                     →  refund
```

Two things follow. The goods must leave Korea, with departure within
[three months](/tracked/tax-refund-export-deadline/) of purchase as a rule; and
they should stay new, unopened and unused.

## Three routes, four thresholds

| | Immediate | Downtown | Airport |
|---|---|---|---|
| Minimum purchase | ₩15,000 | ₩15,000 | ₩15,000 |
| Per-purchase ceiling | under ₩1,000,000 | ₩6,000,000 or less | ordinary rules |
| Cumulative per stay | ₩5,000,000 | operator's terms | ordinary process |
| When you get the money | At the till | Before you fly | On departure |
| Customs confirmation | Not a separate airport step | Still required | Required |

### Immediate refund

The merchant checks your passport, deducts the refundable tax and charges you
the reduced amount. On a ₩300,000 purchase at a participating shop, you pay less
at the counter and there is nothing to collect later.

It applies to a purchase of at least ₩15,000 and
[under ₩1,000,000](/tracked/immediate-refund-per-transaction-limit/), and there
is a cumulative ceiling of
[₩5,000,000](/tracked/immediate-refund-total-limit/) across your stay.

<div class="callout callout-note">
  <p class="callout-title">The cumulative cap is the one that ambushes people</p>
  <p>It runs quietly across separate shops and days. Someone who has had the tax
  taken off at the till all week can be told at a counter that this purchase does
  not qualify, for no visible reason — the reason is a running total nobody
  displays. Above either limit the ordinary route applies instead, with a refund
  slip and a customs step.</p>
</div>

Not every refund merchant offers immediate refund. Ask before assuming the till
will handle it.

### Downtown refund

Some operators run staffed counters and unmanned kiosks in city centres. You
generally need your **passport, the refund slip and an international credit
card**, and the money is paid before you fly, for purchases of
[₩6,000,000 or less](/tracked/downtown-refund-per-receipt-limit/).

<div class="callout callout-warn">
  <p class="callout-title">Being paid early does not end the transaction</p>
  <p>The export condition survives: customs confirmation on departure is still
  required. Where that does not happen, the operator can recover what it has
  already paid through the card you registered. How that recovery works — the
  authorisation held, any additional cost, the settlement terms — is set by the
  operator's own terms, not by a single national rule, so read the ones you are
  actually signing.</p>
</div>

### Airport refund

The standard route: buy, keep the refund slip, and settle it on departure after
customs confirms the export. The ₩6,000,000 per-purchase ceiling that applies to
downtown refunds is not applied in the same way here.

The procedure itself — kiosk, customs, checked baggage order — is the subject of
the afternoon guide, and it is where most refunds are actually lost.

## What you actually get back

Korean VAT is 10%, and the trap is what that 10% is a percentage *of*.

The shelf price already contains the tax. So the tax inside a price is
approximately **one eleventh of it**, not a tenth added on top:

```
price paid              ₩110,000
VAT inside it       ≈    ₩10,000   (about 9.1% of the price)
```

On the ordinary post-purchase route, the refund operator's service cost then
comes out of that figure, so the amount reaching you is smaller again.

<div class="callout callout-warn">
  <p class="callout-title">So "price × 10%" is not the refund</p>
  <p>It overstates it twice — once on the arithmetic, once on the fees. Take the
  expected amount from the refund slip or the operator's system, which is the
  figure that will actually be paid.</p>
</div>

## Operators differ, so identical purchases can refund differently

Several refund operators participate — Global Blue and Global Tax Free among
them. They can differ on payment method (cash, international credit card,
mobile), on service cost, and on how currency conversion is handled.

Two purchases of the same amount at two shops using different operators need not
produce the same net refund. Treating the scheme as the state simply handing
back the VAT you paid will not match the numbers.

Whichever route you use, the payment side is worth thinking about too — what a
foreign card does and does not clear in Korean shops is in
[paying in Korea with a foreign card](/banking/paying-in-korea-with-a-foreign-card/).

The thresholds above are current policy rather than fixed features, so check the
year you are travelling in. What decides whether a refund actually arrives,
though, is not the numbers — it is the twenty minutes at the airport, and that is
the other half of this pair.
