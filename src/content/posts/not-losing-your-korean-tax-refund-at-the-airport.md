Very little tax refund money is lost to eligibility rules. It is lost at the
airport, to sequence — a bag checked in too early, a receipt that was never a
refund slip, a kiosk visited after the customs desk instead of before.

## What "the paperwork" means

At a refund merchant, buying [₩15,000](/tracked/tax-refund-minimum-purchase/) or
more, you present your passport and the shop generates the refund record — a
sales confirmation or refund slip, in paper or electronic form — alongside your
purchase receipt.

Incheon Airport Customs asks for the **sales confirmation document**, not an
ordinary card receipt.

<div class="steps">
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">1</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>Confirm the shop is applying the refund</strong> before paying.</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">2</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>Check your passport details went in correctly.</strong> A typo here is discovered at the airport, by which point it is somebody else's system.</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">3</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>Make sure the slip or electronic record exists</strong>, not just the till receipt.</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">4</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>Keep both</strong>, together, somewhere you are not going to pack.</div></div>
</div>

<div class="callout callout-warn">
  <p class="callout-title">This is not recoverable later</p>
  <p>If the record was never created, there is nothing at the airport to process.
  Arriving at departures with a bag of card receipts and an expectation is the
  single most common way this ends badly.</p>
</div>

## Inspection is decided by the system, not by the price

Older guides — and a lot of travel blogs — say that above some won amount
customs will always want to see the goods.

The current procedure does not work that way. You process your passport and
refund details at the **departures kiosk first**, and where the transaction is
flagged for inspection you take the goods, your passport and the sales
confirmation to the **customs desk**.

```
kiosk: passport and refund details
  → flagged for inspection?
      no  → continue
      yes → present goods at the customs desk
```

So the planning rule is not "under this amount I am fine". It is: **be able to
show the items if asked.**

## Which makes checked baggage the whole problem

If your purchases are going into a suitcase, the order is not optional.

<div class="steps">
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">1</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>At check-in, say it.</strong> "Tax refund items are inside." Incheon's guidance is to tell the airline staff at check-in.</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">2</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>They tag the bag and hand it back</strong> rather than sending it down the belt.</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">3</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>Process the kiosk</strong> with your passport and refund details.</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">4</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>If inspection is required</strong>, present the case and the goods at the customs desk and obtain export confirmation.</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">5</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>Then put the bag on the oversized baggage belt</strong> beside the customs area.</div></div>
</div>

<div class="callout callout-warn">
  <p class="callout-title">Once it is on the belt, it is gone</p>
  <p>Customs asks for goods that can be produced. A case already travelling to
  the aircraft cannot be, and the refund for those items goes with it. Incheon
  Airport Customs is explicit that items needing confirmation must be presented
  before the baggage is checked in.</p>
</div>

## Downtown refunds still need the customs step

Being paid in the city before you fly is a convenience, not a completion. The
export condition remains, and customs confirmation on departure is still
required.

Where that does not happen, the operator can recover what it already paid
through the card you registered when you claimed.

<div class="callout callout-note">
  <p class="callout-title">The recovery terms are the operator's, not the state's</p>
  <p>How much is held on the card, what happens if the confirmation is missing,
  and any additional cost are set in that operator's terms — Global Blue, Global
  Tax Free and others differ. Read the ones you signed rather than a general
  description of "how downtown refunds work".</p>
</div>

## Getting paid, and in what

After departure formalities, the refund is collected at a counter or an unmanned
kiosk in the departure area.

**By card.** Incheon notes this as the route where the amount is large or you
would rather not take cash. It avoids carrying or re-exchanging won. The time to
reach your account depends on the operator and your issuer, so an empty statement
the day after your flight means nothing yet.

**In cash.** Payout currency — won, dollars, yen, yuan — depends on the operator
and what the counter holds. Taking a foreign currency means a conversion at that
counter's rate.

<div class="callout callout-note">
  <p class="callout-title">If you are leaving and will not spend won again</p>
  <p>Compare the card refund against the rate offered on foreign-currency cash.
  Won in hand at the gate has to be converted somewhere, and the counter's rate
  is not automatically the best of the available options.</p>
</div>

## The departure sequence

```
passport + refund documents + goods + boarding pass, together
  → check-in: declare refund items in checked baggage
  → bag tagged, returned to you
  → kiosk: scan passport and refund details
  → inspection required? present the goods at customs
  → oversized baggage belt
  → immigration
  → refund counter or kiosk airside
```

Allow real time for it. This is the part of a departure that expands when a
queue forms in front of the customs desk, and it sits before immigration rather
than after.

Not one of those is a question about tax. Who qualifies, which route applies and

The refund is decided at the till and completed at the airport. Everything in
between is just carrying the paperwork to the right desk in the right order.
