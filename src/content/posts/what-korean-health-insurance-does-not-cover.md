Being enrolled in Korean national health insurance does not mean a hospital
visit is settled. A Korean bill has two halves, and you pay part of both.

<p class="hand">Covered and non-covered are different costs, not different discounts.</p>

## Covered treatment still costs you something

Where treatment is a benefit under the Act, the insurer pays a share and you pay
a statutory portion.

That portion is **not one percentage**. It varies across:

- inpatient and outpatient care
- clinics, hospitals, general hospitals and tertiary hospitals
- pharmacies
- the special calculation scheme for serious conditions

<div class="callout callout-warn">
  <p class="callout-title">So "20% inpatient, 30–60% outpatient" is a flattening</p>
  <p>It removes the variable that actually moves your bill — where you were
  treated, not only what for. The same condition handled at a local clinic and at
  a tertiary hospital produces different arithmetic before anyone reaches the
  non-covered items.</p>
</div>

## Non-covered treatment is outside the scheme entirely

Non-covered treatment is excluded from benefits: the insurer contributes
nothing and you pay the whole amount.

Commonly in this territory:

- some manual therapy, extracorporeal shockwave and prolotherapy
- some non-covered injections
- some MRI and ultrasound
- upgraded room charges
- some robotic surgery
- cosmetic and aesthetic procedures
- some newly approved medical technologies

## But "MRI is non-covered" is not a fact about MRI

CT, MRI and ultrasound each have their own benefit criteria, and MRI in
particular turns on the body part, the condition and medical necessity.

The same scan can be covered for one patient and non-covered for another.

```
before a scheduled scan, ask:
  is this covered?
  if not, what does it cost here?
  what is my share either way?
```

Three questions, asked before the appointment rather than at the payment desk.

## Non-covered prices differ between hospitals

Benefit-covered treatment sits inside a managed fee structure. Non-covered
treatment does not — each institution sets its own price, so the same named
procedure can cost materially different amounts.

Which is precisely why prices for a large list of non-covered items are
published **per institution** —
[753 items in 2026](/tracked/hira-disclosed-noncovered-items/).

<div class="callout callout-note">
  <p class="callout-title">For repeatable treatment, price the year</p>
  <p>A course of manual therapy is not one price; it is a session price times a
  number of sessions you have not yet agreed to. Before starting, get the
  treatment name, the per-session price, the expected number of sessions, the
  annual total, and whether your private policy covers it — in that order.</p>
</div>

## Where private insurance stops, in every generation

Indemnity insurance reimburses medical costs actually incurred for the treatment
of illness or injury. Several categories sit outside that by design:

<div class="steps">
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">1</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>Cosmetic surgery and aesthetic procedures</strong>, and routine skin treatment.</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">2</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>Health promotion</strong> — nutritional or fatigue-relief infusions, preventive check-ups.</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">3</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>Normal pregnancy and childbirth</strong>, as a general structure — though a complication or a distinct condition arising during pregnancy can be assessed differently, on the cause and the diagnosis code.</div></div>
</div>

The same injection can fall inside or outside depending on whether it was given
to treat something. That is a judgement on purpose, not on the substance.

<div class="callout callout-note">
  <p class="callout-title">National support and private cover are separate systems</p>
  <p>The state's pregnancy and childbirth medical expense support is not the same
  thing as an indemnity policy, and one saying no tells you nothing about the
  other.</p>
</div>

## Three things people assume are automatically covered

**An upgraded room.** Using a single room generates a room rate difference
against the standard benefit bed. Whether a policy covers it, and up to what
limit, depends on the generation and terms. Ask the ward grade, the expected
nightly difference and the policy limit *before* admission.

**Robotic surgery.** Some robotic procedures are non-covered. Being non-covered
does not make something payable by a private policy — purpose, medical
necessity, exclusions and whether it counts as a newly approved technology all
enter. For an expensive planned procedure, ask the insurer in advance.

**Korean medicine.** Acupuncture and moxibustion can be benefit-covered; some
non-covered herbal preparations and pharmacopuncture may be limited or excluded
under a policy. Neither "Korean medicine is not covered" nor "it is all covered"
is right; it depends on the item and your policy generation.

## The order to work in

```
is this treatment benefit-covered?
  yes → what is my statutory share, in this setting?
  no  → what does this hospital charge, and what is the annual total?
        → does my policy cover it, at what excess and what limit?
```

The last line is doing more work than it looks, because indemnity cover in Korea
is set by the **generation** of your policy rather than by the insurer — and the
generation on sale changed in May 2026. That comparison is the other half of
today's pair.
