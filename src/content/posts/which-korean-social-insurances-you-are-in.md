Two people do the same job at the same company for the same salary. Their
payslips show different deductions. Both are correct.

That is not an anomaly in Korean payroll — it is the predictable result of
four social insurances that each decide who is covered by a **different
test**, only one of which works the way people expect nationality to work.

## National pension: three things at once

Being foreign neither guarantees coverage nor exempts you from it. The
question runs on:

<div class="steps">
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">1</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>Reciprocity.</strong> Whether your country applies an equivalent scheme to Korean nationals.</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">2</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>A social security agreement.</strong> A worker posted from an agreement country who has filed a certificate of coverage at home can be outside Korean coverage.</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">3</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>Status of stay.</strong> Several statuses are excluded outright.</div></div>
</div>

So a missing pension line on a foreign employee's payslip is not evidence of an
error. It is a prompt to check which of the three applies — and to make sure
one of them actually does.

## The refund is not a consequence of having paid

This is the single most consequential misunderstanding for anyone whose Korean
stay is finite.

Contributions do not convert into a departure payment because they exist. A
lump-sum refund requires one of three routes: your country pays equivalent
benefits to Korean nationals, a social security agreement provides for the
refund, or your status of stay is one the law specifies.

Across the two nationality routes the qualifying list currently runs to
[around fifty countries](/tracked/nps-lump-sum-refund-country-list/), split
between agreement and reciprocity.

<div class="callout callout-warn">
  <p class="callout-title">Check your own country, not a table you found</p>
  <p>The list is specific and it is not predictable from a country's size or
  wealth — several very large economies are absent. Minimum contribution
  periods can also differ by country. Budgeting a departure around a refund you
  have not confirmed is how people discover this at the worst moment; the exit
  sequence is in <a href="/business/leaving-korea-tax-pension-and-refunds/">leaving Korea: tax, pension and refunds</a>.</p>
</div>

## Health insurance does not follow the pension's logic

Workplace health insurance is judged on the **employment relationship** meeting
the statutory conditions — not on nationality reciprocity or a social security
agreement in the way the pension is.

The employer must report the acquisition of subscriber status within
[14 days](/tracked/health-insurance-acquisition-report-deadline/) of the day it
begins.

Which produces the combination that confuses new arrivals most: **no national
pension line, and full health and long-term care lines**, on the same payslip,
correctly. Two statutes, two tests, two answers about the same person.

<div class="callout callout-note">
  <p class="callout-title">A card that never arrives is usually a filing, not a rule</p>
  <p>If weeks pass with no health insurance card, the ordinary explanation is
  that the acquisition report was not filed — a 14-day deadline you can ask
  about by name. It is rarely anything to do with being foreign.</p>
</div>

## Family are not covered on arrival

Becoming a workplace subscriber does not sweep arriving family into the scheme.

Dependant status has relationship, income and property conditions, and for
foreign nationals and overseas Koreans a
[six-month residence requirement](/tracked/foreign-dependant-residence-requirement/)
applies in certain cases. How it lands varies with the relationship and the
family member's own residence status, so a spouse and a minor child are worth
checking individually rather than assumed in either direction.

The exposure is the gap. A hospital visit before registration completes is
billed at the uninsured rate, and Korean hospital pricing without insurance is
not a rounding difference — what that looks like is in
[NHIS health insurance for foreigners](/insurance/nhis-korean-health-insurance-for-foreigners/).

## Employment insurance: the table everyone gets wrong

Coverage is not uniform across foreign employees, and the shape of the rule is
not "some visas in, some visas out".

| Group | How the Act applies |
|---|---|
| F-5, and certain F-2 and F-6 | Applies in full |
| E-1 to E-7, E-9, E-10, F-4, H-2 | Application-based — coverage once an application has been made |

<div class="callout callout-warn">
  <p class="callout-title">Three statements that are all wrong</p>
  <p>"E-9 is always compulsory." "E-7 is always excluded." "F-5 is
  application-based." Each inverts part of the actual structure. Worse, a
  visa-to-coverage table cannot answer the question anyway, because for the
  application-based group the answer depends on whether an application was in
  fact made. Ask payroll what your enrolment state is; do not infer it from
  your visa letter.</p>
</div>

## Being insured and being able to claim are different

Job-seeking benefit requires at least
[180 insured days](/tracked/unemployment-benefit-insured-days/) within the 18
months before leaving — and insured days are not calendar days of employment.
They count days that were the basis for remuneration plus paid weekly holidays,
and exclude unpaid days. A part-time or irregular schedule can therefore fall
short after well over six months on the payroll.

The statutory conditions on the reason for leaving and on active job-seeking
apply as well. And for a foreign claimant there is a second gate that is not in
the Employment Insurance Act at all:

<div class="callout callout-warn">
  <p class="callout-title">You have to be lawfully here, on a status permitting work</p>
  <p>Unemployment and immigration status interact. A status tied to the
  employment you have just lost is not a stable platform from which to claim,
  and the timing of any status change matters as much as the insurance
  conditions. Work out the immigration position first; the benefit question is
  downstream of it.</p>
</div>

Employment insurance is also not only unemployment — parental leave benefit and
maternity-related benefits run through it — but the same status-dependent
application question governs those.

## Industrial accident does not ask

An employee injured at work is not outside industrial accident protection
because of their status of stay. The scheme is judged on the workplace and the
employment relationship.

Where a person's stay is irregular, the compensation question and the
immigration question are assessed separately rather than as one. "You are
foreign, so there is no industrial accident cover" is not a statement of the
law, and it is worth recognising as such at the moment it is said — which is
usually the moment someone is hurt and least able to argue.

## Three checks, in order

<div class="steps">
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">1</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>Enrolment.</strong> Pension by nationality, agreement and status; employment insurance by status and application; health insurance by the employment relationship.</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">2</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>The declared base.</strong> Standard monthly income for the pension, remuneration for the others — do they match what you are actually paid?</div></div>
  <div class="step"><div class="step-num">3</div>
    <div class="step-body"><strong>The rate.</strong> 4.75% pension, 3.595% health, ≈0.4724% long-term care, 0.9% employment insurance, 0% industrial accident — the 2026 figures, set out in <a href="/employment/four-social-insurances-from-the-employee-side/">Korea’s four social insurances, from your side</a>.</div></div>
</div>

Reading these deductions properly is not adding up what left your salary. It is
establishing why you are in each scheme, what base and rate were applied, and
what each one would actually pay if the risk it insures arrived — and for a
foreign employee, whether the payroll team was ever told the status that
decides it.
